# Changelog

## 0.3.1

The compendium follows what the game itself shows. What the game's menus show comes with the thing under *Loose* and *Strict* alike, once the game lists it for your character: a recipe's materials and station, a piece's cost and tool, what a known thing is made into, a station's recipes, a store's wares. A recipe the game does not list yet keeps its row back under either rule, and the section says so.

Up close is where the game names a creature and marks its stars, 10 m unless the server sets otherwise. A creature seen so is kept with the stars and gear it had, the weapon of an attack taken or seen begun among the gear, and under *Strict* the stage shows it only so; a place's example and a raid stand their creatures no higher. Its attack begun up close counts as seen, as taking the hit does, and the attacks and clips not yet seen fold into one faint line and one chip that count them. A boss has no star rows. Your own hits tell how a creature takes a damage type on whichever game runs it; another player's hit tells you nothing, as the game shows you only a number.

What *Strict* keeps back shows as a soft smudge, a name you have not met in a page's text as a dash. The page's title row counts what it keeps back and what you found out, every counted cell and every line of where an item comes from one each, and the stars and gear the stage still lacks; `is:left` finds the pages whose chip still counts something. The *You* box tells how you came to know a thing, when you first saw it with the stars it had, and what is still to find out.

Drops show what you saw fall and how often, a trophy's chance by stars opening as the stars table's cells do. The drops' title counts the kills whose loot you saw fall, its hover setting them against the game's own count. What was seen in all your play, every character's, stands faint beside your own counts, on items' pages too. A chest the world filled counts what it held the first time you opened it, and pairs its items with its place then; a chest you or a friend filled pairs nothing.

*Learned* lists what your character met and the numbers it found out, newest first, each day headed by its world where that is not the one played; what was kept before worlds were stands under its own heading. A day of another world is named by it on a page too; a moment kept before worlds were names none. The notice at the top of the panel tells what came today in the world played, and what came in another world as that world's. A new character's first records are no catch-up: what they tell came to be known now.

Playing together, what you learn depends on where you stand and what you did, never on whose game runs the thing. A kill you had a hand in is yours, as the game credits it. A creature's death seen by its remains counts, with its stars and what fell, even when the game running it has no Scry, and as your own kill where the game credits you within seconds. Your own pick on a thing another game runs counts from your own game. A pair seen together keeps the way it was seen, so an item met beside a creature in a chest opens no drop of it. A taming counts as seen only within half a minute of seeing the creature wild, with a line to it; a friend's taming reads "seen tamed", petting a tame creature "petted"; a station the game listed because you stood by it reads "near". Where a friend's game has no Scry, a notice says what still reaches you and what goes unlearned.

The explored map's biomes are found in your ground as its places are, shown while *Ground you explored* is on and taken back with it; an older book that had them written in gives them back once. A world is told apart by its name for moments and by the game's id for maps and chests. An older book's attacks taken count again, and its chests counted by the game's old numbers let their counts go once.

Fixes: a section's count line says "not yet met" alone where it names nothing; a place with no page of its own counts nowhere; an item's page puts what was seen in all your play after your own counts; a smudge sits on the first line of its text; a cell or chip naming what was not seen together says so on hover; a faint row keeps the hovers of what it draws; a Learned group you folded or opened stays so; the rules notice names the server's seen reach where it differs from 10 m; a weapon's page keeps no attack back; a sighted place pairs with its biome; the search's `drops:`, `gives:` and `from:` ask by their way, and the list follows the page shown where it names what a room holds; peeking leaves the list's notes and marks and the *You* box as the book has them; a room not yet walked is named so everywhere, and a room's page relates what its dungeon saw go together with it; the explored map's news and a name never met are told apart; "immune" still counts as told by a hit, as the health bar not moving is proof enough; the world's own dungeon on the stage keeps its rooms standing as you walk it with the panel open, a room newly walked joining them, where before the whole stage was built anew and flickered, every five seconds while walking and once a minute from the server's answer; the stage is filmed only while something on it moves or changed, within a few frames otherwise, as each film costs the game time and memory; the *You* box's lines read in one grammar, when, where and how, "Killed by it" among them.

## 0.3.0

Scry keeps a compendium for each character, chosen at the top of the panel and offered once on a new character. *Loose* shows only what the character has met; *Strict* also keeps back how things connect, and the numbers the game never shows, until the character found them out. The *?* in the compendium's box explains it in the game, and `is:new`, `is:seen` and `is:walked` search what the character met.

A page's *You* box tells when the character first saw a thing and came to know it, and how its fights went. *Learned* lists what it learned day by day, and *Known up to* opens every tier below the bosses it has beaten.

A character played before is caught up from what the game keeps of it and from its explored map.

Where friends have Scry, what their game runs near you counts for you as it would alone, and a notice names those whose games lack it.

Each world can show more as known, source by source in the compendium's box: explored ground, shared maps, marked places, places on every map. With Scry on the server, the compendium also learns what only the server knows, and the server can set its rules for everyone, which a notice tells once on joining. Explored ground shows its places through its own row, which can be turned off, as the map reaches 100 m around you whatever you saw, and a place counts as seen only once your eye has a clear line to it.

The gear in the title row holds the panel's settings, written to the config file as they are chosen, among them *Stage* set to *Fast* for big dungeons on weaker machines. The spin's speed and the lights below a dungeon's cut are in the View box, reading locations in every world is beside *Find where things are*, and the map's marks are in the compendium's box.

Dungeons are laid out room by room, with a plan to go into each room. The dungeon you are in shows as it was saved, your walks are kept and marked on the large map, and a server reads what was left inside dungeons walked before Scry. Each mark on the large map carries a glyph for the dungeon's kind and stands on its entrance; the settings size the marks, choose which show, and put those you walked on the minimap too; an entrance nobody walked is faint until the pointer rests on it, and a mark's name grows with it.

Skills and traders have tabs of their own, and every figure a skill changes shows what it comes to at your level.

Pages tell more: weapon combos, a windmill's wind, what mods set or add to an item and its tooltip, stars' trophy chances as in Valheim 1.0.20, and readable names for all the game leaves unnamed.

What locations hold and where their floors are is kept between sessions, and big places are built and read over several frames.

A website opened from a mod's page no longer keeps Steam showing Valheim as running, the first step of a combo plays again, and the pointer comes back where it was after looking around.

## 0.2.0

Locations have a tab of their own: every location and dungeon room the world can place, grouped by biome, each telling where it is placed and what it holds. A location stands on the turntable as a new zone would roll it, its creatures included, and *Roll again* rolls it anew.

A dungeon or camp is built on the turntable as an example layout, with its floor plan in the corner, and a click on a room goes to it. Places can be cut open floor by floor with the ruler beside the stage, Page Up and Page Down, or Shift with the wheel; a place with nothing over its floors to take away, such as a ring of standing stones or a wreck, has no ruler and no cut. The rooms below a floor opened are dimmed and unlit; `LightDimmedRooms` keeps their lights.

Raids, biomes, spawners and mods have tabs of their own: what a raid brings and for whom, a biome's weathers, music and what lives there, a spawner's creatures and pace, and what each mod adds and which of the game's rules it hooks into.

*In the game* is laid out by topic, what a page is looked at for first, with tables where numbers change with something, such as a creature's attacks and stars or a weapon's damage by quality. An attack in a table plays when clicked.

Pages tell much more: a creature's senses, breeding, taming and riding, a piece's support, weather and placement rules, what gear resists and changes while worn, what burning, poison, frost and lightning do, fish and bait, keys and doors, and what follows a boss's fall. Pages of one kind show the same rows, saying none where that is the answer.

Loot is told by what it is worth: what nothing else gives is marked *only here*, what a trader buys is marked with its coins, then the rest, the least likely first. An item tells its odds in every chest, rock or tree that gives it.

A creature that comes only during a world event or after a boss's fall, such as Fimbulvinter's or the Charred, is listed apart from where it lives.

What a rock, vein or tree is broken with lists the pickaxes or axes that break it, the weakest first.

Each page names the mods hooking into what it tells, and what Scry is not sure of is marked with a `~` that says why. Drops seen in play are remembered from session to session.

The search takes more terms (`is:`, `weak:`, `resists:`, `immune:`, `damage:`, `skill:`, `drops:`, `from:`, `needs:`, `gives:` and `spawns:`), names in quotes, a comma for either of two values, and a name typed one letter off. Its help has an example of each.

The stage zooms toward the pointer and moves with a right-drag, and the *Ground* backdrop stands a model on its biome's own ground under its sky, its footsteps sounding on it. Volume, size, speed and Repeat sit beside the selection's name.

When part of Scry is off because the game changed, a strip under the header says what in plain words. *Find in locations* is called *Read all locations*. Numbers show their thousands with commas, and `/scry monitor` shows what Scry costs each frame.
