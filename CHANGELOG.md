# Changelog

## 0.2.0

A creature's details tell how near it must see you to turn on you, how far it chases before giving up, and the resistances of its weak spots, such as a troll's head.

A piece's details tell its support, how much of it is lost for each metre sideways and up, whether rain wears it, how it stands up to the Ashlands and heavy snow, and its resistances.

A spawner's details, such as a greydwarf nest's, tell each creature's share of its spawns and stars, the chance of each star, when it works, its pace, how many it keeps alive and how far away it puts them. A creature that comes from one says its share there too.

An item tells its odds in every chest, rock, tree or anything else that can give it: how many a roll gives, its share of a roll, how many rolls, and how often. A rare share keeps a decimal instead of showing as 0%.

Raids have a tab of their own. Each tells for whom it comes, when it is on the table, how the game rolls for raids, how long it lasts and what it brings, each creature with how many, how often and how likely. A creature's "Comes in the raid" line goes to the raid. A raid stands on the turntable as one wave of its creatures, rolled the way the game rolls a raid's first wave, stars and all, each group with room for every body, and *Roll again* rolls another. A raid's music plays when its *Music* line is clicked, and its *Weather* says what the weather does: wet, cold or freezing by day or night, dark, and how windy. Bosses' fights, the music and weather a boss brings while its health bar shows, are listed apart from the raids, named for their bosses and linked to them, and both come in the order the bosses are fought.

Locations have a tab of their own: every location the world can place and every dungeon room, grouped by biome in the order players meet them. A dungeon or camp and its rooms are a group of their own after its biome's other places, the location first and tagged, its rooms indented under it, entrances first and end caps last, each room in one place and linked to every dungeon built with it. Each tells where the world places it, down to the woods, lava or plant growth it keeps to, and once shown what it holds and how likely, the creatures its spawn points place, where its vegvisirs point, the levels it sets for its spawn points and how far around it nothing can be built. It stands on the turntable on the ground the game stands it on, a room on the floor it is walked into on and cut open above head height to look into. Its floors are found in the place itself, a tower's storeys and a cave's levels as much as a room's, and each is cut at head height or just under the floor above; the stage's *Roof* chip takes the roof off and puts it back, on a location too, its arrows and Page Up and Page Down over the stage step a floor down or up, a ruler at the stage's edge shows the floors and the cut and sets it anywhere, and Shift with the wheel moves it. It shows what the game leaves to chance rolled as a new zone rolls it, and *Roll again* rolls it anew. Its details name the music it plays and when, and Enter or a click on it plays it. A location with a runestone shows every text the stone can give under *Runestone texts*. *Find in locations*, now called *Read all locations*, fills in every location and room the same way, and a place in an item's *Found in* goes to that location.

A dungeon or camp shows an example, laid out the way the game lays out a new one and never a world's real one: built on the stage room by room, where a dungeon's *Inside* and *Outside* switch between the example and its entrance, with its floor plan in the stage's corner. A room under the mouse is named on either, a click goes to it, and *Another example*, beside the stage's other buttons, lays out a new one. Its page tells what its rooms hold, the loot in them and the creatures their spawn points place, each with in how many of its kinds of room it is.

A piece says which build tool builds it and on which tab, a mod's own hammer or the cultivator as much as the hammer, and a tool lists everything it builds, tab by tab.

A creature names the mods that hook into the game's own code for what it drops or where it spawns, and a chest, rock, tree or plant those hooking into what it gives, found for any mod, so the details never look more complete than they are.

What creatures drop as you play is watched and kept from session to session, whichever mod put it there: a creature shows what it was seen dropping and in how many of your kills, and an item names every creature seen dropping it. Nothing is changed by watching, and loot another player's game makes is not seen.

*Mod report*, above the list while it shows only what mods added or with `/scry mods`, sums up each mod: what it adds, what Scry links for its crafting stations and build tools, which of the game's drops and spawning it hooks into, and what Scry could not place, such as a station nothing is made or built at or an item with no source Scry sees.

Mods have a tab of their own: every mod loaded has a page telling its version, what it adds kind by kind, its stations and tools, which of the game's drops and spawning it hooks into, and what Scry could not place. The line naming the mod that added an entry goes to that page, and more of what mods add is named for its mod: by the scripts it carries, by the sounds and icons it uses, and, for mods built on Jotunn, their creatures, vegetation and locations too. A recipe or conversion one mod adds for another's item, or for the game's, says which mod added it. A mod installed by a mod manager shows its icon, says what it is, who made it and where its website is, and shows its readme as plain text, all from its package's own files. Every page lists the mods it needs and works with and those needing or working with it, each going to that mod's page, and names any it will not run with.

When part of Scry is off, because the game changed in a way it does not know or a part of it failed, an amber strip under the header says what in plain words instead of a *Game changed* chip, with details that say what to do and copy what is off for a report.
