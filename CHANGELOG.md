# Changelog

## 0.3.0

The compendium shows only what the character playing has come to know. *Compendium* in the title row switches it *Off*, *Loose* or *Strict*, chosen for each character and kept with it, and a new character starts with it off. A creature is known once fought or tamed, and seen as far as its size makes it out; an item once held, made or eaten, its recipe unlocked or its tooltip card shown in a chest, the inventory or a store; anything once used, as a friend's portal, bed or ship; a resource once hit or picked; a place once stepped into and a dungeon once entered; a raid once lived through; a trader once its store is opened. What a known thing plays, leaves behind or gives comes along with it, but not what one only seen does. Anything in the world is seen by one rule, creatures, trees, rocks, buildings, pickables, nests and items on the ground alike: in view with nothing solid between, as far as its size makes it out. Biomes under the ground explored are seen, and so are the places a Vegvisir marks. A page opens with a box titled *You*: what the game counted of it for the character, as kills, things made or picked up, foods eaten and pieces built, and how, on which day and in which biome it came to know it. What was only seen stands on the turntable with its page closed, telling how to come to know it. Lists and search hold only what was met, each tab counting what was found of all there is, and only the world a player meets: no effects, sounds, projectiles or mods, which come back with the compendium off or while peeking. A row shows no prefab name then, and things of one kind sharing a name say which of them each is.

*Loose* shows a known page whole but names only what the character has met: the rest is left out, each section saying faintly how many more were not yet met, a name never met reads ??? in its text, and a place's example stands no creature not yet met. Where the game gives a thing no name its page names it by its prefab's words, and where an item comes from reads as a row for each way, givers alike but for their biome one chip saying how many. The game's commands that spawn or give a thing show only with the compendium off or while peeking. *Strict* shows of how a known thing connects only what the character saw go together: the drops it saw fall, where it met a creature or picked a thing up, what it made at a station, what built a piece, what hit it, what a creature ate, what a trader sells, what went into a station and came out of it, what a tree or rock really dropped, the offering that called a boss, the bait a fish took. A known creature's damage grid keeps back how it takes each type until the game's popup on a hit told it, and other numbers the game never shows wait until the character found them out, each saying what shows it: an attack's damage until it hit you, health until you killed one, taming, pregnancy, growing up and growing back until you saw it, a weak spot until a hit found it, a tree's or rock's health until you broke one, what stars change until you saw a starred one fall, a spawner's pool and pace until you saw it spawn, a producer's pace until you collected from one. What a chest holds is counted the first time you open each one, what a bush gives besides as you pick it, and a biome's weathers as you see them come. Playing with others, the game of whoever came near first runs what is around you, and only that game sees it fall, break or be picked: your own kills, breaks and picks that ran in another player's game are counted apart and told under what they missed, and every hint and count another game may run says it shows where your game runs it, why on hover. Drops and what trees and rocks give show what the character saw fall, how many and how often, not the game's chances, on their own page and in the lines of an item's page telling where it comes from, and where a creature spawns or a plant grows shows only for the biomes it was met in. A section that left something out says at its end how many more were not yet seen, never which, set apart from the page's footnotes by an accent bar and a compass, as is every line telling what is still to find, and names of things never met read ??? in a page's text and its tooltip card. A trader's wares waiting for a boss not yet fallen are not named: under *Loose* the row says how many more there are to unlock, under *Strict* the section's end does.

*Known up to* shows everything of a biome as known once its boss has fallen for the character, tier by tier as far as the bosses beaten in a row, and can be lowered again; what it shows is marked in the list, on its page, and on every chip and link going to it from another page. Each thing's tier is worked out from how it is come by, so iron gear belongs to the Swamp, black metal to the Plains and a guardian power to its boss.

A character played before is caught up from what the game keeps of it: what it carried, made, ate and picked, the recipes and pieces it unlocked, its kills, trophies and biomes, and the guardian power it chose. Its explored map counts too, the kinds of place lying in ground it explored counting as seen, read again each session and told in the notice as news; on a dedicated server that needs Scry on the server, which tells only which kinds of place lie there, never where. *Read the map again* above the Locations list, or `/scry map`, reads it anew. What a character learns is collected even while the compendium is off, so turning it on later shows everything met meanwhile, and each character's knowledge is kept in `BepInEx/cache/isimp.Scry/compendium`.

*Learned* lists everything the character learned, newest first under a heading for each day of the game, older days folded, each row saying how it came, "killed", "picked", "seen" or what it found out of something *Strict* held back, and the eye beside the mode shows everything until the panel closes. The notice under the header tells what happened in words: "Day 14: came to know Troll and Greydwarf Brute, saw 2 more, and found out how fire lands on Neck". A page's *You* box tells the moment the character first saw the thing and came to know it, the day, the time of day, the weather and the biome, a creature's stars, the weapon in hand for a fight; how its kills went, from the game's own counts ("14 times: 9 in melee, 3 with arrows or bolts, 2 with mixed attacks"); and how often a creature killed the character. Under *Strict* it ends with what is still to find out and what to do for each.

Skills have a tab of their own, a page for each: what it changes at no skill, at your level and at the top, by the game's own rules, what raises it and how far your next level is, the effects that add levels or make it rise faster, what trains it and what a death takes. Wherever your skills change a figure on a page, what it comes to at your level shows faint under it: a weapon's damage and each of its attacks with their stamina and eitr, an axe's hits on trees, a block, a bow's draw and a crossbow's reload, how near a creature sees you sneaking, a mount's speed and drain under you, a workbench's making time and bonus, a crop's bonus when picked, a build tool's cost and wear, a scythe's reach, a fishing rod's drain and reeling, what reeling a fish in drains, a staff's summons and how hard a summon hits for you, the protection shield, a weapon's damage and a shield's block at each quality, a parry, and how fast you run on your own page. With the compendium on, a skill shows only once you have learned it, at level 1: before that it is listed nowhere, nothing names it and nothing is told at it.

What *Find where things are* reads is kept, so the next time it is known as soon as the world's catalog is. After an update of the game only the locations it changed are read again, and after a change of mods or of Scry all of them, in the background, with what was known shown meanwhile. `/scry locations` reads them all again.

Where a place's floors are is kept too, so a dungeon's rooms no longer each stall a frame to be measured when its example is built again, in this session or the next. Showing what the locations hold is spread over a few frames, so neither entering a world nor finishing a reading stalls one. The first dungeon or location picked in a session puts its page together in a fraction of the time it took, and a dungeon whose rooms name thousands of parts, as some mods' do, tells its page again in a few milliseconds.

*Look again* above the Locations tab's list, or `/scry forget`, forgets everything Scry kept and reads the locations afresh, for when what is shown seems out of date.

A place zoomed out on the stage keeps all its rooms; the game's level of detail dropped their pieces one by one as they grew small. Locations and dungeon rooms no longer list animations, which were their creatures' or their doors' rather than their own.

A greatsword's, an axe's or a knife's first attack plays its clip again, as do the attacks of creatures that pick one of several animations, such as Bonemass's punch or Yagluth's stomp. After looking around with the right mouse button, the pointer is back where it was rather than in the middle of the screen.

What equipping something changes on you is noted as you play, whichever mod made it so, and the item's page shows it by quality under *Wearing*: the carry weight, armour, movement, resistances and effects a backpack or other mod gear gives only once worn.

An item that a mod's settings are named after, such as a backpack whose size and carry bonus a mod sets in a section of its own, shows those settings on its page under *Set by mods*, as the mod's config holds them now.

An item whose tooltip in the game mods add lines to shows that tooltip on its page under *Tooltip in the game*, at each quality, as a card laid out like the details, the lines the mods added tinted, and the mods hooking the tooltip named under it, each going to its page.

A creature's *With stars* table gives each drop that stars make only likelier a line of its chance at each star, as Valheim 1.0.20 does for every trophy, where it said they doubled in number.

A weapon's attacks table has a line for each step of its combo, the last as the game deals it, each playing its own swing, and says how the combo goes and what starts it over. A windmill's page tells what wind it needs: none below the least, full at the strongest, any facing; what blocks it within 30 m of its hub; when its blades hurt; and how much time away it makes up.

A mod's website opened from its page no longer keeps Steam showing Valheim as running after the game is closed when the browser was not open before.

Traders have a tab of their own, Haldor, Hildir and the Bog Witch among them, and with the compendium on they are listed once met. A trader's page sells everything its store does: what mods add to it, such as Skald's Girdle, and the upgrades the game sells, Wider and Deeper Pockets, with their icons and prices, told bought once you have them. A creature's attacks tell the damage a hit deals as the game rolls it, from 87 to 100% of its figure, and where things come from gives the variants a mod numbers, such as rocks and markers, one chip.

Inside a dungeon you have been in, its own rooms can be shown in place of an example, and the large map marks each such dungeon; a click on the mark opens its map in Scry. This dungeon shows its layout alone, its creatures and chests being the world's, which Scry does not read. The marks keep to the map as it is dragged, a quick drag let go over one no longer opens it, and resting the pointer on one shows its name and how far it was walked under it, as the game names a pin.

Under *Strict* a creature's breeding waits for a birth seen, a crop's time to grow for one grown, and a smelter's, kiln's, cooking station's or fermenter's times and fuel for one finished; a creature's loot and what a tree, rock or bush gives count where the character killed, broke or picked it, however far away, or saw it within 30 m, as a birth and the rest do; a dungeon known brings along only the kinds of room the character has stood in, the rest showing as a room not yet walked. Each giver of where an item comes from is a chip as under *Loose*, its count after its name. With the compendium on, a page's header leaves the prefab's name out, which shows on the name's hover.

A skill's page says that knockback rolls with a hit's damage, and tells the average hit, 92.5% of a weapon's figure at the top, 2.3 times no skill's. What raises each skill follows the game: a swing once however many it hits, a pickaxe on anything that breaks but a tree or a creature, an archery target for bows and crossbows, Blood Magic by summons and a broken shield for the one they follow or shielded, a jump only with the stamina for it, fishing twice as fast with a fish on, crafting each of several made at once, repairing by how worn the item was, and building not after a piece was taken down. A staff that trains its skill slower than the usual hit says so on its chip, and the way to the next level counts hits with the weapon in hand.

The room card says only how many were not yet met where it lists none of a roll, counts copies numbered apart as one thing, and its ways name their rooms alone, cut at the end, with how far and whether a dead end on hover. The Frost Caves' rooms lose their leading "new" and read "Slope room" for "sloperoom". A list row's tag too long for the row keeps its last part, the one telling rows alike apart. The world's own dungeon shows no *Roll again*, the peeking notices are short with the rest on hover, and room names on the plan read on the level plates. A troll's rock throw is called Troll throw, not Fireball attack.

Labels read alike across pages: *Tames in*, *Fed for*, *Grows in*, *Regrows in*, *Trains*, *Trained by*, *Reeling in*, and the skill table's *Stamina:* and *Bonus:* rows. What stands on the stage is a preview, biomes are named as the game names them, and the Locations buttons read *Find where things are* and *Look again*. Tips say what a click does, as an order and without "click"; a creature's stars, an item's quality and the compendium's tiers each have their own word; the world's own dungeon is tagged *In your world* and *Another example* lays out a new one; a dungeon you walked is *Your walk*; buttons say what they do (*Find everything here*, *Open the mod report*, *Stop peeking*), the camera's chips say where they look from, and food heals every 10 s rather than "a tick".

A trader's page is laid out by topic as every other page is, so under *Strict* the wares waiting for a boss are counted at its end. A word of a page's text no longer reads ??? because an effect the compendium never shows goes by it. Names too long for the list are cut with an ellipsis, tags that would be cut to a stub are left for the hover, and tables keep their headings whole, a column of figures kept back as wide as its heading rather than its hidden figures. The plan's tree names stand on a dark backing, lines one deed shows say so once, attacks named after their creature go by what they throw, props a building mod names keep no copy numbers, status effects' settings read in plain words, Hildir's rows say which chest brings them, and the Jump skill tells about how high a jump rises.

What the game names nothing, such as an effect or a projectile, goes by its prefab's words in the list and on its page, without the makers' marks for an effect or a sound, "aoe" as "area" and no word said twice in a row, its prefab's name beside it with the compendium off, and a name the game writes in small letters starts with a capital. A creature's attacks, and what only creatures wear, say which creature carries them and are told apart by it rather than by number. A trader's rows waiting for bosses not yet met, alike once the bosses' names are kept back, are one row counting them all. A dungeon's page lists only the walks with a room walked. A name the game runs together, such as "GrapplingHook", reads as words. A status effect's page says in words what its marks do, such as the wind blowing the way your ship sails or animals near you taming faster, that its looks show on players only, and how much stamina it drains a second, as it does how a shield absorbs more by world and skill level, how much faster the wind behind makes you, how often a tick heals or hurts, and how often feeling sick throws up a food. A place looked at for the first time no longer stalls a frame while the compendium works out its tiers again.

## 0.2.0

Locations have a tab of their own: every location and dungeon room the world can place, grouped by biome, each telling where it is placed and what it holds. A location stands on the turntable as a new zone would roll it, its creatures included, and *Roll again* rolls it anew.

A dungeon or camp is built on the turntable as an example layout, with its floor plan in the corner, and a click on a room goes to it. Places can be cut open floor by floor with the ruler beside the stage, Page Up and Page Down, or Shift with the wheel; a place with nothing over its floors to take away, such as a ring of standing stones or a wreck, has no ruler and no cut. The rooms below a floor opened are dimmed and unlit; `LightDimmedRooms` keeps their lights.

Raids, biomes, spawners and mods have tabs of their own: what a raid brings and for whom, a biome's weathers, music and what lives there, a spawner's creatures and pace, and what each mod adds and which of the game's rules it hooks into.

*In the game* is laid out by topic, what a page is looked at for first, with tables where numbers change with something, such as a creature's attacks and stars or a weapon's damage by quality. An attack in a table plays when clicked.

Pages tell much more: a creature's senses, breeding, taming and riding, a piece's support, weather and placement rules, what gear resists and changes while worn, what burning, poison, frost and lightning do, fish and bait, keys and doors, and what follows a boss's fall. Pages of one kind show the same rows, saying none where that is the answer.

Loot is told by what it is worth: what nothing else gives is marked *only here*, what a trader buys is marked with its coins, then the rest, the least likely first. An item tells its odds in every chest, rock or tree that gives it.

A creature that comes only during a world event or after a boss's fall, such as Fimbulvinter's or the Charred, is listed apart from where it lives.

What a rock, vein or tree is broken with lists the pickaxes or axes that break it, the weakest first.

Each page names the mods hooking into what it tells, and what Scry is not sure of is marked with a `~` that says why. Drops seen in play are remembered from session to session.

The search takes more terms (`is:`, `weak:`, `resists:`, `immune:`, `damage:`, `skill:`, `drops:`, `from:`, `needs:`, `gives:` and `spawns:`), names in quotes, a comma for either of two values, and a name typed one letter off. Its help has an example of each.

The stage zooms toward the pointer and moves with a right-drag, and the *Ground* backdrop stands a model on its biome's own ground under its sky, its footsteps sounding on it. Volume, size, speed and Repeat sit beside the selection's name.

When part of Scry is off because the game changed, a strip under the header says what in plain words. *Find in locations* is called *Read all locations*. Numbers show their thousands with commas, and `/scry monitor` shows what Scry costs each frame.
