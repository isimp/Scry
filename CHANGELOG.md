# Changelog

## 0.2.0

A creature's details tell how near it must see you to turn on you, how far it chases before giving up, whether it attacks all the same in a world with *Passive enemies* on, and the resistances of its weak spots, such as a troll's head.

A piece's details tell its support, how much of it is lost for each metre sideways and up, whether rain wears it, how it stands up to the Ashlands and heavy snow, and its resistances.

A spawner's details, such as a greydwarf nest's, tell each creature's share of its spawns and stars, the chance of each star, when it works, its pace, how many it keeps alive and how far away it puts them. A creature that comes from one says its share there too.

A creature lives only where it comes without a world event or a boss's fall: Fimbulvinter's Jotun warriors and Elakingar are in every biome only during the event, the Charred come to the other biomes once Fader is defeated, and a biome's page lists those apart, under *Here only during a world event* and *Comes here later in the game*; a spawn's line names the event it waits for. Loot is told by what it is worth having: what nothing else in the world gives is first, marked *only here*, then what a trader pays for, marked with its coins, then the rest, each the least likely first; a dungeon's loot in its rooms is told by the best chance a chest there gives it, no longer by how many kinds of room hold it; a place's chests and pickups are told by what they can give, so a chest comes before barley, each marked by the best of its loot. An item tells its odds in every chest, rock, tree or anything else that can give it: how many a roll gives, its share of a roll, how many rolls, and how often. A rare share keeps a decimal instead of showing as 0%.

Lists come in an order that says something: loot rarest first, creatures toughest first, where something comes from surest first, and on a biome's page its places rarest first and what grows hardest to gather first. A location's parts are told by what they are, chests and pickups first, then what to gather, the rest, and its building pieces last.

Raids have a tab of their own. Each tells for whom it comes, when it is on the table, how the game rolls for raids, how long it lasts and what it brings, each creature with how many, how often and how likely. A creature's "Comes in the raid" line goes to the raid. Its page tells that its creatures keep coming for as long as it lasts, each rolled again at its pace and the fallen replaced. A raid stands on the turntable as the first roll of each of its creatures, rolled the way the game rolls it, stars and all, each group with room for every body, and *Roll again* rolls them anew. A raid's music plays when its *Music* line is clicked, and its *Weather* says what the weather does: wet, cold or freezing by day or night, dark, and how windy. Bosses' fights, the music and weather a boss brings while its health bar shows, are listed apart from the raids, named for their bosses and linked to them, and both come in the order the bosses are fought.

Locations have a tab of their own: every location the world can place and every dungeon room, grouped by biome in the order players meet them. A dungeon or camp and its rooms are a group of their own after its biome's other places, the location first and tagged, its rooms indented under it, entrances first and end caps last, each room in one place and linked to every dungeon built with it. Each tells where the world places it, down to the woods, lava or plant growth it keeps to, and once shown what it holds and how likely, the creatures its spawn points place, where its vegvisirs point, the levels it sets for its spawn points and how far around it nothing can be built. It stands on the turntable on the ground the game stands it on, a room on the floor it is walked into on and cut open above head height to look into. A large location is put on the stage over several frames rather than holding the game up, and a dungeon's example finds its floors as quickly with a hundred rooms as with ten. Its floors are found in the place itself, a tower's storeys and a cave's levels as much as a room's, and every room of a deep dungeon, its galleries and a sloping corridor too, on a floor one can open, and only where one can stand with something over it: roofs, ramps, beams, tables and beds are no floor, and each is cut at head height or just under the floor above; a roof button over the ruler at the stage's edge takes the roof off and puts it back, on a location too, Page Up and Page Down over the stage step a floor down or up, a ruler at the stage's edge shows the floors and the cut and sets it anywhere, and Shift with the wheel moves it, the camera staying above it to look in. The world's weather, a lightning strike's flash among it, stays off the stage. It shows what the game leaves to chance rolled as a new zone rolls it, and *Roll again* rolls it anew. The creatures its spawn points put there stand in it, on the ground under their points as the game drops them and those that fly over it, a dungeon's rooms each with their own, rolled as the game spawns them: a spawn group keeps only so many, picked by weight, and each rolls its stars, standing whole and still above a floor's cut, and those on a floor above it cut away with that floor; the stage's *Creatures* chip puts them away and back. The wheel zooms toward what is under the pointer, as near as a couple of metres however big a place is, and a right-drag moves the view with the pointer; with a floor opened the camera looks at that floor, framed on its rooms, and glides along as floors are stepped. Its details name the music it plays and when, and Enter or a click on it plays it. A location with a runestone shows every text the stone can give under *Runestone texts*. *Find in locations*, now called *Read all locations*, fills in every location and room the same way, and a place in an item's *Found in* goes to that location.

A dungeon or camp shows an example, laid out the way the game lays out a new one and never a world's real one: built on the stage room by room, where a dungeon's *Inside* and *Outside* switch between the example and its entrance, with its floor plan in the stage's corner, turning with the view, framed on its rooms, and with a floor opened showing only that floor's rooms; on the stage the rooms below an opened floor stand dimmed and those above are put away, the floor named beside the ruler with how many rooms it has. A room under the mouse is named on either, a click goes to it, and *Another layout*, beside the stage's other buttons, lays out a new one. Its page tells what its rooms hold, the loot in them and the creatures their spawn points place, each with in how many of its kinds of room it is.

A piece says which build tool builds it and on which tab, a mod's own hammer or the cultivator as much as the hammer, and a tool lists everything it builds, tab by tab.

A creature names the mods that hook into the game's own code for what it drops or where it spawns, and a chest, rock, tree or plant those hooking into what it gives, found for any mod, so the details never look more complete than they are. The same holds for the rest of what Scry tells: comfort, how smelters, cooking stations, fermenters, beehives and fires work, wear and support, costs, item stats, eating, growing, the weather, raids, taming and breeding, trading and container sizes each name the mods hooking into them. A page tells them all in one soft line at its end, how many mods and what they hook into, and names them on hover.

A chest, cart or ship tells how many slots it holds.

What details name goes there as a chip in more places: the biomes a plant grows in, a beehive works in and a piece may be placed in, the pieces a piece keeps its distance from and the one it must stand near, the status effects one cannot take while another lasts, the mods present that a mod will not run with, and the boss a raid waits for.

Armour tells what it resists while worn and a shield what it resists while blocking. Gear tells what it changes while worn, as the game's tooltip does: heat resistance, eitr regeneration, the stamina of each kind of action, the most adrenaline, and what full adrenaline gives. A weapon with a second attack shows both as a table, each its damage, knockback, stagger and cost, its name playing the attack on the person wearing it and stopping it again; shown on its own, a click puts it on the person first.

A tame creature tells how it breeds: what it needs, how its love points come, how long it is pregnant, when too many near stop it, and its young. A young one tells what it grows into and when, an egg what hatches from it and where it must lie, and each names where it comes from. A creature with a saddle tells what it is ridden with and its stamina while ridden.

A fish tells which baits it bites on and how likely, and a bait which fish it catches. A locked door tells its key, and a key the doors it opens. A boss tells its Forsaken power, and its trophy the power it gives on its boss stone.

A piece tells where it may be placed: on the ground or cultivated ground only, not in water, not on wood, only in one biome, apart from the pieces it must keep away from, and the rest of what the game checks. A station's upgrade names its station and how near it must stand, a station how far from it you may build, and whatever counts as a base, warmth, fire or a place monsters avoid says so and how far it reaches. A bed tells what claiming it and sleeping in it need.

A ballista tells what it fires and at whom, and the trophies that make it shoot only their creatures; a trap on whom it springs and how hard; a ship whether the Ashlands' seas burn it and what it takes capsized; a cart what it weighs with its load; a catapult what it loads.

Pages of one type show the same rows, saying "none" or "no" where that is the answer: a creature whether it attacks, has weak spots, can be tamed and drops anything; every item whether it goes through portals; gear how far it upgrades, whether it wears out and where it is repaired; armour its armour, movement and set bonus; weapons their block and second attack; furniture its comfort; a piece its cost; a rock or tree the tool tier it needs and whether it gives anything.

Resistances show as a grid of every damage type, the same on every creature, piece, rock and tree, each the share of that damage taken (50% resisted, 150% weak, 0% immune) and coloured by it, so pages line up whatever they resist. Armour and shields show theirs the same way, or say they resist nothing.

What Scry is not sure of is marked with a `~` and a softer colour, and says why when hovered: which mod added something when only clues tell it, what mods hooking into a rule may change, what was seen in play, and where Scry found nothing. Everything else is read from the game or worked out by its rules. An item or creature Scry finds no source for says so instead of leaving the place empty.

Biomes have a tab of their own. A biome's page tells each of its weathers, how likely it is and what it does, its music for each time of day, playing when clicked, and what lives, swims, grows, stands and raids there, apart from what comes only in a world event's weather; *Everything here* searches for all of it, and the biome chips on every page go to it.

A boss, or any creature whose defeat moves the world on, tells what follows its fall: the raids that may then come and those that stop, what then spawns and what spawners stop placing, and what traders then sell.

What creatures drop as you play is watched and kept from session to session, whichever mod put it there: a creature shows what it was seen dropping and in how many of your kills, and an item names every creature seen dropping it. Nothing is changed by watching, and loot another player's game makes is not seen.

*Mod report*, above the list while it shows only what mods added or with `/scry mods`, sums up each mod: what it adds, what Scry links for its crafting stations and build tools, which of the game's rules it hooks into, and what Scry could not place, such as a station nothing is made or built at or an item with no source Scry sees.

Mods have a tab of their own: every mod loaded has a page telling its version, what it adds kind by kind, its stations and tools, which of the game's rules it hooks into, and what Scry could not place. The line naming the mod that added an entry goes to that page, and more of what mods add is named for its mod: by the scripts it carries, by the sounds and icons it uses, and, for mods built on Jotunn, their creatures, vegetation and locations too. A recipe or conversion one mod adds for another's item, or for the game's, says which mod added it. A mod installed by a mod manager shows its icon, says what it is, who made it and where its website is, and shows its readme as plain text, all from its package's own files. Every page lists the mods it needs and works with and those needing or working with it, each going to that mod's page, and names any it will not run with.

When part of Scry is off, because the game changed in a way it does not know or a part of it failed, an amber strip under the header says what in plain words instead of a *Game changed* chip, with details that say what to do and copy what is off for a report.

Volume, size and animation speed are small icons beside the selection's name, each opening a short slider, and one Repeat beside them makes clips, effects and sounds play again as they end; under the name a line of the page's sections, with their counts, goes to each and lights the one being read; *Adjust* keeps stars, wear, look and a projectile's speed. The stage keeps few chips: *View* opens a box for the spin, backdrop, lighting and a person for size, a place's roof is put on and off over the ruler, and an example's plan folds on itself. A resource monitor (*ResourceMonitor* under Diagnostics, or `/scry monitor`) shows in a corner of the screen what Scry costs each frame, with a graph of the last ten seconds, what it allocates and what it holds. With the *Ground* backdrop, with or without the sky, a model stands on its own biome's ground, drawn as the world draws its terrain, fading out toward a horizon, with the biome's grass and small plants, the sea's floor under the world's water, a place's paths and paving painted on it, the biome's own light and sky at the lighting's time of day, and shadows; the View box picks another biome, and a dungeon's inside keeps the plain floor. A creature's footsteps sound on the ground the stage shows, as they would there in the game: snow in the mountains, mud in the swamp, water on the sea's floor; the *Ground* switch over its animations is gone, and a creature with no step for a ground takes another rather than walking in silence.

Every number Scry shows has its thousands by commas, 5,000 as much in a creature's health as in a count, and reads the same whatever language the PC is set to. The panel's place is read line by line, so one damaged line no longer loses the rest.

Who plays an effect or a sound, what a thing leaves behind, what a place holds and what an effect list's chip names count only what the game plays: a slot a list leaves switched off counts nowhere.

A creature's attacks are a table, each attack's damage, how it is made (naming the projectile it fires), what it puts on you, its reach and how often it comes side by side, the projectile and the effect each going to its page; its fight names what to bring, each a search: weapons dealing what it is weak to (`damage:fire is:weapon`), gear resisting the elements its attacks deal (`resists:poison is:wearable`), its name playing the attack and stopping it again, and so is what stars add, its health, how hard it hits and how much it drops at each star, and what grows with quality at each level, a weapon's or armour's damage, armour, block, block force, durability and the station level it needs, and a raid's creatures, a line each with how many at once, how often, how likely, their stars and the rest, and a spawner's with their share and stars, and a biome's weathers (each one's share of the time and what it does) and its music by the time of day, each music playing when clicked. A cave's long slope is opened a floor every two metres, each cut over ground it reaches, so no part of a level stands above the cut that opens it; the floor ruler cuts anywhere the place stands before it puts the roof on. Breeding, planting and summoning show whole on each page of them, right under its tiles, the page's own step lit and each other going to its page: a hen, its egg, the chick and the hen again; carrot seeds, what they are planted as, the grown carrots and the carrot picked; Eikthyr's offering, his altar, Eikthyr, his trophy and the power it gives. Each topic leads with what it is looked at for: a weapon's fight with its attacks and its damage by quality, then what attacking costs, its blocking after; armour's wearing with what it is at each quality, then its set; a creature's fight with its attacks and what stars add, then what hurts it; a raid's and a spawner's creatures first. A creature's *In the game* is laid out by topic: its health, faction, how it moves and whether it can be tamed as tiles at the top, then its fight, its loot with what stars and the world change about it right under its drops, where it lives, taming and breeding, riding, its senses and behaviour and what follows its fall, each in a box of its own under its name; a boss's page tells how to summon it and what its fall opens before its loot, and a tameable creature's tells its taming and riding first. An item's is too: the numbers that matter for what it is as tiles (a weapon's damage, weight, quality and wear; armour's armour, weight, quality and movement; food's food, healing and how long it lasts), then what it is for where that is its point (a trophy's Forsaken power, what a building tool builds, what a fish bites on and a bait catches, what a key opens), hatching, how it fights, what it does worn or as food, how it is made, where it comes from and what it is used for. A piece's shows its health, comfort, material and support as tiles, a chest's, cart's or ship's slots and a station's building reach first, then what it does (a station's making, a kiln's fuel and pace, a ship's or cart's load and how a ship fares at sea, a ballista's ammo, a fire's warmth, a door's key, what a chest holds), how it is built, what it gives for rest (its comfort and sleeping in it) and how it stands up to weight and weather. A tree's, rock's or plant's shows its health and the tool it needs, then what gathering it gives with what breaks it (the pickaxes or axes of its tier and up), how a plant grows and where it grows. A projectile's shows its own damage and flight time, then what fires it, how it hits and how it flies. A location's or room's shows its biome, how many a world has and its size, then what it and its rooms hold, how it is laid out, its music and where it is placed; a room's leads with the dungeon it is built into. A raid's shows how long it lasts, its weather and what it comes for, then what it brings, when it comes and its music. A biome's shows what its weather puts on you (wet, cold, freezing, at night where only then) and how many creatures and places it has as tiles, then what is there, its weathers with what each puts on you and its music, and a status effect's how long it lasts, then how you get it (what gives it, from food and gear to beds, fires and weather), what it changes and what it cannot be taken with; a creature's fight also names the items it carries and what its attacks put on you, a piece of an armour set names the set's other pieces right under its set bonus, and its variants are listed indented right under it, and Linked no longer repeats a link In the game shows; a mod's page shows its version and maker, then what it adds, what it hooks into, its ties to other mods and about it. Attack items no creature carries, left in the game's data, are listed apart from the swords and axes, after the creatures' own attacks. Spawners are a kind of their own with a tab, `kind:spawner` and a colour, nests apart from spawn points, where they were among other things before: a spawner's page shows its pace, how many it keeps alive and its health, then what it spawns, breaking it and where it is, and a biome's page lists the spawners there. The search's help has an example of every term, and a click on one tries it. The list lights the part of each name the search matched, and a search finding nothing reads a word one slip off as the name it was meant to be, saying so ("No match for greydwraf; showing greydwarf"). The search takes more: `is:` asks what a thing is (`is:boss`, `is:tameable`, `is:food`, `is:weapon`, `is:buildable` and more, listed in the search's help); `weak:fire`, `resists:frost` and `immune:poison` find what takes more, less or none of a damage type, creatures and worn armour alike, `damage:spirit` what deals it, creatures by their attacks, and `skill:axes` what trains a skill; `drops:resin`, `from:troll`, `needs:bronze`, `gives:` and `spawns:greydwarf` follow what the details link, `gives:rested` finding beds (a status effect's *Given by* now names what the game's own code gives: beds Rested, fires warmth and rest, seats rest by a fire, Resting Rested, and a biome's weathers and a raid's or boss fight's weather Wet, Cold and Freezing, a biome's page naming what its weather puts on you); a name in quotes finds only what is called exactly that (`"troll"`, not Troll hat); a comma in a term reads as or, so `biome:swamp,plains` finds what lives in either, and Tab after the comma suggests the next value.

The items creatures attack with stay out of the weapons' groups: an item no inventory can show, one without an icon, is under *Creature attacks*, or *Attacks no creature carries* where no creature carries it, even where a mod gives it a source.

Chips, tabs and badges keep round ends however low they are drawn; one lower than 30 pixels showed stray lines at its ends. A loot mark is a solid amber pill with dark words.
