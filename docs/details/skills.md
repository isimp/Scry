# Skills

The skills tab, each skill's page, and the faint lines under every figure a skill changes, by the game's own formulas.

Code: `src/Model/SkillMath.cs`, `src/Catalog/YourSkills.cs`, `src/Model/SkillPageWords.cs`, `src/Model/Groups.cs`, `src/Model/CompendiumView.cs`, `src/Model/Explorer.cs`, `src/Model/SkillWords.cs`, `src/Catalog/SkillReading.cs`.

See also: [game rules](game-rules.md), [showing](../compendium/showing.md); all notes in [the index](../index.md).

## Skills

Every skill the character can have is an entry of its own in a *Skills* tab, read from the character's own list of skills (`Skills.m_skills`, which holds a skill a mod adds too) or else the player prefab's, named and described as the game's skill screen names it, with its icon, and listed by what it is for: fighting, magic, moving, gathering and making, then what mods add (`Groups.Skill`). With the compendium on, a skill shows once the character has learned it, kept at level 1 or more (`SkillMath.Learned`; what effects add teaches none), and before that it is listed nowhere, never heard of nor opened by the floor, a fact naming it is left out and no faint line is told at it; its name stays in a page's text, as a skill's name is mostly a word of what one does (run, swim, sneak) (`CompendiumView.SkillsLearned`, `YourSkills.Learned`, `Explorer.NotMet`). A death taking a first level back below 1 hides the skill again. A skill learned is never left out under *Strict*, whatever was seen together.

The character's levels are read at most once a second while a page asks (`YourSkills`): each level as the game works with it, the one kept with what effects add, counted whole (`Skills.GetSkillLevel`), worked out from what the character has (`Skills.GetSkillList`) and its effects (`SEMan.ModifySkillLevel`) rather than by asking the game, whose `GetSkillLevel` gives the character a skill it never trained, which its save then keeps. A page told at the character's levels is told again when one of them changes.

Where a figure a page tells changes with a skill, what it comes to at the character's level stands faint under it, saying on hover that what is worn and other effects change it further (`SkillWords`, `SkillMath`, each rule as the game's code has it): a hit's damage and knockback, rolled between 25 and 55% of it at no skill and between 85 and 100% at the top (`Skills.GetRandomSkillRange`), under the damage, under each line of the attacks table, swing by swing, and at each quality in the table by quality, and ammo's knockback; an attack's stamina, eitr and health, a third less at the top (`Attack.GetAttackStamina`, `Attack.GetAttackEitr`, `Attack.GetAttackHealth`); an axe's hits on trees by the skill trees take where its attack names one (`Attack.m_specialHitSkill`, Wood Cutting); a block, half as much again at the top (`ItemDrop.ItemData.GetBlockPower`), at each quality too, and what a parry holds back (`Humanoid.BlockAttack`); a bow's full draw, down to a fifth, and its drain while drawn, and a crossbow's reload, down to half (`Humanoid.GetAttackDrawPercentage`, `ItemDrop.ItemData.GetWeaponLoadingTime`); how near a creature sees you and turns on you while you sneak, from the dark to full light (`Player.UpdateStealth`); a mount's drain and running speed with your Riding (`Sadle.UpdateRiding`, `Character.GetRunSpeedFactor`); a station's making time and its bonus of what stacks (`InventoryGui.UpdateRecipe`, `InventoryGui.DoCrafting`), a cooking station's bonus and a plant's when picked (`CookingStation.OnInteract`, `Pickable.Interact`); a build tool's cost and wear for each use (`Player.GetBuildStamina`, `Player.GetPlaceDurability`), a scythe's reach, a fishing rod's drain and reeling and what reeling a fish in drains (`FishingFloat.FixedUpdate`), a staff's summons, how many it keeps at once and how hard they hit (`SpawnAbility.Spawn`), a summon's own page how hard it hits summoned at the character's level, rolled as anything but a player rolls a hit (`Character.GetRandomSkillFactor`), and whose skill its hits train (`Character.RaiseSkill`), and the protection shield's absorbing (`SE_Shield.SetLevel`), the summons and the shield by the whole level, so effects lift them past the top; the player's own page how fast it runs at its Run. An archery target says what it trains, and an effect adding levels to a skill goes to the skill's page.

A skill's page tells the game's description of it, then a table of what it changes at no skill, at the character's level and at the top, a hit's damage and knockback with its average, the middle of its two ends as the roll falls evenly between them once they are clamped (92.5% at the top, 2.3 times no skill's), each line only where something in the world uses the skill (`SkillPageWords.Changes`, from `SkillReading`, read as a step of the catalog's reading so no page drawn pays for it), the player's own figures for moving; what raises it, and in the *You* box how far the next level is and about how much of what raises it is left, rested too (`Skills.Skill.GetNextLevelRequirement`, `Skills.Skill.Raise`); what raises it as the game raises it (`SkillPageWords.RisesBy`: a swing once however many it hits and half as much again on a creature, an area attack or a projectile as it is, `SkillMath.RisePerHit`; a pickaxe on anything that breaks but a tree or a creature; an archery target for bows and crossbows, by its share and less the farther from its middle; Blood Magic by summons and a broken shield for the one they follow or shielded; no tired jump; fishing twice as fast with a fish on; each piece taken down with the hammer cancelling the next one built, up to 20, `Player.m_buildRemoveDebt`), the way to the next level counted in hits with the weapon in hand where it trains the skill; the effects adding levels or making it rise faster; what trains it, a weapon whose hits rise the skill another share than the usual hit saying so on its chip ("×0.2 a hit"), the tools it eases, the stations it speeds and what gives a bonus by it; and what a death takes (`Skills.OnDeath`: the player prefab's share, nothing within the wait after a death, `Player.OnDeath`). A world that changes how skills rise or fall says so without changing the figures. The page for Pickaxes says that digging the ground is the same at every level, as its skill is mining. A skill a mod adds tells its level, its description and what effects add to it, as only the mod knows what it changes.
