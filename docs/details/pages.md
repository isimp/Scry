# How pages read

How a page is laid out and worded: the grid, the standard rows, the order of lists, what Scry is unsure of, topics, the header, what follows from what, and links and leftovers.

Code: `src/Model/FactLayout.cs`, `src/Catalog/Facts/Facts.cs`, `src/Model/ContentOrder.cs`, `src/Model/ItemWords.cs`, `src/UI/ScryPanel.cs`, `src/Model/CombatWords.cs`, `src/Preview/Previews.cs`, `src/Model/LinkBook.cs`.

See also: [game rules](game-rules.md), [sources and mods](sources-and-mods.md), [standards](../standards.md); all notes in [the index](../index.md).

## How pages read

Resistances show as a grid of the ten damage types in the game's order, the same on every creature, damageable piece, rock and tree, each cell the share of that damage taken as `HitData.ApplyModifier` scales it (75, 50 or 25% resisted, 125, 150 or 200% for weakness, 0% for immune or unaffected), coloured by how it lands, the game's word on hover (`ResistWords`). Up to five cells stand in a row, fewer where the side is narrow. Armour's and a shield's own show as a grid when they resist anything, else as a line saying they resist nothing; a weak spot stays one line, telling how it differs from the body.

Pages of one type show the same rows a player looks for, saying "none" or "no" where that is the answer rather than leaving the line out; a "none" nobody asks about is left out, so pages stay short. A creature always tells its attacks (a hunting one with no attack Scry can read says so as unsure), weak spots, whether it can be tamed and its drops, and an animal that it only flees (`AnimalAI.UpdateAI`). Every item tells whether it goes through portals; gear how far it upgrades, its wear (or that it never wears out) and where it is repaired or that it cannot be (`InventoryGui.CanRepair`, `ItemWords`); armour its armour, movement, set bonus and what it resists or changes while worn; a weapon or shield its block and a weapon its second attack. Furniture tells its comfort, a piece some tool builds its cost (free where it is), and what breaks its tool tier ("any") and that it gives nothing where it does.

Lists of content come in one order on every page (`ContentOrder`). Loot comes rarest first: a creature's drops by their chance, a drop table's items (a chest's filling, what a rock or a tree gives) by their weight, which within one table orders them as their chance does, a place's chests and pickups by how likely each is there, and the loot of a dungeon's rooms in the fewest kinds of room first. Creatures come toughest first, bosses and then the most health: on a biome's page, at a place's or its rooms' spawn points, in a raid and in a nest. Where a thing comes from comes surest first, each line knowing how often it gives the thing (`SourceWords.Sureness`: a drop's chance, about how often one opening of a drop table gives it as `DropTable.GetDropList` rolls, a nest's share, everything else always), equally sure ones by the biome players reach first, and the places it is found in go by the biome of the location going by each, then by name. On a biome's page what grows comes hardest to gather first (the tool tier it needs, then its health), places the fewest the world places first (`ZoneLocation.m_quantity` over every set of rules it is placed by), raids in the order the Raids tab lists them, fish and the rest by name. Each order keeps names in order among equals, and what the game itself lists in an order players see in play (a trader's wares, a station's recipes, a recipe's ingredients) keeps it. Every order reads each item's keys once into a table of numbers and sorts the items' places in it, the order given deciding between equals as LINQ's ordering does, so ordering one class of item shares its compiled code with every other.

Scry is sure of what it reads from the game's data and of what it works out by the game's own rules, whose code the startup check watches. The rest it tells as its best knowledge, marked with a `~` before the label, in a softer colour, saying why on hover (`UnsureWords`): a mod named by clues rather than by Jotunn's registry, where the mod registers what it adds (the clue each thing was named by is kept, `Entry.ModClue`, and a mod's page lists clue-matched things in rows of their own); every note on mods hooking into a rule, as only the mod knows what its code does; what was seen dropping in play, which is not the odds; an item or creature Scry found nothing giving or placing, which something it cannot read still may; a skill a mod adds that it names nowhere Scry reads; and the report's things not placed.

*In the game* is laid out by topic for the kinds laid out so far, each named topic in a box of its own, its name bright in the box's top left, so it is told apart from the page's sections (`ScryPanel.FactPlan.cs`, `Skin.TopicFill`, `TopicName`), every kind but other: creatures, items, pieces, resources, spawners, projectiles, places, raids, biomes, status effects and mods (`FactLayout`): the chains it is a step of right under its tiles, each shown whole with its own step lit and the others going to their pages (`ChainBook`, `Chains`: a creature's parents to what its young grows into, a seed to its harvest, a boss's offering through its altar to its power; the altar is the place a location's altar stands in, `Summon.PlacePrefab`), with the compendium on a step not met reading ??? and going nowhere, so the chain stands whole whatever the character met of it (`ScryPanel.ChainRow`); topics in an order fixed for the kind, or for what the page tells, the first order whose key fact or row is told (a boss's, a tameable creature's, a room's), each under a small heading, led by what the topic is looked at for (`FactLayout.Lead`: a weapon's attacks and damage by quality before what attacking costs and its blocking, armour by quality and its set, a creature's attacks and stars before what hurts it, a raid's and a spawner's creatures), the rest in the order read; a few headline numbers as tiles, a set of them chosen by what the thing is (a creature's health, faction, how it moves, whether it can be tamed, and what it sees and hears; a weapon's damage, weight, quality and wear, armour's armour and movement, food's food); what an item or piece is for in a topic of its own right under the tiles, *What it does*, where that is the point of the page (a trophy's Forsaken power, a building tool's tabs, what a fish bites on, a bait catches and a key opens; a station's making, a machine's work, an area's, a chest's slots and contents, a door's key), shown only where told; the list of where it comes from and what it is used for under their topic's heading without a title of their own; what qualifies a row told under it as a note (what stars leave as it is among a creature's drops (`DropWords.StarDrops`) and the world's rate under its drops); facts that vary along one thing as a table, a creature's attacks by what each does, how (naming the projectile it fires), what it puts on you where any does, its reach and how often (`CombatWords.AttackColumnsFor`, `Facts.Row.Columns`), the projectile and the status effect each a link to its page (`Facts.Row.CellLinks`), a rock's, vein's or tree's tools that break it, the weapons, pickaxes and torches players swing (`ItemData.IsWeapon`; a trophy's damage figure is never dealt) whose tool tier is at least its own and which deal a damage type it takes, of those the tools made for it by the skill the game trains with them, axes for what is chopped and pickaxes for what is mined (a crossbow's bit of chop makes it no tool for a tree), every one where any is and all that break it where none is, the weakest first (`GatherWords.BreaksIt`; a world with tools locked to the world level refuses more), and what to bring against it as chips that run the search (`SearchFight.Bring`, `EntryKeys.Search`): the weapons dealing a type it takes more of, the gear resisting an element its attacks deal, each attack's name playing the clip that begins it on the stage (`Previews.ClipOfAttackItem`), lit while it plays and stopping it when clicked again, as the clip's chip does, a weapon's attacks, one or two, the same on the person wearing it, put on the person first where it is shown on its own (`Previews.PlayAttackWhenKnown`), its health, damage and, where they grow with stars, drops by star (`CombatWords.StarLines`), what grows with quality by level, damage, armour, block, block force, durability and the station level each quality needs, a weapon's with its fight and armour's with its wearing (`ItemWords.DamageByQuality`, `ByQuality`, `StationLevels`), the facts themselves at the first quality, a raid's creatures by how many at once, how often, at what chance, their stars and the rest (`RaidWords.BringLine`), a spawner's by their share and stars (`SpawnWords.PoolLine`), a biome's weathers and its music by the time of day (`BiomeWords.WeatherColumns`, `MusicColumns`), a line naming music playing it, a column as wide as what it holds; every fact read shown once, going to a topic by its label, else by the part of the reader that told it (`Facts.PairParts`, `Row.Part`, noted as each part reads), else to *More* at the end. The other kinds keep their facts, rows and blocks in the order they always had, until they are laid out.

A page's header stays in sight while its sections scroll under it, the same rows in the full view and the compact one: the name, with copying it and keeping it a favourite as square buttons at its row's end; the line under it saying where it comes from (the kind first in the compact view); a toolbar, what the entry does as one joined group from the left (*Show in the world*, *Ragdoll*, *Fire where you look*, *Play where you look*...), the leading one in the accent where it sends something into the world and a switch lit as the panel's switches are while on, one that can do nothing for now greyed in its place, one that needs the stage left out without one, and the slider icons as one joined group at its first line's end, the actions wrapping as a group under them where the line has no room (`PageToolbar`, `ScryPanel.Toolbar`); and a line naming each section, with how many it holds where its heading tells it (`SectionLine`, `ScryPanel.SectionLine.cs`): a click brings that section's heading to the top, as far as the page scrolls, opening it where it is folded, and the section being read is lit, the one a click went to while the page stays there, else the last whose heading has come within the top third, or the last of all at the page's end. Each heading notes where it stands as the sections are drawn, and the line, drawn before them, is laid out from where the last draw found them; a page of one section names it too, and at the line's end an icon folds every section away, or opens every one where none is open. What the actions note, a dungeon example's roll, a copy in the world being yours alone, stands at the top of the page under the header.

## What follows from what

A creature whose defeat sets a world key (`Character.m_defeatSetGlobalKey`) tells what follows: the raids that may then come and those that stop (`RandomEvent.m_requiredGlobalKeys`, `m_notRequiredGlobalKeys`), what the world's spawning and spawners then place and what spawners stop placing (`SpawnData.m_requiredGlobalKey`, `CreatureSpawner.m_requiredGlobalKey`, `m_blockingGlobalKey`), and what traders then sell (`Trader.TradeItem.m_requiredGlobalKey`), each kept as the catalog reads them (`UnlockBook`). A world set to pick raids by each player's own progress checks other keys, so its raids are not told under a key.

A copy let fall strikes the ground as its prefab does: the copies keep none of the game's scripts, so where the prefab has an `ImpactEffect` (a felled log has one) Scry plays its hit effect where the copy strikes, faster than its least speed and no sooner than its interval, on the stage as the stage's and in the world where it strikes (`Thud`), in the frame after the contact, as making the effect's copies strips them, which Unity forbids during a physics contact; it deals no damage. Everything a felling or breaking leaves on the stage is heard as if beside you, the log's own sounds too.

A ballista names the ammunition it fires, how many shots it holds, whom it shoots within its sight and how often, and the trophies that, given to it, make it shoot only their creatures (`Turret`, through `BaseAI.FindClosestCreature`); a trap on whom it springs, when it rearms, the damage of what it sets off and whether it staggers (`Trap`, its `Aoe`); a ship whether the Ashlands' boiling seas burn it and what it takes capsized (`Ship.TakeAshlandsDamage`, `m_upsideDownDmg`); a cart its own weight and how much of its load counts (`Vagon.UpdateMass`); a catapult what it loads by item type, with the items it always or never loads, and that it throws whoever stands where it is loaded (`Catapult.CanItemBeLoaded`, `CollectLaunchCharacters`). The words are `MachineWords`.

A fish names the baits it bites on, each with the chance it bites once it reaches the hook (`Fish.TestBate`), and a bait the fish it catches; a locked door names its key and whether it is used up (`Door.m_keyItem`, `m_consumeKey`) and a key the doors it opens; a boss stone's stand gives a Forsaken power for the trophies it takes (`ItemStand.m_guardianPower`, `m_supportedItems`), so a trophy names the power it gives there and a boss dropping it names its power. Each is read from the catalog's own prefabs, boss stones and dungeon doors among them as the networked prefabs they are.

Young come from somewhere too: a breeding creature's young is born to it, a young one grows up into what it names and an egg hatches into what it names, and each is told on the other's side as where it comes from (`Knowledge.Breeding`), so a mod's foal or a hen's chick has a source like any spawn.

## Links and leftovers

Besides effect lists, prefabs point at one another in many ways, and each becomes a link shown at both ends under its own heading: sounds an animation clip names, footsteps, the items a humanoid may carry, any prefab or item a field names (a projectile an attack fires, a creature's saddle, a door's key), armour sets, station upgrades, ammo, and the status effects prefabs give. A crafting station's upgrades are the pieces whose own station goes by its name, as the game counts them (`StationExtension.FindExtensions` compares the names; `LinkBook.AddUpgrades`), so a piece naming a mod's copy of a station upgrades the game's too; one naming a station no prefab of the catalog goes by, or one of no name, is linked to the station it names. The groups a page's topics are about show in those topics of *In the game* instead of under *Linked* (`FactLayout.Places`): what gives a status effect under *How you get it*, a set piece's other pieces right under its set bonus, a creature's carried items and what its attacks put on you in its fight, what a biome's weather puts on you with its weathers. *Linked* leaves out any link *In the game* shows already (`Facts.LinkedTargets`, `FactLayout.LeftInLinked`), so each shows once, but for an animation's, whose chip also plays it. The groups' names are words (`LinkWords`). A prefab the scene does not register has no entry, yet many do their work through one (a staff's projectile raising a troll); what its own fields name is linked from its user instead.

A creature's home is where its world spawns put it with no world event or key asked for (`SpawnBiomes`): a spawn asking for a weather only a world event brings, or waiting for a world event to be on where it would spawn (`m_requiredPersistentEvent`, Fimbulvinter's Jotun warriors and Elakingar), brings it there only during the event; a spawn waiting for a world key adds the biomes it names as where it comes later (the Charred to the other biomes once Fader is defeated), unless every spawn of it waits for one (the Jotun warriors' Deep North patrols), which makes them its home. A biome's page lists each apart. Loot is told by what it is worth having (`ContentOrder.LootFirst`): what nothing else in the world gives and nothing makes first, marked *only here* (`LootMarks.OnlyHere`, from every source line, station and place that gives it), then what a trader pays for, the most first, marked with its coins (`StoreGui.SellItem` pays an item's value for each one; coins themselves none), then the rest; each the least likely first, by a real chance: a creature's drop by its chance a kill, a table's by its share of a roll, a dungeon's room loot by the best chance a chest or pickup in its rooms gives it, which its chip tells. A place's chests and pickups are told the same way by what they can give (`ContentOrder.GiversFirst`): one that can give what only this place gives first, then by the most a trader pays for anything it can give, then by how many kinds of loot it gives (a chest before barley), each marked as its loot would be.

What a prefab leaves behind is paired with it: a creature's ragdoll, a tree's log and stump, a log's halves, a rock's broken version and the debris of anything destroyed. A leftover is named after its owner ("Troll · ragdoll"), takes its owner's kind so it sits beside it in the list, and links to it. One shared by many owners is named for what it is and how many leave it ("Debris of 30"). In a kind's tab a leftover stands indented right under its owner where both are in one group, and so does an item's variant under the item it copies (`Leftovers.UnderOwners`, `LinkBook.VariantOf`).
