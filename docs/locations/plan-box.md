# The plan box, rooms and the room card

The plan box inside a dungeon (Above, Levels, Tree), a room peeked or gone into, and the room card.

Code: `src/Preview/Stage.cs`, `src/Model/RoomFocus.cs`, `src/Model/DungeonWays.cs`, `src/UI/ScryPanel.cs`, `src/Model/RoomLight.cs`, `src/Model/PanelPlace.cs`, `src/Model/CornerFit.cs`, `src/Preview/StageExample.cs`.

See also: [examples](examples.md), [this dungeon](this-dungeon.md); all notes in [the index](../index.md).

## The plan box

A dungeon's inside has its plan box in the stage's lower left corner (`ScryPanel.PlanBox`, its words from `PlanBoxWords`). Its header holds three segments for its views from the left, and in the box's top right corner, beside them, a button that grows it to about 400 by 330, scaled with the panel, and its fold, marked with a chevron pointing down, both square buttons; where the segments at their width leave them no room, the segments are drawn tighter, and only where even that leaves none do the two buttons take a second row under the segments. A line under the header runs across the box. Under it the plan's own switches stand over the plan's top right corner, the switch for the rooms below (with *Above* only) and the *Aa* switch (on the grown plan only, as only it names rooms), joined into one piece rounded at its ends with a point's seam between them where both stand, each mark drawn over its button in the accent while on, the plan's rooms under them not picked through them (`PlanBoxHeader`), and how much of the world's own dungeon is walked stands in the plan's bottom left corner. *Above* is the plan from above, turned with the view and at one scale whichever way, each room shaded by its ground, the deepest darkest and the entrance in the accent; with a room gone into, that room is lit and the rooms above it, put away on the stage, stand as dashes, so the rooms under them show.

*Levels* shows each level of the example as a floor plate seen at a slant, the highest first and one under the other, turned with the view as Above is and at one scale, each plate's depth squashed to 45% and its far side shifted left by 35% of the depth between, taken from the whole example's far side so a spot over another on the next level stands straight above it, the plates as big as the box holds (`LevelPlates`); the plates and the rooms' tiles on them are worked out again only as the levels, the turn or the box change; going down from the highest room, a room joins the level above it while its ground lies within two storeys (5 m) under that level's lowest and it covers none of the level's rooms from above, else it begins a level under it, so rooms stepping down a little at a time share a level, a room far under the rest stands on its own, and no room of a level covers another (`PlanLevels`); each level's depth, its highest room's ground in whole metres from the entrance's, stands beside it, and a way down drops straight from its doorway on the level above to the same doorway on the level below. *Tree* sets a row for each room more in from the entrance, the entrance alone at the top and rooms no way reaches in a row under the rest; each room hangs under the room it is first reached from on the way in, those under one room as a person walking into it meets their doors: behind a door on the left to the left, straight on below, on the right to the right, by the turn from the way walked in (within an eighth of a turn straight on), the entrance walked into through its own door out; a room with none under it has a place of its own, all such places evenly across, and a room stands over the room straight on from it, so a corridor that carries on keeps its line, else over the middle of those under it, so each branch keeps its own span and the tree's lines never cross, ways closing a loop dashed; the tree stands the same whichever way the view turns, the same ways laying out the same each time (`TreeRows`); crossroads are outlined, a dead end is shut at its foot by a heavier bottom edge, and beside a node, right of it or else left where it covers no other label or node, those of the room gone into and the rooms lit first, stands only what stands out: the entrance and the deepest room named in the accent, and an accent dot where this roll left chests or pickups in the room (`ScryPanel.TreeMarksForTest`); grown, it also gives its name under it, or over it for every other node of its row, as wide as two places of the row and in smaller type where needed, left out where it still does not fit.

From above the ways between rooms are walkways, so they run along the rooms' walls as a person walks them rather than across rooms: in each room a hall along its longer side through its middle, reaching the farthest foot of its doorways either way and always its middle, and each doorway it shares with another room joined to the hall square to its walls, a doorway in a wall along the hall straight in, one in an end wall beside the hall stepping in 3 m first (half way to the middle in a small room) and then turning to it, a hole in the floor as the wall it is nearer would (`RoomWalkway`); a hall reaches only as far as the ways on the way from the entrance, a way closing a loop going on past its end to its doorway as part of that way; the way from the entrance runs along the halls from the entrance's middle through each doorway to the room's middle, along the halls of the rooms it passes rather than by their middles; all of it is worked out once for an example's ways and only turned and scaled as the view turns (`PlanWalkways`). Every room of a dungeon's layout stands at a quarter turn to the others in the layouts looked at, so the walkways stand square to each other too; a room at any other turn would have its walkway square to its own walls.

In the levels the same walkways stand on each level's plate, a way between rooms on two plates dropping in dashes from its doorway on the one to the same doorway on the other, the way from the entrance dropping so too; in the tree each way runs square as well, down from the higher node to half way over the lower one's row, across and down into it, straight down where one stands under the other, two nodes of one row joined under it, or over it on the last row (`TreeElbow`); from above and in the levels a dot at each doorway (`DungeonWays.DoorBetween`); in every view loops dashed (`DungeonWays.IsLoop`: a way on neither room's way from the entrance through the fewest). A room under the mouse, in the box or on the stage, is lit in both, that room alone rather than every room of its name: framed in the box, the way from the entrance to it amber and bolder, and outlined on the stage with the fainter amber line, with or without a room gone into, as a way chip of the room card lights its room (`RoomLight`); the box finds the room under the mouse only once its rooms are drawn, so it frames the one found when last looked; a click on a room in the box goes into it. With none lit, the way to the room gone into is amber and bolder.

Where the view is stands on the breadcrumb in the stage's top left, where the kind badge stands elsewhere (`DungeonTrail`, `ScryPanel.Breadcrumb`): the dungeon, named as its page names it, a click on it showing the whole of it with the roof on, a ▾ beside it opening a menu that goes into the entrance or the deepest room, the one the most rooms in from the entrance (the tree's last row, not merely one of the lowest; of those as far in the lowest, then the one placed first, `DungeonWays.Deepest`); the level open or the one the room gone into is on, a click on it coming back out of the room to it, or, with no room gone into, framing the level again; and the room gone into, a click on it framing it again; the part where the view is stands lit. The *Aa* switch beside grow names the rooms on the grown plan in every view, or leaves every name off, the tree's depths kept; it is remembered with the panel (`ScryPanel.PlanNamesShown`). The switch for the rooms below acts on the plan from above alone: off, it leaves out of it the rooms whose ground lies below the level looked at, the room gone into's or the floor opened, with neither nothing, and marks each way down from a room it shows with a downward mark in the accent at its doorway, which stands for the room it leads to: the mouse on it lights that room as a room under the mouse is lit, and a click goes into it, the scale kept so nothing jumps; the stage shows those rooms as it does either way (`RoomFocus.HiddenBelow`); it is kept with the panel's place (`PanelPlace.PlanBelow`); a self-test run shows them while it runs and puts the switch back after, and while it runs the player's own mouse lights no room, in the box, on the stage or on the card, only where the test points it (`ScryPanel.MouseAwayForTest`).

The box stays under the breadcrumb, and with a room gone into, under the card at its least; with a room gone into on a stage too short for it at its own least, it folds to its tab, unlit, its tip saying why, and comes back as the stage grows or the room is come out of (`CornerFit.Shape`). Grown, the stage frames what it shows beside the box rather than behind it (`Stage.ViewShift`, the camera's target moved left by that share of the picture). Which view is shown and whether the box is grown are kept with the panel's place (`PanelPlace.PlanView`, `PlanBig`). The levels tell rooms over each other apart by level, a level's depth within it only by its shade; the tree tells the ways in, not where the rooms lie, which Above and Levels show; a crowded row of the compact tree leaves some labels out, which growing the box gives room.

## Rooms peeked and gone into

A room of a dungeon's example under the mouse, on the stage or in the plan box, peeks: its name, where it is, how many creatures and chests and pickups this copy rolled in it, and what a click and a double-click do (`StageWords.RoomPeek`). A click picks it, outlined and shown on the card where the view is, the view left as it is, a click on no room or Esc letting go of it (`Stage.PickedRoom`); a double-click, Enter, or *Go in* on the card of the room picked goes into it (a camp's room still goes to its entry on a click): the room stands whole, cut at head height over its own ground, or just under its own ceiling where that is lower (`RoomFocus.Cut`), its ground being its own main floor as its rays found it where that floor has room to stand about, 30 m² as a level of its own needs, rather than a tunnel's scrap of flat rock, else its lowest doorway, where it is walked into (`RoomFocus.Ground`, `RoomFocus.RealFloor`). A room with no such floor whose doorways stand more than a storey apart, a rock tube in The Hole climbing 10 m between its doorways or the sunken crypts' stairs, is cut instead along its slope: a plane a head's height over the middle of its lowest doorways and the middle of its highest, level square to the way between them, so its roof comes off along its whole length and its floor stays (`SlopeCut`, `RoomCutPlane`, laid as the near plane as a level cut is, `Stage.SlopeInWorld`); where that way is steeper than 60°, a shaft such as the caves' holes, it is cut flat a head's height over its highest doorways, so it is looked down.

The slope moves up and down with the cut; the outline's top follows it corner by corner; each creature is kept to the cut's height where it stands; and a click picks from where its ray crosses the slope. Beyond the room the slope goes on, so a room past its lower end is cut lower and one past its upper end higher, and a tube that bends between its doorways is cut by a straight slope. An end cap or a divider takes the look of the room it closes, the room the layout joined it to, so it is never lit, dimmed or put away apart from it, and the mouse on it on the stage finds that room (`ExamplePlan.LooksAs`, `Stage.ExampleRoomAt`). Around the room gone into, the rooms whose ground lies within 1.5 m of its own stand half dimmed (every colour at 45%), those lower dimmed (at 30%) and those higher put away (`RoomFocus.Look`, `StageExample.LookOf`), and the camera glides to look down into it, framed on its box, at the zoom the view had where it comes from another room and afresh from the whole dungeon (`RoomFocus.ZoomGoingInto`, `StageExample.FocusAcross`). An amber line, the panel's accent, traces every edge of its box from its ground up to its top, however low the cut, drawn over everything so it is found among rooms that look alike and its whole size shows while the floor is lowered (`RoomOutline`, `Stage.Outline`, drawn with Unity's own shader for coloured lines, else the sprites' one, which walls hide); the outlines stand on a layer of their own, which the film leaves out, and are drawn at the very end of the film by a command buffer with the camera's projection without the cut, at no cost of filming again (`Stage.OutlinesInFilm`), and where the stage finds no third free layer, they are drawn with the cut, to just under it.

A room has floors of its own (*Floors in this room* on its card; a level is always the dungeon's, as the plan, the breadcrumb and the ruler name it): its ground, and each other floor its rays found it stands on with 30 m² to stand about on and at least head height (2.5 m) from its ground and from each other, the bigger kept where two are nearer (`RoomLevels`), as a tall Mörkhalla hall's second storey or a cave's gallery high up, where steps, landings and platforms near the ground are none; opening one moves the cut to head height over it and the camera to it, the rooms around keeping their looks by the room's ground (`Stage.OpenRoomLevel`). An example that is mostly one stack of rooms, with no crossroads and more than half its rooms under one whose foot is at least 2 m higher (`DungeonWays.IsStack`; of every dungeon over eight rolls each, only Mörkhalla), opens cut from the side (`Stage.CutFromSide`; the cut control's *Top* and *Side* choose either while a dungeon's inside is shown): an upright plane through the room gone into's middle, else the middle of the example's rooms, facing away from the camera level with the ground, takes away the half of every room facing the camera and turns as the camera turns (`SideCut`, laid as the near plane as the cut from above is); a room gone into so holds no cut over it, and the rooms above it are dimmed rather than put away, as the section shows them; creatures are cut with the rest rather than drawn again, as those in the half taken away would stand before the cut's face; and a click on the stage picks from the cut on.

The cut is held at the room's height whatever the place's floors (`FloorCut.Hold`), and the ruler at the stage's right edge is the room's (`RoomRuler`, `Stage.RoomRulerNow`): it spans the room's lowest level up to the top of the rooms above it, with a whole mark for each of its own floors, named as the card names them, and a shorter one for each level of the rooms above, those within 1.5 m of each other sharing a mark named by how many rooms and how far up, the mark the cut stands over lit and named beside the bar. A drag on the ruler moves the cut within it, never lower than half a metre over its lowest floor, raised past the top the room's roof on; each notch of Shift and the wheel, of the wheel on the ruler, the plan box or the card, and each press of Page Up and Down steps the cut to the next mark, past the top the room's roof on and down from there its top mark again; down past its lowest mark, or up with its roof on, a notch goes on to the level below or above (`WheelNotches`, `Stage.StepRoomCut`); the cut control's *No cut* and *Cut from above* put the room's roof on and take it off, still in the room (`FloorCut.HeldRoofOn`). Page Up and Down carry on past the room's ruler: down past its lowest tick, out to the level below, up past its roof, out to the level above, each opened on the room as coming back out does, past the top level the roof on (`Stage.ComeOutTo`, `LevelCuts.ComeOutTo`).

A room above, put away, comes back half dimmed, cut with the rest, once the cut is half a metre over its ground; one standing over the room gone into, by its box seen from above, only once the cut is also raised past where the room was first cut, so a shaft first cut a head's height over its highest doorway does not bring back at once a room over it there; the plan box dashes the rooms put away so. Its ruler lights a mark of the rooms above only once they are back, and with the room's roof on every room above is back (`RoomFocus.WithCut`); the room's outline stands meanwhile, so it is found under them. The card's level segments light the level the cut stands over. Cut from the side, the room has no height to cut at: no ruler stands and the wheel leaves the cut as it is, a room dimmed or half dimmed around it can be clicked to go into next, as with a floor opened can a room dimmed below it, each lit as the mouse is on it (`RoomLight.Choosable`), and Esc or the breadcrumb's level comes back out to the level of the plan the room is on, opened, the view on the room at the zoom and reach it had before the first room was gone into, the room outlined until the mouse moves (`Stage.ComeOut`, `Stage.CameOutOf`); cut from the side, or out of a room on no level, back to the whole example with the roof on, as another place shown does.

Going into a room, from room to room and coming back out are steps of a history, as in a browser: the mouse's back and forward buttons, Alt and the arrows and the panel's ‹ and › step back to the view a step was taken from, as it was left, and forward again, before they step through the entries shown (`Stage.StepBack`, `ViewSteps`); a change of the floor opened is no step, and the steps go with the example they are of.

## The room card

While a room is gone into, its card stands in the stage's top left, under the breadcrumb and above the plan, built from the panel's own labels, chips, segments and buttons (`ScryPanel.RoomCard`, its words from `RoomCardWords` and `RoomCard`). It gives the room's name, its page a link at the line's end (*Its page*, going to the room's entry); how far its ground lies below or above the entrance's in whole metres, how many rooms in it is and how many ways lead on (`DungeonWays.StepsIn`, `WaysOf`); the creatures this preview's spawn points rolled in it, counted by name, the most first (`StageCreatures.RolledIn`); and the chests and pickups this preview's rolls left in it, copies numbered apart such as "Morkhalla Eye (4)" counted as one thing (`Naming.WithoutVariant`), each with the chance its own page gives it, read off the copy while it still sleeps and before its rolls by the same rule the page is read by (`PlaceReader.LootChances`, kept on `PlaceRoll.Loot`). A chip for each way on, the nearest the entrance first, names its room alone, cut at its end with an ellipsis where too long (`TextFit.Cut`), and says on hover how far up or down a way more than 1.5 m off the room's ground goes and whether it is a dead end; a click goes into its room, and while hovered it lights that room on the plan and on the stage with a fainter amber line, its whole box outlined however low the cut (`Stage.LitRoom`).

Where the room has floors beside its ground, they stand as segments under *Floors in this room*, the one open lit. The creatures and the chests and pickups each stand folded to a heading with how many they hold until opened, a click on the heading opening or folding the list as a page's section does, remembered with the panel (`ScryPanel.CardListOpen`). An open list holds six lines at most, the last saying how many more, and a creature's or chest's line with a page of its own goes to it on a click; the card stands as tall as it wants, else as much as is left over the plan box, giving way to it and scrolling the rest, down to its least, under which the plan gives way instead (`CornerFit`); under the strict rules, what was not seen together with the dungeon's page is left out and counted, as on a page, a list whose every line was left out saying only how many (`RoomCardWords.ListLines`). The card takes the mouse where it stands, so a click or the wheel on it neither turns nor zooms the stage.
