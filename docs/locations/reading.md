# Locations: reading them

How the world's locations and dungeon rooms are read, kept and shown on the stage with their creatures, what their copy leaves out, their runestones and music.

Code: `src/Model/PlaceFinds.cs`, `src/Model/StageCamera.cs`, `src/Model/SpawnPoints.cs`, `src/Catalog/PlaceReader.cs`, `src/Model/PreOrderWalk.cs`, `src/Model/PlaceContents.cs`, `src/Model/PlaceTerms.cs`, `src/Model/Places.cs`.

See also: [floors](floors.md), [examples](examples.md), [catalog](../catalog.md); all notes in [the index](../index.md).

## Reading them

Where things are found in the world's locations and dungeon rooms is read in the background: with *Find where things are*, with `/scry locations`, or by itself when `ReadLocationsAutomatically` is on, as it is unless turned off (*Every world* beside *Find where things are* above the Locations tab's list, which starts the reading here as it is turned on), and once read it is kept for the next time. The game keeps locations and rooms as soft references to asset bundles it loads while building a zone, and loading its 550 or so at once stalls the game for seconds. So one at a time is loaded in the background, read three milliseconds a frame, and released: two to three minutes in all, about two seconds of it Scry's own work. A location's contents are read a part of the reading at a time, each a walk over its prefab for one kind of part (`PlaceReader.Steps`), and then its parts a few at a time in the order the prefab holds them (`PreOrderWalk`), so a location of tens of thousands of parts takes no frame of its own. Only names and figures are kept: a record of each place as its bundle holds it (`PlaceRecord`), with the game's keys for its names and texts rather than their words, the weathers it sets by name, and which of its rolls can come out otherwise, told in the world and language it is shown in whenever it is told (`PlaceContents.Tell`, `PlaceTerms`), so a place read in English reads right in German, and its stars leave it to chance only in a world that rolls stars. Nothing a reading reads shows until all of it is read, so a reading can be stopped at any point and leaves the world as it was. A place in an entry's *Found in* goes to the location of that name, the first one where several share it, as the variants of a dungeon do; a dungeon room's place is its dungeon's (`Places.LocationNamed`). A place no location goes by, a mod's room no dungeon here is built with, searches for what is found there instead.

What is read is kept from one session to the next in `BepInEx/cache/isimp.Scry/places.dat` (`PlaceRecords`, `KeptPlaces`): a run of lines for each place closed by an end line, so a place cut short, or with a line that cannot be read, is read again while the rest are kept, and a file of another kind gives nothing. Each place carries a stamp (`PlaceStamps`): the build of Scry that read it, the mods loaded then, as any of them may change a place in memory, and its bundle's file by its name, size and when it was written, as the game's own list of its bundles names it (its `SoftRef` manifest, read as `AssetBundleManifest.DeserializeFromDisk` reads it). On entering a world, once its catalog is read, the file and the stamps are found on a worker thread and what is kept of the world's places is shown at once (`PlacePlan`): a place whose stamp is the same now, and whose names the catalog holds as many of as when it was read, holds the same; one whose stamp changed (an update of the game rewrote its bundle, the mods changed, or another build of Scry read it), or that names a prefab the catalog no longer has or has gained, is shown as kept and read again in the background, and the world's places are told anew once that is done, each time from the catalog as it was made, so nothing of what was told before is left (`PlaceFinds`); a place never kept is read with them.

Telling the places on the catalog, from what was kept or once a reading is done, is spread over frames three milliseconds at a time: each place told in the session's terms and where everything it names is found worked out and ordered a slice at a time (`PlaceFinds.Steps`), the catalog left as it was meanwhile, then all of it put on at once in a frame of its own (`PlaceFinds.Publish`), so no frame shows half of it; a reading waits while it is told. With nothing kept, the places wait to be asked for, or are read by themselves with `ReadLocationsAutomatically`. `/scry locations` reads every one again, what is known staying shown meanwhile. `/scry forget`, or *Look again* in the bar above the Locations tab's list once the places are known, forgets everything kept, the places file and every floor file, for when what is kept seems wrong though no stamp changed (`Locations.ForgetKept`): the world's places are told as never read, each place going by what it went by before it was read and no entry found in any (`PlaceFinds`), and every one is read afresh in the background. The file is written on a worker thread, from the places as they were once read, one job after another with its finding (`FileQueue`), aside and then swapped into place (`FileSwap`). It lives in BepInEx's cache folder, which a profile's sync and export leave out (they take the config folders and every file of the kinds settings are written in), so each player keeps their own, and a place read again whenever it may have changed costs nothing to lose.

Every location and room has an entry from the start, made from what the game keeps of it in memory without loading its bundle: a location from the world's location list (`ZoneSystem.m_locations`, one entry for all the rule sets of one prefab), a room from the dungeon database. A location's details tell the rules each rule set places it by, as `ZoneSystem.GenerateLocationsTimeSliced` applies them to every point it tries, each rule left out while it is at its default. Woods are told by how open they are, the game's forest factor (`WorldGenerator.GetForestFactor`), noise from 0 for the thickest woods to about 2.2, with the Meadows wooded below 1.15. The ground value a location asks for is the lava in the Ashlands (from 0.6 the game counts it as lava), the Mistlands' growth, and 0 in every other biome (`WorldGenerator.GetBiomeHeight`), so there a minimum is never met and a maximum always is; a check of the surroundings, which keeps spots with more of that value around them than the average spot meeting the other rules, is left out where the biome has none, as there it measures nothing.

## On the stage

Selecting one loads its bundle in the background and holds it while it is shown, one at a time; the stage says so while it loads, and a bundle that cannot be loaded says so there and in the details (`PlaceAssets`). Once loaded it is read (`PlaceReader`), a part of the reading at a time within three milliseconds of each frame, its copy shown once it is, the stage saying meanwhile that its model is loading (`Previews.ReadPlaceOn`): its game name, the networked parts it places with how many and how likely, the creatures its spawn points place, where its vegvisirs point, the levels it sets for its spawn points, how far around it nothing can be built, how a dungeon or camp in it is built, and a room's box, kind and doorways. A part's chance multiplies every roll above it, as `ZoneSystem.SpawnLocation` and `DungeonGenerator.PlaceRoom` roll them: each `RandomSpawn` at its chance, each `RandomObject` picking one entry by weight (an entry without an object leaves the whole pick out), and a `RandomSpawn`'s object for when it is off the rest of the time. Its entry then takes the game's name, and *Find where things are* fills every entry the same way. Its page tells its parts in a row by what each is (`PlaceParts.RoleOf`): chests and pickups, what is gathered (rocks, trees and ore, and what only breaks), the rest (spawners, creatures, stations, beds, fires, altars, item stands, vegvisirs, runestones, portals, traders), then the creatures its spawn points place, and its building pieces last under *Built of*. A chest or a pickup counts as one whatever else it is, a spawner or something used for what it does goes with the rest even when it is built, and something mined or felled is gathered even when it is a piece.

The stage copy is rolled the same way while it still sleeps, so the prefab itself is never touched (`PlaceCopy`), and *Roll again* makes a new copy with new rolls; it shows only for a place where something is left to chance, its creatures included. The wheel zooms toward what is under the pointer, which stays under it, as near as two metres however big what is framed (`StageCamera`), over a single thing rather than a place only up or down, so it keeps turning about its middle as it spins (`StageCamera.ZoomTowardHeight`); an effect that has played out keeps its stage, filmed empty, and a click on it plays it again (`StageWords.PlayedOut`), and a drag with the right button keeps what was grabbed under the pointer. With a floor opened the camera looks at that floor: at its height, over the middle of the rooms of the example standing on it (or of the whole place), framed on how far they reach, gliding to the next as floors are stepped; what is under the pointer is then found on that floor, and a drag moves the view along it. The camera stays over a floor's cut, as a cut only opens what is seen from above it: raised by a step or by the ruler, the cut takes the camera up with it, and the wheel comes no nearer than keeps it there (`StageCamera.OverCut`). A location's *Biome* line goes to the biome's page, or names its biomes as chips where it has several.

## Their creatures

Its creature spawn points (`CreatureSpawner`) put their creatures on the stage with it, a dungeon's or camp's rooms each with their own, rolled as the game spawns them once you come near (`SpawnPoints`): a point on its own always spawns; points of one group within their two radii of each other, step by step, keep up to their most alive, each picked by weight among those not yet spawned (`CheckGroupSpawnBlocked`, `Group.SpawnWeighted`); a group the location blocks spawns nothing (`Location.m_blockSpawnGroups`). Each creature's level goes up from the point's least while a roll under its chance holds, to its most, the location's overrides applying to every group it does not leave out (`CreatureSpawner.Spawn`), the chance as the world has it (`SpawnSystem.GetLevelUpChance`, its world level or enemy level-up rate; the share a world sector adds is left out, as the stage stands in none). Every point is rolled whatever the world's progress or the time of day.

The points are read off the copy while it sleeps, before stripping takes them off; each creature stands on the first solid thing straight down from a metre above its point, as the game drops what it spawns (`ZoneSystem.FindFloor`, on the layers it counts solid, noted off the copy while it sleeps, as awake it is all on the stage's layer), or at its point where nothing is under it; one that flies (`Character.m_flying`) is as high over that ground as its AI keeps it at the least once it takes off (`BaseAI.m_flyAltitudeMin`) (`SpawnPoints.Rise`). Each faces a way of its own as the game turns one it spawns, dressed and starred as a creature selected is, held in its first pose (its animator evaluated once and switched off, to spare the frame) and muted, hung on the point so it goes with what holds it, dimmed with its room. Hanging inside the place's copy, their animators are left out of the one the stage takes as the copy's own (`ClipPlayer.AnimatorOf`, `StageCreatures.Holds`), and a place's details offer no animations at all (`Kinds.OffersClips`): its doors, gates and creatures are not it. A few are made each frame. They stand on a second free layer, the next unnamed one below the stage's or else one of those Unity keeps spare under 8 (7, 6, 3), as a game with mods can have every layer from 8 up named but one.

A floor's cut, the near plane laid along it, cuts them with all else; one whose ground is above the cut stands on a floor above and stays cut away with it, on the stage's own layer while the cut is there (`SpawnPoints.AboveCut`); what of them is above it is then drawn again over the picture, by a projection whose far end lies along the cut and whose depth is cleared first (`StageCamera.AboveCutRow`). Nothing below the cut can stand before what is above it, as the camera looks from above, so a tall creature stands whole and what stands before its lower part still hides it; with no second free layer they are cut as everything is. The stage's *Creatures* chip puts them away and back, and is remembered.

## What the copy leaves out

A part's own conditions (a biome, a height, lava, a dungeon's kind) are not applied, since the copy stands in no world. A dungeon's interior is left out of the copy: the game builds it some 5,000 m above its entrance, and a camera framing both would frame neither. So is an `EnvZone`'s view of the outside, which the game draws only for someone inside. A location stands on its root, where the game stands it on the terrain, so what reaches below it is under the floor as it is under the ground; a room stands on its lowest doorway, the floor it is walked into on (`PlaceView.Ground`).

## Runestones and music

A place's runestones are read with it (`RuneStone`): every text each can give, the one it has or every one of its random texts, with their titles, a stone told once however often the place holds one alike (`PlaceRunes`). A stone with several texts gives one picked with its place in the world as the seed (`RuneStone.GetRandomText`), so a stone always gives the same one, which its section says. They show under *Runestone texts*, which folds like the other sections.

A place's music is read with it: a source of its own that `MusicLocation` starts on coming near, a piece of the game's music list that `MusicVolume` starts on stepping inside (at its chance, unless it fades in by distance), and the music of the weather an `EnvZone` sets inside (the environment's day music, else its night, morning or evening music). Enter, or a click on the *Music* line, plays the first that can be found (`MusicPreview`): the place's own clip from its loaded model, or a named piece's clip from `MusicMan.m_music`, once through, at your ears in the game's music mix and at your music volume. The game's music is muted meanwhile rather than stopped, so it goes on underneath and is heard again when the piece ends, when Enter stops it, or when anything else is selected or played.
