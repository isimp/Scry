# Floors and the cut

How a place's floors are found by rays over its colliders and kept, and how the stage cuts a place open floor by floor.

Code: `src/Model/FloorFinder.cs`, `src/Model/PlaceView.cs`, `src/Model/FloorRecord.cs`, `src/Preview/Stage.cs`, `src/Preview/FloorProbe.cs`, `src/Model/FloorFiles.cs`, `src/Preview/StageExample.cs`, `src/Model/FloorCut.cs`.

See also: [reading](reading.md), [examples](examples.md), [stage](../stage.md); all notes in [the index](../index.md).

## Floors and the cut

A dungeon whose location draws nothing outside but its example (the sealed tower) shows its inside however the Inside chip was left, with no chip to switch (`StageExample.NothingOutside`). A room opens on the stage cut above head height (2.5 m) over its top floor, or under the ceiling over that floor where that is lower, looked down into the first time; opening or stepping to a floor after leaves the camera where it was put; a location keeps its roof, and a new copy of the same (rolled again) keeps the cut it had, its roof on or the floor opened where it still has it (`FloorCut`, which keeps the floors, the floor opened and the cut's height).

Its floors are found in the place itself (`FloorProbe`, `FloorFinder`): its copy is made with its colliders kept, rays are cast straight down over it on a grid every half metre (at most forty along its longer side), each going down through every surface it meets rather than only the first of each collider (a cave room's rock is one collider, the top of its rock and its floor both), and where they land on what is flat (about 18 degrees at the steepest, so a roof, whose pieces lean 26 or 45 degrees, or a ramp is none) is counted in bands of a quarter metre, each with the bands either side. Only ground a person can walk to counts (`FloorWalk`): a room is come into by its doorways, onto the first ground under each within a metre and a half (or two rays) of it, however far down, so a doorway high in a wall or over a deep room drops onto its floor; a location from its ground within a metre of its root and from anything under the open sky. From there one stands on ground with 1.7 m over it free, steps onto the next ray's first surface no more than 0.6 m up (or the rise of a stair over the rays' spacing, where they lie farther apart), and drops onto the first ground under where it falls; what lies flat no more than 0.75 m under ground so reached is part of it (ice on a cave's rock). So the tops of pillars, walls, altars, tombs and a cave's rock, beams, and a deck with no way up are no floor, whatever room they hold. What has nothing of the place above it, half a metre or more, is open: a flat roof's top, a cave's rock over its hollow, a tower's open deck, seen with the roof on and no floor to cut to. Only ground one can stand on counts: a spot whose neighbours all four ways landed in the same bands, so a beam, a table, a bed or a single stair step adds nothing.

A band whose such spots hold 2 m² and 1.5% of the place's ground is a floor, those with the most room first; a dungeon's example has a floor at each room's main ground, where it has the most room (2 m² at the least, `FloorFinder.MainFloor`), however little of all the example's ground that is, and at each level of a room's own as big as a room's floor, 60 m² to stand and its share of the room's ground (`FloorFinder.RoomFloors`, `FloorFinder.BigLevel`), as a deep chamber's floor or a hall's gallery is, where a ledge, a tomb's top or a step down a shaft is none, end caps and dividers left out, so a cave whose rooms step down a few metres at a time has a floor at each step, as one walks it; a dungeon room's ground walked to counts with nothing of the room over it, as read alone a room whose ceiling is the room above (each of Mörkhalla's) has nothing of its own there, but not, in the entrance, open near or over its doorway out, nor more than 2 m over its highest doorway, the world's ground round its gate (`FloorFinder.Inside`, `Outdoors`, `AboveDoors`), so a sealed tower's storey by its doorway up and each of Mörkhalla's rooms are floors while the ground round a dungeon's gate is not; a room standing whole on none of them, a sloping corridor with no flat ground, gets a floor at its lowest doorway, where it is walked into (`PlaceView.ReachingEveryRoom`); and one within 2 m of a floor already taken is part of it (a step, a dais).

Each room of an example is a patch of its own, its spots never neighbours of another's; each room's rays are summed up once, band by band, as it comes in (`FloorPatch`), and the example's floors come from those added up, so a dungeon of a hundred rooms finds its floors as quickly as one of ten. The colliders are taken away once read. Each floor is cut at head height (2.5 m) over it, or just under the floor above (0.6 m) or under the ceiling over it (0.1 m), whichever is lowest, but never lower than 1.4 m over it (`PlaceView.CutHeight`): the ceiling is what hides its ground from above, the first thing of the place at least 1.4 m over each of its flat spots within 0.3 m of it (`FloorLanding.Over`), taken low among them (30% of them from the lowest up) so a cut under it opens most of the floor, where at least a quarter of its spots have anything over them at all (`FloorFinder.CeilingOver`); so a top floor under a low roof, a sealed tower's top storey or a low-roofed house's floor opens with its roof taken away rather than left over it. The ruler puts the roof on above half a metre over the top floor's cut, or above the place's top where that is higher, the highest its rays met (`FloorRecord.Highest`, an example's over all its rooms read), whatever of it is drawn at the time.

A location's ground, its root, is a floor too where none was found near it, as bare earth is the world's own; but a location keeps only floors with something of it over them to cut away, 2 m² of a floor's flat spots or more with anything of the place over them, however small a share of the floor that is, as a hut's deck under the half of its roof a roll left (`PlaceView.LocationFloors`, `FloorFinder.Covered`), so the ground round a hut's stilts is none, and a place with nothing over any floor (a ring of standing stones, a runestone, a wreck, a roofless ruin) has no floor at all, and with it no ruler, no cut and no stepping: it is seen whole with its roof on. Of 76 locations whose rays were kept, 37 had nothing over their ground; a house whose floor is bare earth under its roof has no flat spot of its own there for a ceiling to be found over, so it would lose its cut, and none of those 76 was one. A room with nothing found keeps its doorways' heights (`PlaceView.Floors`), and a dungeon's example is read room by room as it is built, each room's floors in a frame of their own after the frame it is made in, as reading a big room takes as long as a frame should (a room the floor opened has put away meanwhile is switched on for its rays, which meet only what is switched on), its floors renewed as rooms come in; while its entrance is shown, each room is woken alone to be read, beside the sleeping example.

What the rays met is kept for each kind of place, so its floors need no copy woken to be found again (`FloorRecord`, `FloorRecords`): the grid laid over every part a roll can bring, in the place's own frame, and each surface a ray met with the part it is on, as probes of its rolls saw them; a part is an object holding a collider that is there or that the place's rolls switch on (`PlaceCopy` notes what they switch in the copy's `PlaceRoll`, which goes with it to the probe). A roll whose parts were all seen tells its floors from the record as its rays would, each ray's flat surfaces among the parts there, open where nothing of them is half a metre over it; a roll bringing a part not yet seen is probed, and what it sees is added, so a kind of room is probed until its rolls have shown every part, usually on its first one or two copies. Where a spawn point's creature stands is kept the same way, each spawn point by the parts it was probed with. The rays pass through every other copy on the stage, each step taking the nearest hit of the copy's own among all it meets (`FloorProbe.OwnDown`), so what is kept of a kind of place does not depend on the rooms that stood beside the copy it was probed on, and a room's floor reaches its doorways however its neighbours' floors overlap them.

They go a centimetre and a half beside the grid's lines (`FloorRecord.Beside`): a room's walls and floors stand on half-metre lines from its edge, and a ray right on an edge meets or misses it by the last digit of where its copy stands, so copies of one kind would meet a wall's top differently. A record goes with the place's stamp (`PlaceStamps`), and one whose copy has other parts than it keeps is passed over. Each kind of place has a file of its own in `BepInEx/cache/isimp.Scry/floors/`, read only once that kind of place is shown, so a profile that has shown hundreds holds only those of this session (`FloorFiles`): the folder is listed on a worker thread on entering a world, a place's file is found on one as its copy starts to be made (a dungeon's rooms' all at once as its example starts), and a record is written on one as it grows. The files are read and written one job after another, so a place shown after a run of others waits behind every file they wanted, and a frame that waited for them would freeze the game for as long. So the stage asks each frame whether a kind's file is found yet (`FloorFiles.Ready`) and goes on meanwhile: a place made is shown once its file is found, and a dungeon's rooms go on being made while each room's floors wait for its kind's file, read in the order the rooms were made. Its floors are then taken from what was found, as before. A copy that does not stand upright is probed on a grid of the stage's own and nothing kept.

Each floor is cut at head height, or just under the floor above where that is lower (0.6 m under it, but never lower than 1.4 m over the floor), so a low storey's ceiling goes whole and what stands on the floor stays (`PlaceView.CutHeights`).

The stage's controls stand in its top right corner as three groups from the right, each on a backing of the stage's colour so they read over any picture, the pieces of a group joined into one with a point's seam between them: *View*; what the place shows, *Inside* and *Creatures*; and the cut control, three icons named by their tips, a house with its roof on (*No cut*) puts the roof on, its roof lifted off (*Cut from above*) takes it off, opening the floor last opened, and inside a dungeon half a house beside the cut (*Side*) cuts from the side, the part lit as the stage is cut now (`CutControl`, `Stage.ChooseCut`, `Skin.PillPiece`); it stands for every place with floors, a dungeon's inside having all three; Page Up and Page Down with the mouse over the stage (over the list they page it) step a floor down, staying on the lowest, or up, and from the top floor put the roof back (`PlaceView.StepCut`). The ruler at the stage's right edge has a tick for each floor and a bar where the cut is: a click on a tick opens that floor, a click or drag elsewhere cuts there, over the floor below it (`PlaceView.LevelAt`), above the top floor puts the roof on, and the wheel over it steps floors; in a room of a dungeon gone into, it is the room's instead (below).

Shift with the wheel over the stage steps the floors as Page Up and Down do, a floor a notch, each opened at its own cut: down with the roof on, the top floor; up from the top floor, the roof back on (`ScryPanel.WheelStep`); in a room gone into, that room's marks. Going into a room, coming back out and putting the roof on or taking it off each frame what is to be seen afresh, at the framing's own distance and on its middle, a zoom or drag on what was framed before let go of and the camera's turn kept (`Stage.FreshView`). From a floor to another, by the ruler, Page Up or Down or the wheel, the view stays on the spot it looked at, at its zoom and as dragged, only the height looked at following the floor (`Stage.KeepLevelFrame`); *Fit* or a double-click frames the floor opened afresh. A ? in the stage's bottom right corner, left of which *Fit*, *Top*, *Side* and *Front* show while the mouse is on the stage, holds its key sheet as its tip, a control a line, for what is shown now: turning, moving and zooming; with floors, stepping them and moving the cut; in a room gone into, its cut, on past its ends, and coming back out (`StageWords.KeySheet`).

The cut is the camera's near plane laid along that height (`Camera.CalculateObliqueMatrix`), so it opens any model whatever its meshes; the far plane, which the oblique projection tilts, is pushed out to keep the backdrop, and from below the cut nothing is cut (`Stage.Cut.cs`).
