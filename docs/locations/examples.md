# Example dungeons

How a dungeon or camp is laid out as an example by the game's rules, its plan, floors and levels, and how it is built on the stage.

Code: `src/Model/LevelCuts.cs`, `src/Preview/StageExample.cs`, `src/Preview/Stage.cs`, `src/Model/ExamplePlan.cs`, `src/Model/PlaceView.cs`, `src/Model/DungeonLayout.cs`, `src/Model/FloorFinder.cs`, `src/Model/FloorCut.cs`.

See also: [plan box](plan-box.md), [this dungeon](this-dungeon.md), [floors](floors.md); all notes in [the index](../index.md).

## Example layouts

A dungeon's rooms and a camp's tents are built when the game makes the zone, so under a dungeon's or camp's details an example layout stands in for them. The rooms of its kinds are loaded one at a time in the background and read, which fills their own entries too (`ExampleLayouts`), and then laid out by the rules `DungeonGenerator` follows for a new one, in Scry's own code and with dice of its own (`DungeonLayout`): a dungeon from a random entrance whose way in is at the generator, a room at a random open doorway for each try, stopping early once its required rooms are in, end caps and dividers on what is left open, and a door rolled for each doorway between rooms (a dungeon's details name the doors in a row for each type of doorway, how many of the doorways that allow one get one, each door listed alike likely at its own chance or the dungeon's, `DungeonWords.DoorRows`); a camp on a grid or scattered in a ring and walled.

A room may not overlap one placed before it, tested a tenth of a metre smaller (`Boxes.Overlap` stands in for `Physics.ComputePenetration`), nor leave its zone's box, which lies round the generator as a new zone puts it (`DungeonLayout.Site`). The ground's tilt and height, which keep camp rooms off steep or low ground, play no part.

Its plan is drawn in the stage's lower left corner, from above, turned with the view so what lies ahead of the camera is up, its box framed on its rooms as they are turned and its scale kept as the view turns, by how far apart its farthest corners are (`ExamplePlan`). Each room is a tile of one tone laid over the stage's colour, so rooms overlapping are no darker, with every outline drawn over all the tiles. With a floor opened, the floor is named over the plan as beside the ruler; only the rooms with a floor of their own there are tiled, as the rays found it, not a tall room from the floor under it whose box reaches up through it, and a room with no floor of its own at any of the floors, a tunnel sloping between them, a passage of rock or an end cap, where its box reaches the floor; the rooms standing on the floor under it are outlined in dashes; and a door where a room of the floor meets one standing on another floor is marked up or down to the nearest such floor (`ExamplePlan.Tile`, `ExamplePlan.Leads`). That plan stays a camp's; a dungeon's inside has the plan box instead.

With a floor opened inside a dungeon's example, the stage stands whole the rooms the plan tiles on that floor and dims the rest (every colour each material's shader takes, its tint, glow and snow colour alike, at 30% through a property block, its see-through kept, `Dim`, `DimColour`; a shader taking no colour, such as a particle's, draws its part as it was; darkening the tint alone left snowy rock as bright, its snow coloured apart), their own lights off unless `LightDimmedRooms` keeps them (*Lights below* in the View box; never on a stage filmed fast), as the stage films by the forward path, drawing a part again for each light on it, and a big dungeon's rooms below its floor opened hold more than a hundred, while those above it are put away (`ExamplePlan.OnStage`, `StageExample.RoomShown`): a room is on a floor where its own rays found a floor of the room's own within a metre of it, its main ground or a level with 6 m² to stand and its share of the room's ground (`FloorFinder.StandsOn`, `StageExample.HasFloorAt`), so a tall rock room from a floor under it reaching up through the floor opened is dimmed with the rooms below; a room with no floor of its own at any of the floors, a tunnel sloping between them, a passage of rock or an end cap, is on a floor where its box, centred on its pivot as the game sizes it, reaches into the two metres over it; a stair's slope, a landing or a chest's lid near a floor only touches it, so the room is neither shown whole on that floor nor counted where the camera centres on it; should no room stand on the floor opened, every room stands, so the stage is never left empty.

Any ground near a floor still reaches the room for the floors themselves: a room no floor reaches, by its box or any ground of its rays (`FloorFinder.Holds`, `StageExample.Reaches`), gets a floor where it is walked into. The floor ruler cuts anywhere the place stands, putting the roof on only above its top (`PlaceView.RoofAbove`), a few milliseconds' worth of rooms changed each frame, those not on the floor passed by under the mouse, and the floor is named beside the ruler's cut, from the top as the ruler's ticks count, with how many rooms stand on it by the same rule, end caps and dividers left out (`PlaceView.FloorLabel`, `StageExample.OnFloorCount`).

The whole of a dungeon's example opens from above by the plan's levels rather than by floors: the ruler has a mark for each level, named by its depth from the entrance (`Stage.ExampleMark`); a camp's example keeps its floors. One cut opens a room only over its floor, by what stands on it, and under its ceiling, and a level of the plan can hold rooms a few metres apart, a low corridor under higher rooms; so each level's rooms are put in groups one cut opens, going down from its highest room, each room joining the group above it while one cut, head height over the group's highest ground and under its lowest ceiling as the rooms' rays found them (else the top of the room's box), still opens them all, a room too low to stand in alone and keeping its roof (`LevelCuts`). A level one cut opens is filmed as any place is; one that needs more is filmed a group at a time from its lowest up, each film with the near plane at its group's cut and the other groups' renderers left out (`Renderer.forceRenderingOff`), the rooms of the levels below going with the lowest, each painted over the one before as the creatures above the cut are (`Stage.FilmGroups`): the whole level opens in one click, at the cost of a film per group, and where a lower room's wall should hide part of a higher room's floor the higher floor is painted over it; the creatures are filmed with every group.

Every notch of the wheel that moves the cut steps it a level, as Page Up and Down do: Shift and the wheel on the stage, the wheel on the ruler, on the plan box or on the room's card, however the wheel tells its turns (a mouse's notch is one step, a touchpad's small turns add up to one, never more than one step a turn, `WheelNotches`); each level is opened at its own cut whichever way it is stepped to, down from the roof the top level, down from the lowest level no lower, up from the top level the roof on (`FloorCut.Step`). Only a drag or a click on the ruler cuts between, exactly where it is set, opening the level it is over (`FloorCut.CutTo`). A drag on the ruler moves such a level's cut by hand as any other: the film of its first group is cut where the cut stands, every other moved as much, no higher than under its own lowest ceiling where its cut is not already over it, nor lower than half a metre over its highest floor (`LevelCuts.Group.CutBy`). A tall room of a level below, a shaft or a high cave, rises into the levels over it: where one could stand in it over a group's lowest floor, its ceiling a head height over it, it stands whole with its level, cut with it (in the lowest film), rather than dimmed among its rooms (`LevelCuts.Reaches`, `LevelCuts.StandsWith`).

A level is framed on the whole of it, its own rooms and those rising into it, looking at its highest ground (`LevelCuts.TopOf`). A room of a level above is put away only where it would hide the level: standing over a room of it, by its box seen from above, or with none of its own floors half a metre under the cut, of which only what is under them would show; else it stands dimmed, filmed with the level's first group (`RoomFocus.HidesFromAbove`). A room with a floor of its own on another level, a hall with a pit or a gallery over a chamber, stands whole on that level too, as the cut there meets that floor, filmed with its first group (`LevelCuts.OnLevelByFloor`). Every room put away, by a level or by a room gone into, stands as a ghost: its footprint at its floor in faint thin lines, one mesh for them all drawn with the outlines past the cut and laid again only as the rooms put away change, so a room taken off the stage never just vanishes (`RoomOutline.Ghost`, `Stage.KeepGhosts`). The plan from above leaves out only what lies below the lowest room of the level opened (`Stage.ExampleOpenMarkLow`).

A fold in its corner puts it away, leaving a small *Plan* tab where it stood that brings it back; *Another example*, beside the stage's other buttons, lays out a new one, and the line under them says what the example holds, or how far reading its rooms has got. The plan stands over the stage, which does not scroll, as its rooms are drawn turned (`GUIUtility.RotateAroundPivot`) and a turned drawing is not clipped to a scrolling view. A room under the mouse is lit on the plan whether the mouse is on the plan or on the stage, and a click on it goes to its entry. It is never a world's real layout, but for the dungeon the player is in or at (below), and no location ever shows where it is in a world.

The example is also built on the stage from copies of its rooms, a few milliseconds' worth a frame in the order they were placed, the entrance first (`Stage.StepExample`), each rolled like any place's copy. It stands from its generator (`DungeonExample.FromGenerator`): a camp's rooms around the location's own parts, where its generator is in it; a dungeon's rooms in place of its entrance, since the game builds them far above it, so the stage's *Inside* and *Outside* switch between the example and the entrance; an example taken down switches the entrance's own parts back on (`StageExample.Forget`), so one laid out again over the same location, as the fog is when a peek ends or rooms are walked, still has its entrance to show, and a dungeon of the world no room of which is shown shows it. Its page tells what its rooms hold, as its own prefab holds little: every part across its kinds of room, in the rows a location's page has, the loot those give right after its chests (`Knowledge.LootOf`: an item lying there, a pickable's item, what any drop table holds, such as a chest's filling), and the creatures their spawn points place before its building pieces, each with in how many of its kinds of room it is (`PlaceParts.Across`, `PlaceParts.LootAcross`). Its rooms name the same chests and pickups again and again, so what each kind of part gives and which row it goes in are read once a telling, not once a line; the rooms are read as its example is laid out, or with every location.

Inside, it stands on its lowest floor and opens on its top floor like a room, its floors being where its rooms' doorways are (`PlaceView.ExampleFloors`). The bundles of the kinds of room it uses are held while it is shown, since a copy needs its bundle, and let go of when the location is left or the panel closes; the kinds it does not use are let go of a frame after the stage has let go of their copies. A room under the mouse is named, found by the ray from the camera through the room boxes below the cut (`DungeonExample.Pick`), and a click that is not a drag goes to its entry.
