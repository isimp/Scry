# This dungeon and its walks

The dungeon the player is in, shown as saved, the walk tracker, the fog by rooms walked, and the maps kept per character.

## This dungeon

Where the player is in or at a dungeon (`Location.GetLocation`, as the compendium has it), its own rooms are shown in place of an example once its generator is loaded, which is only while its zone is (`WorldDungeons`). The generator is found standing in the location's zone, made from its own network object, and its rooms read from what it saved there (`DungeonGenerator.Save`; `DungeonGenerator.Load` reads them back the same way): a count, then each room's name's hash, its place and its turn as angles, or the older keys a room each where a world still has them (`SavedRooms`). Each room stands by its hash among the rooms of the dungeon's kinds read for its example, turned as saved, the generator's place and turn the site's; the way in is the first entrance, as the game saves its start room first, and an end cap or divider is joined to the room whose doorway it closes, by the doorway they share (`RealDungeon`). A room of a kind not read is left out and counted on the line under the stage's buttons (`DungeonWords.WorldsNote`). It is looked for once a second while the dungeon's page is shown, and read once, as a dungeon's rooms never change once laid out; the stage, the plan box, the levels, the rooms and their card work as for an example, the creatures and chests in its rooms this copy's own rolls, not the world's.

A tag at the head of the breadcrumb, *In your world*, says it is this dungeon (`PlanBoxWords.ThisDungeon`), drawn as the kind badge is, and an example is tagged *Example* in the same place (`PlanBoxWords.Example`), so the stage always says which it shows; an example's note names what it is one way of ("An example: one way Burial Chambers can come out. Each one in your world lays out its own.", `DungeonWords.ExampleNote`), and this dungeon's says how far it was walked, or that none of its rooms was and peeking shows them (`DungeonWords.WalkedInYourWorld`); the menu beside the dungeon then holds *Show an example*, which lays out an example in its place, and from an example *Show it in your world* brings it back while it is at hand (`DungeonTrail.MenuRows`). *Another example* lays out an example too. Nothing is spawned, the game's generator is never run, and nothing is sent. This dungeon is shown by its layout alone (`PlaceRoll.LayoutOnly`): what lives and lies in it is the world's, which Scry does not read, so its rooms stand with no creatures, chests or pickups, the room card and the peek list none, and the stage offers no creatures; each room's chance parts still pick one look, as a room shows only one.

Inside a dungeon, whether the panel is open or not, Scry looks twice a second for the room the player stands in (`DungeonWalks`): the one whose box holds them, each kind of room as big as its rooms standing under the generator are (the game makes each room under it, `DungeonGenerator.PlaceRoom`), turned as saved, end caps and dividers no rooms; of boxes overlapping, the one whose foot is nearest under them (`SavedRooms.Holding`). The dungeon is kept on first being in it, by the world and where its location stands, to the metre (`DungeonMaps.PlaceIdOf`), which the world's making fixed for good: the game numbers every object afresh each time it loads the world, in the order its file is read (`ZDO.Load` sets each id from a running count), so a generator's network id names another object after a restart. Maps kept under such an id before are re-keyed by place where maps come in, the character's file, the server's records and its answers, those of one dungeon kept under several numbers merged (`DungeonMaps.ByPlace`), and written again. It keeps its rooms as saved and where its location stands, and each room stood in is walked, by its place in the saved rooms (`DungeonMaps`). Outside an interior it only asks whether the player is in one.

With the compendium off, every room of this dungeon shows. With it on, only the rooms walked show, an end cap or divider as the room it is joined to, and each doorway of theirs leading on to a room not walked is an open way, an accent arrow out of the doorway in each of the plan box's views (`DungeonFog`, `ScryPanel.PlanBoxYou`); the rest is not drawn, named or counted, and the plan's bottom left corner and the line under the stage's buttons say how many rooms are walked of all (`PlanBoxWords.Walked`). Peeking shows every room, those not walked dashed and faint on the plan and dimmed on the stage, but the one gone into, under the warning "Peeking: 12 rooms not walked shown", the rest on its hover (`PlanBoxWords.PeekingRooms`). The stage is given only the rooms the fog shows, and follows the fog laid again in place (`DungeonFog.Relay`, `StageExample.Follow`): the rooms that join are made after those standing, the rooms that leave are taken down, the rest stand as they were, and nothing moves where nothing changed; out of a room taken down the view comes as *Whole dungeon* does. The fog is laid again at once for a peek or the compendium changed, and for rooms walked while it is shown (*WalkWhileOpen*) at most every five seconds.

Where the player stands in this dungeon, the plan box outlines their room in the accent and draws their arrow where they stand, facing as they face, over their room's node in the tree; on the stage an accent arrow lies on the floor under them with a thin post over it, one mesh drawn with the outlines past the cut, so the cut never hides it (`Stage.KeepYouMark`). With no room gone into, the breadcrumb ends at their room, "Start corridor · you", a click on it going into it; gone into their room, its part is marked so.

The maps are the character's whose book the compendium holds, kept in `BepInEx/cache/isimp.Scry/maps/` in a file named as the book's, read and written on a worker thread as the book is, at most every ten seconds and as the game quits, a file that could not be read set aside as the book is and new maps started (`DungeonWalks`, `KeptFile`). A line a dungeon: its id, its location, where the location and the generator stand, the generator's turn, the rooms walked, and its rooms as the world saves them, in base64; a line that cannot be read is passed over (`DungeonMaps`). The game's days it was first and last walked in (`DungeonMap.FirstDay`, `LastDay`, -1 where not known), stamped from the game's day count as it is first kept and as each day in it begins (`DungeonMaps.Keep`, `Visit`, `EnvMan.GetDay`), go on a line of their own after its own, `days`, its id, the first and last day, so an older Scry reads the dungeon's line as before and passes the days over; a walk kept before days were knows its last day once walked again and never a first one. Under a dungeon location's stage buttons, below the heading "Walked in this world (9)", each dungeon of it walked in the world played has one line, the character's walk and the server's record of it together (`WalkLines`): where it lies from where the player stands, "340 m north-east" (to ten metres under a kilometre, "1.2 km", whole kilometres from ten, "Where you stand" within thirty metres), then "you 4 of 18 rooms" and "on this server 6 of 18 rooms, 2 from traces"; the days each walked it and what was left inside on its hover (`DungeonMapWords.Line`, `LineTip`).

The server's records carry no place of the world, so a dungeon's place comes from the character's walk of it or from the ground the compendium's sources hold it in (`DungeonsInGround`); one with neither says "One you have not found", its place kept hidden. Those found stand nearest first, rebuilt as the player moves ten metres, and those not found after them in the order kept, so their order tells nothing of where they lie; past four the rest fold behind one chip. A dot before each tells a walk of yours, one on this server alone, or one not found; one walked whole, the map's ticked mark. A walk of yours counts the server's record of it whatever the compendium shows, the server asked for it as its page shows, "walked whole on this server · you 5 of 9 rooms" where you and others walked every room between you (`WalkLine.Together`, `DungeonMaps.WalkedTogether`); the server's other walks show where the compendium opens them. Each has *On the map* where found, which puts the panel away and opens Valheim's large map there (`Minimap.ShowPointOnMap`), *Show it*, "Shown" while the stage shows it, which shows it on the stage as far as it was walked wherever the player is (the dungeon at hand comes first), and *Forget* for a walk of yours, after which an example stands in its place (`ScryPanel.MapLines`). `scry forget maps` forgets the character's maps of the world played. A map is known again only in the same world, and the same character on another computer starts afresh, as its book does.
