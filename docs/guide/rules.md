# The compendium's rules

The compendium shows only what the character you play has come to know, and grows as you play. It is chosen for each character in the *Compendium* box at the top of the panel, and a character whose compendium is off is asked once whether to keep one. The *?* in that box tells the same as this page, in the game.

![The compendium's guide, opened by the ? in its box](../images/compendium-guide.webp)

## Off, Loose and Strict

*Off* shows everything the game holds, as a lookup.

*Loose* shows a thing you know with its whole page, but names only what your character has met: a creature once fought, an item once held or made, a place once stepped into. What the game's own menus show comes with the thing, once the game lists it for your character (a recipe once its station and every material are known; until then its row waits, under either rules): a recipe's materials and station, a piece's cost and tool, what a known thing is made into, a station's recipes, a store's wares, as the game shows them the moment the thing is known. The rest of a page is left out, its loot and where it lives, a dungeon room's card and a raid's card saying how many more are not yet met, and a name never met reads as a dash in a page's text. What you have only seen stands on the stage with its page closed, telling what sight gave, the stars and gear it was seen with up close and where it was seen, and how to come to know it; its row in the list is faint, noted seen.

*Strict* also keeps back what the game never shows of how things connect until your character saw them go together, each the way the row tells it: a creature's drops once seen to fall from it, not merely seen beside it; where it met a creature; what a chest held. Numbers the game never shows wait until you found them out: an attack's damage until it hit you, a creature's health until you killed one, a tree's health until you broke one, what one star changes until one with one star was seen to fall. Each says what shows it, and a page's title row counts how many of those numbers were found out, each counted cell and each line of where an item comes from one, "7 of 12 found out", its hover listing what is left; where *Known up to* shows a page whole, the row says so. Drops show what your character saw fall and how often, not the game's chances, the row's title counting the kills whose loot it saw fall and its hover setting them against the game's own count of your kills; a trophy's chance by stars as your own count at those stars. The stage shows a creature only with the stars it was seen with up close and only carrying what it was seen carrying, as the game names a creature and marks its stars within 10 m; a place's example and a raid stand their creatures no higher. A creature's attack begun within those 10 m, in view, counts as seen: its damage, and its animations, which *Strict* keeps back with it, show from then on, as after taking the hit. What *Strict* keeps back shows as a soft smudge where the value would stand, its hover saying what frees it; an attack's animations wait as one chip counting them. An item, piece or resource is seen within those 10 m too, as the game names it only up close. Your own hits tell how a type lands where you saw its popup, on whichever game the creature runs; another player's hit tells nothing, as the game shows you only a number. Riding a creature shows its health, as the game prints it for the one ridden. The *You* box also lists the star levels and gear the stage still lacks of a creature, counted in its chip. What a starred creature's fall shows of its stars waits until you saw those stars up close, at the death or before; a kill credited from afar counts the fall and its loot alone. A creature is seen within those 10 m too, as the game names it there, and the stars a first sighting tells are those seen up close. A hit's popup shows how a type lands within 30 m, your own hits included. A chest you or a friend filled pairs nothing with its place. The search finds a known thing only by what its page shows, and `is:left` finds the pages whose title chip still counts something to find out, as the chip counts it. Peeking shows a page whole and hides nothing your book knows: the list's rows, *Learned*'s days and the *You* box's own lines stay.

## Known up to, peeking and Learned

*Known up to* shows everything of the biomes whose bosses your character has beaten as known, tier by tier, marked with stairs wherever it shows something, and can be lowered again.

The eye beside the compendium's button peeks at the page shown: it shows whole until you go to another, a step back or forward to a page you left peeked at peeking at it again until the peek is stopped, and its notice's *Every page* shows every page whole until the panel closes.

*Learned* lists what your character learned, newest first, day by day, under *Strict* also the numbers it found out, which *Loose* shows from the start (in the search, `is:new` finds what it learned today, `is:seen` what it has only seen, `is:walked` the dungeons it walked, and under *Strict* `is:left` the pages whose title chip still counts something to find out), and a page's *You* box tells when it first saw the thing and came to know it, in what weather and with what in hand, and how its kills went. A row of *Learned* opens its page at what changed, the line telling it lit a moment.

## What a world shows as known

The compendium's box also chooses what the world itself may show as known, source by source. *Ground you explored* and *Places marked for you* (a Vegvisir or a rune stone) start at *Seen*; *Shared maps* (a cartography table), *Places on every map* (traders, the start, Hildir's places) and *The whole world* start off. Each can be raised to *Known*, or *With insides* to show the dungeons in it with whatever anyone on the server walked inside them; *The whole world* is *Counted*, making every kind of place the world holds heard of. What shows so is marked with a folded map, and your book keeps only what you met yourself, so turning a source down takes it all back. Your map reaches 100 m around you whatever you saw, so *Ground you explored* can also be turned off, leaving only the places and biomes you saw, and so can *Places marked for you*, which you never looked at either. The biomes under your explored ground show seen the same way, never more whatever the source is raised to, and go with the source.

![The compendium's box, each source of the world at the level chosen](../images/compendium-box.webp)

The pin at the end of *Ground you explored* and *Shared maps* marks every dungeon entrance lying in that ground on Valheim's large map: solid where you walked it, ticked where every room of it was walked, by you alone or by you and others on the server together, hollow where only others did, ticked in outline once they walked it all, plain for an entrance alone. A mark you walked and its page's line count the rooms others on the server walked whatever the compendium shows: "Walked whole on this server: all 9 rooms, 5 of them by you". A click on a mark shows that dungeon in Scry. *Marks on the map*, under the pins, turns every mark of Scry's on the map on or off.

## Playing together

What you learn depends on where you stand and what you did, never on whose game runs the thing. Valheim runs each creature and each thing on one player's game, and most of what happens to it, a death, a pick, a birth, only there. Your own hits count from your own game, and so does a kill you had a hand in, which the game itself credits to every player who hit the creature. Where your friends have Scry, what their game runs near you, a kill or a drop or a pick, counts for you as it would alone, if you did it or saw it, within 30 m with nothing solid between, as a wall or a hill hides a place. Scry tells worlds apart by name for the moments it keeps and by the game's id for its maps and chests, so a world rebuilt under an old name shares the one and not the other. Where a friend's game has no Scry, a notice names them, and you still see a creature's death by the remains it leaves, with its stars and what fell, which count as your own kill where the game credits you with it within seconds, and your own pick by what the thing gives; but a tree or a rock broken there, a birth, a growing up, a station's work and what a creature ate are told by the game that runs them alone, and go unlearned. Another player's hit teaches you nothing of how a creature takes a damage type, on any game, as the game shows you only a number.

A server can set the rules for everyone and cap each source ([server settings](server-settings.md)); the box then shows the server's choice locked, your own kept beneath it.

A few words the panel uses: *seen* is in view once, *met* is seen or known, *known* opens a page; a name *left out* or a number *kept back* waits for you, and the row's hover says for what; *found out* is a number you earned; *counted* is a drop or a yield tallied as you saw it, and *Counted* on a source is every kind of place heard of; *up close* is within the seen reach, 10 m unless the server sets it; *wait* is said of attacks, clips and recipe rows not yet yours; *near* is a station the game listed because you stood by it; *seen tamed* and *petted* are tamings you watched or a tame creature you touched; *World not kept* and *Earlier* head what was learned before worlds or days were kept; your *book* is what Scry keeps of your character.
