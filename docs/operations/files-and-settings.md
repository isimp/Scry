# Files, settings, logging and the monitor

Where Scry keeps its files, its settings, what it writes to the log, and the resource monitor.

Code: `src/Core/Settings.cs`, `src/UI/ScryPanel.Settings.cs`, `src/Model/SettingsWords.cs`, `src/Core/Log.cs`, `src/Core/Faults.cs`, `src/Model/AllocationMeter.cs`, `src/Monitor.cs`, `src/UI/ScryPanel.Monitor.cs`, `src/Model/MonitorWindow.cs`, `src/Core/Kept.cs`.

## Files and settings

The settings are in `BepInEx/config/isimp.Scry.cfg`: the key that opens the panel (`OpenKey`), its scale, whether the search has the keyboard on opening, walking and looking while open, how long before a tip shows, how many entries *Recent* keeps, how long after entering a world the catalog is read, whether the locations are read by themselves, the model's spinning and its speed, whether a sound plays when selected, whether the rooms dimmed below a dungeon's opened floor keep their own lights, how the stage is filmed (`StageQuality`), whether the notice tells what was learned (`NewsNotice`), whether the big map shows Scry's marks and whether their names show always (`DungeonMarkNamesAlways`), their size (`DungeonMarkSize`), which show (`DungeonMarksWalkedByYou`, `DungeonMarksWalkedOnServer`, `DungeonMarkEntrances`) and whether the minimap shows those you walked (`DungeonMarksOnMinimap`), and the diagnostics: what the log tells and whether the resource monitor shows. Most are set in the panel too, each written to the config file as it is chosen, so the file and a mod manager's editor stay the one place they are kept: the gear in the title row opens a card in the list's place with the panel's size (a slider taking the size once let go, as the panel changing size under it would move it from the mouse), the key opening it (its name sunken as a value, and a button waiting for the next key, Escape keeping the one there is, `Settings.OpensPanel`), walking and looking while open, the search on opening, sounds, the wait before a tip, the stage's quality, the news and the dungeon marks' names, its labels in a column as wide as the widest of them so each row's choices stand on one line beside it (`ScryPanel.SettingsCard`, `SettingsWords`, `LabelColumn`); the spin, its speed and the lights below a dungeon's cut are in the View box, reading the locations in every world beside *Find where things are* above the Locations tab's list, and the big map's marks in the compendium's box under the grounds' pins. Favourites and the panel's state (its place in both views, the stage's look, the list's width and which sections are folded) are kept in `BepInEx/config/isimp.Scry/`, so they go with the profile. What the locations hold, once read, where places' floors are, once found, and what each character knows for the compendium are kept in `BepInEx/cache/isimp.Scry/`, out of a profile's sync and export. Favourites of prefabs not in the game right now are kept.

## Logging

The log is written one way, by kind of line: a failure through `Faults`, told once and counted for the self-test; a line Scry always writes through `Log.Report`; something about the game or the PC a player should know, such as a key already taken or no free layer for the stage, through `Log.Warn`; and what a preview did through `Log.Note`, written only with `LogPreviews` on. A standards test holds every write to the log to these. With `LogPreviews` off (the default), Scry writes only the check's line, how long reading the catalog and the locations took, what was asked for (`/scry dump`, the self-test), warnings about the game or the PC, and anything that failed. With it on, the log also tells what each effect list and clip played and whether each part was seen or heard, what each creature's animator was seen to do and its gear, what destroyed things leave and where fallen things came to rest, any frame in which Scry's own work took 20 ms or more (split by part, with how much memory each part allocated, counted for Scry's thread alone where the runtime keeps that count, else told roughly by the memory in use, in steps of 4 KB, which counts other mods' threads too (`AllocationMeter`; the game's runtime has the count's method but reads nought from it on every thread, so in the game it is the memory in use; the self-test's idle part waits as long again each frame doing nothing to tell other threads' share, `OtherThreads`), and where a memory cleanup of the runtime ran), and every half minute how many cleanups ran and how much of the allocation was Scry's. `/scry dump` writes every renderer of the stage copy to the log, for finding out why part of a model does not show.

## Resource monitor

`ResourceMonitor` under Diagnostics, or `/scry monitor`, shows a small box at the screen's right edge, over everything and also with the panel closed (`Monitor`, `ScryPanel.Monitor.cs`). It tells Scry's own time each frame now, on average and at the most over the last ten seconds, and its share of the frames, with a graph of those ten seconds, a column for each sixth of a second at its most (`MonitorWindow`); its outer parts (update, panel, render, its own drawing as monitor) and its largest inner parts, each smoothed over a couple of seconds; what its work allocates a second and the memory cleanups that ran in it, beside all the cleanups in the last ten seconds; and what it holds, counted once a second: its catalog's entries, the bundles it has loaded, the parts drawn on its stage, its creatures and grass, whether previews stand in the world, and the textures, render textures and meshes it made and keeps, noted as they are made so they are counted without searching all of Unity's objects (`Kept`); what it keeps in the cache folder for the next session, the places read and the kinds of place whose floors are kept, and whether those files are being read or written; with the game's managed and native memory for scale. Its figures are made again four times a second. Scry measures its own work while the monitor shows, as with `LogPreviews` on, so a frame costs a little more then. For finding faults, not for play.
