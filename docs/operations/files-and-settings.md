Where Scry keeps its files, its settings, what it writes to the log, and the resource monitor.

## Files and settings

The settings are in `BepInEx/config/isimp.Scry.cfg`: the key that opens the panel (`OpenKey`), its scale, whether the search has the keyboard on opening (off by default, so the keys walk), walking and looking while open, how long before a tip shows, how many entries *Recent* keeps, how long after entering a world the catalog is read, whether the locations are read by themselves, the model's spinning and its speed, whether a sound plays when selected, whether the rooms dimmed below a dungeon's opened floor keep their own lights, how the stage is filmed (`StageQuality`) and from how long its still film takes it is filmed lighter while its camera moves (`LighterFilmFromMs`), what the notice tells of what was learned (`News`), whether the dungeon marks' names show always (`DungeonMarkNamesAlways`), their size (`DungeonMarkSize`: 0.5, 0.7, 1 or 1.4 of a pin, a size kept from before taken to the nearest), the dots' size (`ShowOnMapDotSize`: XSmall, Small, Normal or Large), and whether the minimap shows what is kept on the map (`DungeonMarksOnMinimap`) and at what size, apart from the map's (`MinimapDotSize`, the same four, starting as the map's dot size), whether what the world's server tells of places never come near is shown (`ShowOnMapSquares`, *Told by the server*), how loud a sound plays when selected (`PreviewLoudness`), whether what the character saw is remembered for the map (`RecordSeenSpots`); a dungeon is marked where walked or its entrance seen, what stands on the map is kept with each character (`CompendiumBook.Kept`), and the older settings choosing which marks show (`DungeonMarksWalkedByYou`, `DungeonMarksWalkedOnServer`, `DungeonMarkEntrances`, `DungeonMarksOnMap`) are taken out of the file (`Settings.Retire`), `DungeonMarksOnMap` once the first character read has kept the dungeons off where it was off, and the diagnostics: what the log tells and whether the resource monitor shows. Most are set in the panel too, each written to the config file as it is chosen, so the file and a mod manager's editor stay the one place they are kept: the gear in the title row opens a card in the list's place that shows every setting, in sections (*Panel*, *Preview*, *Compendium*, *Map*, *Background*, *This server* and *For checking Scry*), each row with its explanation under it (`ScryPanel.SettingsCard`, `SettingsCatalog`, `SettingsWords`). *Compendium* holds the rules, *Known up to* and what each source shows, as the compendium's box sets them, with what the world's server allows of each under it; *This server* shows what the server decides, locked while it decides it. The key opening the panel shows its name sunken as a value, with a button that waits for the next key, Escape keeping the one there is (`Settings.OpensPanel`), and the panel's size is a slider that takes the size once let go, as the panel changing size under it would move it from the mouse. A first start tells the key once and offers a short setup of the key, the compendium's rules for the character (or the server's, where it sets them), walking and looking while open, and remembering what you see (`SettingsCatalog.Setup`, `SetupCard`); *Run the setup again* on the card brings it back. The spin, its speed and the lights below a dungeon's cut are also in the View box, and reading the locations in every world also beside *Find where things are* above the Locations tab's list. Favourites and the panel's state (its place in both views, the stage's look, the list's width and which sections are folded) are kept in `BepInEx/config/isimp.Scry/`, so they go with the profile. What the locations hold, once read, where places' floors are, once found, what each animator was seen to play (clips.dat), and what each character knows for the compendium are kept in `BepInEx/cache/isimp.Scry/`, out of a profile's sync and export. Favourites of prefabs not in the game right now are kept.

## Logging

The log is written one way, by kind of line: a failure through `Faults`, told once and counted for the self-test; a line Scry always writes through `Log.Report`; something about the game or the PC a player should know, such as a key already taken or no free layer for the stage, through `Log.Warn`; and what a preview did through `Log.Note`, written only with `LogPreviews` on. A standards test holds every write to the log to these. With `LogPreviews` off (the default), Scry writes only the check's line, how long reading the catalog and the locations took, what was asked for (`/scry dump`, the self-test), warnings about the game or the PC, and anything that failed. With it on, the log also tells what each effect list and clip played and whether each part was seen or heard, what each creature's animator was seen to do and its gear, what destroyed things leave and where fallen things came to rest, any frame in which Scry's own work took 20 ms or more (split by part, with how much memory each part allocated, counted for Scry's thread alone where the runtime keeps that count, else told roughly by the memory in use, in steps of 4 KB, which counts other mods' threads too (`AllocationMeter`; the game's runtime has the count's method but reads nought from it on every thread, so in the game it is the memory in use; the self-test's idle part waits as long again each frame doing nothing to tell other threads' share, `OtherThreads`), and where a memory cleanup of the runtime ran), and every half minute how many cleanups ran, how much of the allocation was Scry's, and what Scry's work took a frame on average and at most, by part, the parts inside others told apart (`PartWindow`). Once the catalog or the locations are read, it names the pieces of the reading that took over 8 ms in one go: a catalog piece by the prefab it read, a location's step by the place and what it read there (`LongPieces`; the steps of `PlaceReader.Steps` are timed under those names too). `/scry dump` writes every renderer of the stage copy to the log, for finding out why part of a model does not show.

## Resource monitor

`ResourceMonitor` under Diagnostics, or `/scry monitor`, shows a small box at the screen's right edge, over everything and also with the panel closed (`Monitor`, `ScryPanel.Monitor.cs`). It tells Scry's own time each frame now, on average and at the most over the last ten seconds, and its share of the frames, with a graph of those ten seconds, a column for each sixth of a second at its most (`MonitorWindow`); its outer parts (update, panel, render, its own drawing as monitor) and its largest inner parts, each smoothed over a couple of seconds; what its work allocates a second and the memory cleanups that ran in it, beside all the cleanups in the last ten seconds; and what it holds, counted once a second: its catalog's entries, the bundles it has loaded, the parts drawn on its stage, its creatures and grass, whether previews stand in the world, and the textures, render textures and meshes it made and keeps, noted as they are made so they are counted without searching all of Unity's objects (`Kept`); what it keeps in the cache folder for the next session, the places read and the kinds of place whose floors are kept, and whether those files are being read or written; with the game's managed and native memory for scale. Its figures are made again four times a second. Scry measures its own work while the monitor shows, as with `LogPreviews` on, so a frame costs a little more then. For finding faults, not for play.
