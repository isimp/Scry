# The self-test

The in-game self-test: how runs are started and chosen, what each area of parts covers, its pictures, and how to read its log.

Code: `src/Preview/Stage.cs`, `src/Core/Timing.cs`, `src/Core/Faults.cs`, `src/Core/SelfTestHost.cs`, `src/UI/ScryPanel.Drawn.cs`, `src/Model/FrameTimes.cs`, `src/Model/FrameStats.cs`, `src/Model/Explorer.cs`.

## Self-test

The self-test is a tool for finding faults, a plugin of its own beside Scry (`Scry.SelfTest.dll`, from `selftest/`) that a development build deploys and a release never packages, so a player's Scry holds nothing of it. Everything of it lives there: its runner and words (`selftest/Model`, tested with the model), the `/scry` words it answers (`SelfTestCommand`) and how it is drawn in the panel (`SelfTestPanel`, with the panel's own strips and cards). Scry holds only the hook it plugs into as it loads (`SelfTestHost`): a tick each frame, the words typed after `/scry`, and room for its strip under the header and its details in the list's place; it reaches Scry's insides through `InternalsVisibleTo`. Installing it is what turns it on, so Scry has no setting for it, and without it `/scry selftest` is a search like any other text (`SelfTestContainmentTests` holds Scry to this). `/scry selftest` tries Scry out in the world it is in and writes what it found to `BepInEx/Scry-selftest.log`, appended so earlier runs stay; with the file `BepInEx/Scry-selftest.run` present it starts by itself a few seconds after the catalog is read, once per world, and `/scry selftest stop` stops it. It uses the panel as a player would, through the same paths, in some hundred parts, run in the order `SelfTest.Scenarios` lists them and written in files named for what they test (`SelfTest.Search`, `SelfTest.Panel`, `SelfTest.Stage`, `SelfTest.Details`, `SelfTest.Mods`, `SelfTest.Previews`, `SelfTest.Raids`, `SelfTest.Locations`, `SelfTest.Layouts`, `SelfTest.Sweeps`).

`/scry selftest` runs a quick set: every part but the two that read every location again (*reading every location* and *Look again*, minutes each, `Scenario.Full`), which rely instead on what is kept, and the parts that go through many places go through a few of them (`SelfTestSample`): the dungeon parts the Frost Caves alone of the five reference dungeons, the climbing rooms a slope and a shaft, the music eight places spread over the list. `/scry selftest deep` runs the same parts through every place; `/scry selftest full` runs every part; `/scry selftest failed` runs again the parts that failed last; any other words run the parts whose names hold them all, in any case, a full-only part too (`SelfTestCommand`, `Scenario.ForRun`); and `/scry selftest list` writes every part's name to the log with how long it took last. `/scry selftest help` (or `?`) says in the console what each run takes: how many parts a quick, deep, full and failed run would run and about how long by their last times, the parts that failed last by name, and a few words that run some parts, with how many each catches (`SelfTestWords.Help`). Each part's last time is kept in `BepInEx/Scry-selftest.times` and the parts that failed in `BepInEx/Scry-selftest.failed` beside the log (`SelfTestTimes`), so a run says on starting about how long it takes; a part that ran this time takes its new time, and the failed list keeps those failed before and not run, with those that failed now. The marker file runs by the same words, a quick set where it says nothing. The header of each run in the log says which it was.

The first parts check the startup check, the catalog and which locations and raids count as the game's own, and the catalog's soundness (every key once, every entry named and in a group, the tabs' counts adding up to all). They search by name, kind and biome and with each kind of search term, time the slowest search against 50 ms, keep favourites, *Recent* and the history, list every tab group by group, move with the arrow keys, filter by *Game* and *Mods*, see a tab with nothing for the search show every kind's matches, open the search's help, the settings card (a stage filmed fast at half the size, the View box's speed stepping), the compendium's guide and the question of keeping a compendium, go round a page by the line of its sections, draw both views and search from `/scry`, and see the panel's own strip name the part running.

Then one entry of each kind goes on the stage and has its details read, and the details of creatures (weak spots, when they turn on you, how far they chase, what stars add), a piece (support and wear), items (what they are used for and where they are made), a workbench (what is made at it and built near it), a smelter, a bed, build tools and their pieces leading to each other (every tool, mods' too), the mods hooking into drops, loot and spawning with every hook looked at, the mod report written out whole and every mod station's pieces and recipes as the game names them checked against what Scry links, the report opened in the panel, the hooks watching deaths' loot in place and what was seen so far told on its creature and items, every mod loaded having a page that tells what it adds, an item a mod's settings are named after showing them, what equipping was seen to change told on its item, an item's tooltip read without the mods' hooks and one whose mods add lines shown with them marked, a spawner and its creatures, and the odds items tell. The stage's lighting, backdrops and views are drawn, a bigger size makes a bigger copy, a creature stands with stars and in each of its looks, a slower animation slows its animator, a piece stands in every state of wear, the loudness follows its slider, and a new selection starts every adjustment afresh. A spread of every kind stands on the stage; selecting a different entry every frame leaves the last one shown, and after many others the stage holds no more than before (`Stage.Held`).

The skills' parts see every skill of the character's list with a page telling what it changes at the character's level, what raises it and what a death takes, the *You* box opening with the level, and the character holding no more skills after every page is read than before; an iron sword's damage at the character's Swords as the game's own range for its tooltip where Swords was trained, every line of its attacks with its faint line, drawn, an axe telling its hits on trees, a creature how near it sees you sneaking, the workbench its making time, a crop its bonus when picked and the hammer its cost; with pictures of Swords' and Run's pages, the sword's attacks and the creature's senses.

The previews come next: an attack, a clip played, paused, sought, looped and stopped, a sound played, stopped, paused and sought and in the variant chosen, an effect looped until stopped, an effect and a status effect on you, an item worn by the person and taken off, a tree felled whose debris goes again (`Stage.PlayedCount`), a copy standing in the world and a projectile fired, and *Clear world* taking them away. Closing the panel quiets it and opening it again brings the selection back. It follows every raid to what it brings and a raid to its creature and back by the creature's own line, sees the bosses' fights grouped apart from the raids in the order the bosses are fought and linked to their bosses both ways, stands a raid on the stage as the first roll of its creatures with every creature rolled there, rolls it again eight times, and stands every raid that brings creatures.

With the locations it loads one, sees the stage frame its entrance on its own ground, and rolls it again; shows a spread of places and a dungeon twice, the second time with each floor told from what was kept checked against rays cast over the same roll, and a dungeon's next layout told from what was kept, its files written; reads a runestone location's texts and sees them drawn, switches between locations while they load and closes the panel on one, reads a dungeon room and sees it stand on its lowest doorway, cut open over its top floor, stepping down a floor at a time to the lowest and up to the roof, the roof button taking the roof off again, the cut moving by hand, starting afresh when the room is left and come back to, a location keeping its roof until the chip takes it off, and the cut set anywhere as the ruler sets it; it sees a spread of places, and the towers and caves among them, each with floors found from the top down and cut over each, no collider left on a copy, and the ruler drawn. It lays each biome's ground with the Ground backdrop, notes the colour of the ground before the model for each and saves a picture of the stage beside the log, and sees a dungeon room keep the plain floor.

It sees a dungeon's stage show three chips at the most with the roof over its ruler, and its plan fold to a tab. It opens a tall dungeon's example on a floor and sees the camera look at that floor with every room on it in the picture, and at the next a floor down, the wheel zoom toward what is under the pointer and a drag keep what was grabbed under it, with a floor opened and with the roof on; it cuts through a place's tallest creature and sees what of it is above the cut still drawn, the picture there changing as the creatures are put away. It goes into each such dungeon's deepest room by the click's own path and sees the room gone into, the cut at head height over its ground, every room standing, half dimmed, dimmed or put away as its ground has it, and the room in the camera's view, pictured, and its outline adding amber pixels to the film against the same film without it, counted by colour as moving water changes pixels too, and coming back out with every room as it was, then goes into a room with floors of its own and opens each, the cut at head height over it, picturing the top one. It sees Mörkhalla open cut from the side and a frost cave from above, goes into Mörkhalla's deepest room so and sees no cut held, no room put away, the room outlined in view and no creature drawn again, switches to the cut from above and back, and pictures it whole and gone into.

It films the inside of each dungeon the inside view is judged by (Frost Caves, Mörkhalla, Sunken Crypts, The Hole and Hildir's cave) with every room put away, whole, dimmed, and dimmed with every colour black, and checks that dimmed rooms keep at most 60% of the brightness their colours give them on the stage and that rooms half dimmed keep more, what is above the film with every colour black, as fog and highlights stay however dark the colours and make most of dark stone's brightness (`DimLook`), telling which shaders the rooms are drawn with and how many of their parts the dimming reaches (`ShaderTally`). It lays out a dungeon and a camp, sees each page tell what its rooms hold and the dungeon's the loot in them, and sees each built on the stage room by room (the dungeon inside, opened on its top floor and stepped down floor by floor, a room found under the mouse, and its entrance outside; the camp around the location), sees its plan draw in the stage's corner (`ScryPanel.Drawn`), and on each floor of the towers and caves the stage stand whole the rooms the plan tiles there, follows a room and a vegvisir to their entries and back, and checks the placement rows.

Last it reads every location, every one again where they are known from before, while one stands on the stage, which must keep its looks as bundles are loaded and let go of around it; sees what was read kept in the cache folder, every place coming back from the file holding what it was read to hold, and, on entering the world again, every place known from the file with none read again and every entry telling the same of them as straight after reading; forgets it all as *Look again* does, sees its files gone and every place told as never read while each is read afresh, and once read every entry telling the same as before and what was read kept again; lays out every dungeon and camp, sees every dungeon head its group with its rooms under it, stands a spread of locations and of rooms on the stage, reads every entry's details with every chip leading somewhere, noting the names of entries told as text with no chip for them anywhere on the page (`NamesInText`: whole words, the first capitalised, the longest name at a place), sees the places items are found in go to their locations, plays a place's music as Enter does with the game's music muted meanwhile and given back after, plays every location's own music, sees every boss tell its altar and every trader what it sells, sees every runestone's texts and titles in words, and sees that closing the panel lets go of every bundle.

The frame figures leave out the self-test's own checks, which run inside Scry's update (reading every entry's details at once is no player's frame): its tick is timed as a part of its own, less the parts timed inside it, so Scry's work it calls, such as a copy made, still counts (`Timing.Own`, `FrameTimes.TotalWithout`); the most its own checks took in a frame is noted. Each part also fails when any part of Scry failed while it ran (`Faults.Count`) or a feature went off for a game change, and a part of a single prefab left out (`Faults.Skipped`) fails it too, except in the parts that read mods' locations and rooms as well, where it is only noted; each tells how long Scry's own work took in its frames (`Timing.Measuring`, `FrameStats`) and how long the part took, and the run ends with the whole run's figures, its slowest frames and its length. A part that runs out of time is ended with a cleanup that puts back what it changed, so the next starts as it expects.

While it runs, a strip under the panel's header says which part runs, how far the test has got (`ScenarioRunner.Done`, `Total`, `Current`, `FailedSoFar`) and how many failed so far, with a bar and a *Stop* button; the chat says at the start how many parts there are and how to stop it. When it ends, the strip gives its headline, and its *Details* stand in the list's place: every failed part with the checks that failed in it, every skipped part with why, how many passed, and what to do next, with *Copy summary* (`SelfTestWords`). The chat gives the headline too, and the log ends with the same summary, and begins with a key to its lines.

The scenarios run one after another on the game's clock (`ScenarioRunner`, tested on a made-up clock): a failed check, an exception or a scenario that never finishes fails that one only, and its cleanup always runs. Nothing is spawned; a scenario that would stand a copy in the world or fire one skips when something of the player's is already there. When it ends the explorer is put back whole (`Explorer.Keep` and `Restore`: search, filters, selection, recent entries and history), the previews it played are stopped, the plan's section is folded again if it was, and the panel closes again if it was closed. The locations it read stay read, as *Find where things are* leaves them. Adjustments on the entry selected before are back at their defaults, as on selecting any entry.
