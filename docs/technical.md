# Technical notes

## The catalog

The catalog is read once per world, the first time the panel opens, and the time it took is written to the log. It holds every prefab registered with the scene, every status effect in the object database, and every prefab reached through an effect list. Most sounds and many visual effects are never registered on their own: they hang off the effect lists of the items, attacks, pieces, creatures, status effects and interface that play them, so those lists are followed, including effects that point at further effects. Effects that belong only to locations, such as boss altars and runestones, are not reached, because locations are loaded from their bundles on demand and walking them would force the loads.

Each entry's kind comes from its components. What a prefab does outranks how it looks: anything with a character is a creature, then projectiles, items and pieces. Something that only has sound is a sound. Particles with nothing solid, or anything drawn that is only ever played from an effect list, is an effect. Everything else, such as trees, rocks and spawners, is other. Entries with nothing to draw, light or hear are listed but marked as having nothing to preview.

A prefab is from the game when it was in the scene's lists before any mod ran, read by a first-priority prefix on `ZNetScene.Awake`; status effects the same way on `ObjectDB.Awake`. An effect is from the game when any game prefab, status effect or the interface uses it.

## Effects

Most creatures have no death animation: the game swaps them for a ragdoll that falls under physics. The few that do set their animator's dead flag first. So Death on a creature that leaves a ragdoll lets the copy fall as one, as `Character.OnDeath` does: a copy of the ragdoll takes its place in the level's colours and wearing its armour (not what it held, which the game drops), knocked back a little, and lies there as long as the ragdoll's own time before its parting effect plays and the creature stands again. Otherwise an effect list animates the creature the way the game does when it plays that list, where the game sets something on the animator for it: a jump by the jump trigger (a flyer's by its take-off), a death without a ragdoll by the dead switch, being alerted by the alert switch, waking by letting the sleeping switch go, eating by the eat or consume trigger, a block by holding the blocking switch a moment, and an item's attack lists, swung by the person holding it, by the trigger the attack names (`Attack.Start`), the first link of a chain; what the attack plays at its strike (its hit, its trigger, its hit on the ground) plays when the swing's animation says it strikes, where the attack reaches (as `Attack.GetProjectileSpawnPoint` places it: from its origin, up by its height, out by its range), and an attack that throws or shoots throws its projectile then.

A creature's attacks are not lists of their own: they play with its animation clips, as in the game, which starts an attack by the animator trigger it names, so the clip that trigger leads to is the attack's. The animator's states cannot be read at run time, so once per creature each attack trigger is pulled on a hidden copy of its animated body and watched as the game watches an attack (`Humanoid.InAttack`): every clip it plays while in a state tagged "attack" is the attack's, in order, so a draugr's bow drawn in one clip and let go in the next is both. Each run is watched beside one left alone; the game picks idle clips at random (`RandomIdle`), so every run is seeded alike and the two go the same way until what was done takes effect. Where an animator tags no attack, the clip of the first state a trigger takes it to that the run left alone is not in is taken, even a clip it also idles in (an asksvin eats in its idle too). The log lists what each trigger plays. Where that shows none, the clip named as the trigger is taken ("attack_claws" is "Attack Claws"), or else the only clip whose name holds the trigger whole ("stomp_l" in "Attack Stomp L"). A clip two attacks play belongs to the one the creature has in the look shown, else to the first it may carry. Playing an attack's clips plays the attack whole, each part where the game plays it: its first clip the weapon's and the attack's start and trail as it begins, at the joint the attack comes from (found, as `Attack.GetAttackOrigin` finds it, in the part called Visual, not in an old model a frost troll keeps switched off beside it); a clip that says when it strikes their trigger at that moment, at that joint, or for a throw where it lets go; their hit where the attack reaches (or, with no hit of its own, their hit on the ground there), with what it spawns there (`m_spawnOnTrigger`); and what it throws, which hits with its own effects where it lands, so a throw plays no hit of the attack's at the hand; an attack none of whose clips says when it strikes strikes halfway through its first. The same watch finds the clips the game's own actions lead to (a switch set as the animator starts, since some go to sleep only from there, and waking only once asleep), where its code plays an effect list as it moves the animator: the jump trigger with the jump effects (`Character.ForceJump`), the consume trigger with eating (`MonsterAI.UpdateConsumeItem`), the sleeping switch on and off with going to sleep and waking (`MonsterAI.Sleep`, `Wakeup`), the alert switch with being alerted (`BaseAI.SetAlerted`); each such clip plays that list as it starts, unless an attack plays the clip or its own Jump event plays the jump. Only what the animator was seen to do counts here, never a clip's name. Some clips the game plays nothing with, yet something is heard around them, and those are listed apart, under Heard around it, and played as the clip starts: with waking, and with a spawn roar (the wakeup switch a spawner sets on what it spawns, `SpawnArea`, `SpawnAbility`), what the creature calls out once alerted, which the game plays when it spots you next (a troll wakes silently and roars then); with the clips it idles in (those the animator plays left alone, over a few seeds), its idle sound, which the game plays now and then while it is awake (`BaseAI.DoIdleSound`). An attack clip hears nothing around it. The game gives creatures their weapons as items, many never drawn and named only for its makers ("slap", "fireballattack"); their other lists, such as a block, are one chip for each list that plays alike, called by the game's name where it has a translated one, else by the item's prefab name made readable without the creature's own name in it ("troll_throw" on a troll is "Throw"). Of the items a creature carries, those of the look shown offer these chips: all it carries in the set it rolled and what it always has, since its AI takes any of its weapons in a fight. When the game would set something the creature's animator does not have, the log says so, since the game then animates nothing either. A hit animates nothing, as in the game. Only for a list the game sets nothing for, such as a mod's, is an animation looked for by its name: death with a death, die or dead clip, a stagger with a stagger clip, being alerted, waking up, eating, blocking and attacking with theirs. The plainest (shortest) clip with the first matching word is played.

The effect a piece, tree, rock or log plays when destroyed destroys the copy as the game destroys the prefab: the copy is not drawn while what it leaves behind falls, and then stands again. What falls is what the game leaves: the debris the list throws (loose parts under physics, which other effect lists leave out), the prefab's own parts when the game breaks it apart (`Destructible.CreateFragments`: each mesh on its own, a little smaller, flung out from the middle and gone after two to four seconds, along set lines when the piece has them), or a tree's log, tipped over away from you as `TreeBase.SpawnLog` tips it, beside its stump. The log says once per prefab which of these it found.

Falling copies keep their bodies, colliders and joints. Their colliders are on Scry's own layer, which is set to meet only the ground (terrain, rocks, buildings) and itself, so in the world they land on the real ground while players, creatures, arrows and every check the game makes pass through them. On the stage they land on an invisible ground under the model.

Effect lists sometimes point at other whole models: creatures a creature splits into, items and skinned bodies. These would only stand frozen beside the preview, so playing an effect leaves them out.

A sound or effect shows the effect lists it is part of, as rows: what the list is for, who plays it, and what else it plays, each of which goes where it names. Lists alike in purpose and members are one row naming all their players, since many prefabs share them. Play plays the whole list together: an effect's around it on the stage, a sound's where you are looking. On a creature, piece or item, the list played last shows what it is made of in the same way.

A lit play button stops what it started when clicked again, and playing again starts over rather than adding to it. A clip's chip lists what the clip plays of itself (the effects its events name, its footsteps, and all its attack plays), each lit while it plays. Footsteps can be heard on any ground the creature's FootStep table knows. A swimming clip steps as the game steps in water: on water, and only where the creature has a swimming step (`FootStep.FindBestStepEffect`). Feet the game names in a model kept switched off are watched in the body, by name. While a copy stands in the world, what plays is heard from there, where it is, and the stage's copies are only seen, so nothing sounds twice; their sound scripts are switched off too, since the game lets only so many of one sound play at once, some anywhere at all (`AudioMan.RequestPlaySound`), and a muted one on the stage would keep the world's from playing. What a model plays is shown at the size the model is shown: twice as big, its hits, steps and effects are twice as big and what it throws flies twice as far in the same time; for that a particle effect takes the size of everything above it, where the game's own heed only their own, and trails and lights reach as far again. At the game's own size nothing of them is changed. A play button stays lit until everything it started has finished: while a copy it made has particles alive, a sound playing or parts falling, or, having none of those, until it is gone. Each part of a list is lit while its own copy plays.

## Preview copies

Every preview is a copy of the prefab made under an inactive holder, so none of its scripts wake. While it sleeps everything is taken off except what draws, animates, lights or sounds: renderers, meshes, particle systems, animators, lights, audio sources, level-of-detail groups, cloth, projectors, and a few of the game's own effect scripts that need neither a network view nor a character (light flicker, random sound clips, timed destruction, fades, billboards and decals). Scripts go first, then joints, then colliders and bodies, and a component another still needs is only removed after it. Scripts added by mods are always removed, since what they need cannot be known. Only then is the copy moved into place and woken. It has no network view, so no other player sees it and nothing of it is saved. Animations play in place. Their events, which the game sends to scripts the copy no longer has, are answered by Scry itself when every event a creature's clips send is one the game's CharacterAnimEvent or AnimationEffect knows: footsteps play from the prefab's FootStep list (a walk on plain ground), swings from the attack whose animation name matches the clip, jumps and deaths from the character's own lists, an effect the event names itself (a wolf's howl) at the bone it names, and a prop the event names hangs on its bone until the clip ends. The rest are heard and ignored. Footsteps are not animation events in the game any more: its walk animations carry a value for the animator that changes sign as a foot comes down, which a clip played on its own does not set. So while a walking, running or sneaking clip plays, the feet the prefab's FootStep names are watched, and a step sounds where one comes down: at the lowest point of its stride, near the bottom of how high it goes, not again within a fifth of a second, and never for a foot that only sways or dips in the air. The step is the prefab's own for plain ground and that way of moving. Unity does not allow a new copy's components to be taken off inside an animation event's callback, so an event is only noted there and answered on the next update. A clip Scry plays itself sends its events from Scry as the clip's time passes them, with the animator's own events off meanwhile, so each is heard once. When a clip sends anything else, as some mods' do, its events stay off, since an event nothing hears makes Unity log an error each time; the log says which events kept a creature silent.

Parts the game fades in by script (`EffectFade`, such as a portal's swirl) start faded out, as they do in the game, until a look fades them in. Star levels and wear are switched by scripts the copy no longer has, so their settings are read from the untouched prefab and applied to the matching parts of the copy, found by their position in the hierarchy.

Animations are played clip by clip rather than through the animator's switches. The switches only move the animator between states under conditions the game sets together (waking only while asleep, jumping only in the air), and those conditions cannot be read at run time, so a switch pulled on its own often does nothing. Each clip in the animator's controller is instead played directly on the copy through a playable graph; when it ends or is stopped the graph is destroyed and the animator carries on as before.

A status effect offers every effect list it carries that has something in it: its start effects are shown on you until taken off, and the others, such as stop, tick or break, play once. One with none says so.

## Looks and gear

Some prefabs have several looks that the game switches by script, and one kind is offered per prefab, in this order: a plant's growth (the grown plant is a prefab of its own, so it is copied instead), a fire's state (the enabled, low, high, full, half and empty objects of `Fireplace`, set as `Fireplace.UpdateState` sets them), a portal's state (its runes in the unconnected or connected colour of `TeleportWorld`, and its swirl faded in when open), a door shut or open either way (its animator's state and open part, as `Door.SetState`), a chest shut or open, a windmill still or turning (its blades and millstone turned as `Windmill.Update` turns them at full wind), a smelter, kiln or furnace cold, loaded or working (its parts and bellows as `Smelter.UpdateState`), a fermenter empty, fermenting or ready, a sap collector idle, working or full, a crafting station cold, with its fire lit or in use, a tameable creature with or without its saddle, a pickable's picked state (its hidden-when-picked object), a humanoid's gear, and an item's style (set through the game's material manager as `ItemStyle.Setup` does).

Gear is put on as `VisEquipment.AttachItem` puts it on: each item's `attach` part hangs on the matching hand or head bone, and an `attach_skin` part is bound to the body's bones. A creature always carrying the same things offers one gear look with its default items; one given one of several sets offers each set. A humanoid rolls one of its gear sets when it spawns, each a look (a set listed twice is one); a set with nothing drawn, such as a troll's without its log, is a look too. A creature holds one weapon at a time, the one it rolled, one it always carries or one of its set; only weapons that show in the hand are choices, one of each that shows alike, so a troll has none to choose (its two log swings draw the same log, its punch and throw nothing), and what draws nothing is not put on the copy at all, as its AI picks them in a fight (`MonsterAI` and `Humanoid.EquipBestWeapon`), chosen in a Holding row; a weapon held in both hands puts the shield away. The items in hand set the stance on the animator as `Humanoid.SetupAnimationState` does, so a draugr stands with its bow as with a bow and a person trying on an axe holds it as one; an item's attack chips on the person swing it by the player's own trigger. Items are handed out in the order `Humanoid.GiveDefaultItems` hands them out (default items, shield, weapon, armour, set, extras), and each takes the place of one worn in the same slot, as equipping does. A creature that rolls its weapon, shield or armour from a list when it spawns (`Humanoid.GiveDefaultItems`) has a row for each list with a choice in it, starting on the first entry, with an empty entry offered as nothing and an item listed several times to make it commoner offered once; the extras it may be given by chance can be put on or taken off, one of each kind of item. Items are hung keeping their size in the world, as the game hangs them, since a creature's bones are often scaled; the item's equip offset and its equipped glow are applied too. A prefab whose body mesh is chosen at run time, such as the player, gets the first of its models. Armour made for a skeleton with a different number of bones is left off rather than drawn twisted. An item shown worn is put on a copy of the player the same way, over whatever items are kept on (one per slot, a two-handed weapon taking both hands), and chest and leg armour also paint the body with their own textures, as the game swaps them. /scry dump writes every renderer of the stage copy to the log, for finding out why part of a model does not show.

## Knowledge

With the catalog, Scry reads where things live and where they come from: the world spawn lists of the running spawn systems (biome, time of day, levels, group size, required key), raids, nests and spawn points among the registered prefabs, and the world's vegetation (biome and altitude). Items learn what drops them from creatures' drop lists, what they are picked from, every drop table on any prefab, every list that turns one item into another (smelters, kilns, fermenters, cooking stations, and mods' that work the same way), and what loaded traders sell. Status effects learn what gives them: any item, attack or part of a prefab that refers to them, or names them. Spawn points sit inside dungeons and locations, which the game only loads nearby, so their biome is not known. Biomes found this way are searchable with biome:.

Which mod added a prefab is read from Jotunn's registry when Jotunn is installed, by reflection, so Scry does not depend on it. For the rest, the loaded asset bundle holding an asset of the prefab's name is found, and the mod shipping a bundle of that name, as a file in its folder or embedded in its assembly, is named. A prefab made in code, or a bundle named unlike anything its mod ships, stays unnamed.

What a status effect changes is every number, switch or choice on it that differs from a fresh status effect of the same kind, plus its damage modifiers, so status effects from mods show too.

## Links

Besides effect lists, prefabs point at one another in other ways, and each becomes a link shown at both ends, each under its own heading: the sounds and effects a creature's animation clips name in their events (the wolf's howl), by clip, where going to the creature plays that clip; a creature's footsteps from its FootStep table, by gait and ground; the items a humanoid may be handed when it spawns, whose attacks also count as the creature's own, so their effects play from its effect chips (with the attack's swing) and name it as their player; any other prefab a field names, directly, in a list, or one level down in the game's small data classes (the projectile an attack fires, what a projectile spawns on hit, a plant's grown version, a spawner's creature), leaving out parts of the prefab itself, drop tables, carried items and leftovers, which are told elsewhere; items of the same armour set, where the game's copies of a piece for creatures to wear (same name, same set, such as the FallenWarrior's FW_ and ShadowPerson's SP_ troll leather) are linked to it as its variants rather than listed as more pieces; a crafting station's upgrades; weapons and the ammo of their ammo type; and the status effects prefabs give, by reference, by name, or by their damage (fire burns, frost, lightning, poison and spirit each put on theirs, as the game adds them). An item's own status effects (worn, used, set bonus, on hit, and those its damage causes) are told in its facts, with links, so only the status effect's side lists the item. Links to anything not in the catalog, and to itself, are left out.

## Leftovers

What a prefab leaves behind is paired with it: a creature's ragdoll and the remains its death throws, a tree's log and stump, a log's halves, a rock's broken version, and the debris anything throws when destroyed. Only what would otherwise be listed as other is paired. A leftover is named after its owner ("Troll · ragdoll") and takes its owner's kind, so it sits beside it in the list and a search for the owner finds it; the two link to each other. One shared by owners of different names, such as the debris of every wooden piece, is named for what it is and how many leave it ("Debris of 30"), and one shared by owners of different kinds stays among the others.

## The stage

The turntable is a copy far above the world on a layer the game does not name or use. It has its own camera, which renders into a texture the panel draws, and its own lights. The world's sun and the main camera are told to ignore that layer, and fog and ambient light are set for the stage only while its camera renders, then restored. The camera frames the copy from the size of what it draws, with particles left out when there is anything else. Effects on the stage are heard as if beside you; anything else on the stage is muted. The stage copy is taken down while the panel is closed.

## In the world

A model shown in the world stands where the camera was looking when it was placed, facing you. Effects play at the same spot or attached to you, and are removed after ten seconds if they do not end themselves. Projectiles lose their own script, so they are flown instead with the prefab's gravity and a chosen speed, and a ray along the path stops them at the first ground or building and plays their hit effects there. The ray only looks. Status effects show their start effects attached to you as the game would attach them, and their stop effects when taken off; the status effect itself is never added. Sounds are copies attached to the camera, kept for as long as something on them plays or is paused, so long clips such as music play to the end and can be skipped through by setting the audio source's position. Most sounds hold several clips and the game's sound script picks one at random each time it plays, when it starts a frame after the copy wakes. To play one variant on purpose, the copy's list is narrowed to that clip before then, so it still plays with the sound's own volume, pitch variation and mix; parts of the same sound that do not hold the clip are kept quiet. The panel lights the clip that is actually playing, so a random play shows which variant was heard. Skipping through or pausing a sound hands its end to Scry: the prefab's timed destruction and the sound script's scheduled fade are called off, since both count time played rather than the place in the clip. Location music does not play on its own; the game's `MusicLocation` starts it when you come near, so Scry starts it instead, at your music volume.

## Input

While the panel is open, `TextInput.IsVisible` answers yes. The game already stops moving, looking, attacking and opening its menu while a text box is up, and frees the cursor, so that covers all of it. The camera zoom reads the wheel regardless, so the one method all of the game's wheel reads go through, `ZInput.Internal_GetMouseScrollWheel`, answers nothing while the panel is open. The block lasts one frame past closing, so the Escape that closed the panel does not also open the game's menu.

Holding the right mouse button, pressed outside the panel, looks around. The game asks for mouse look separately from movement and actions (`PlayerController.TakeInput(look: true)`), so only that question is answered yes while the button is held, and the cursor is captured as in normal play. Walking, blocking and attacking stay off.

The compact view is a slim column at the side of the screen without the turntable, which is not rendered while it is in use. Both views remember their own place and size. The character can walk while the panel is open: the movement half of `TakeInput` is answered yes whenever none of the panel's text boxes has the keyboard, and any click lets go of the keyboard unless it lands on a text box. The full view opens with the search focused, the compact view does not. While the panel is open, `Player.SetControls` never receives attack, block or dodge, so clicks on the panel and the look button stay harmless.

## Files

Favourites and the panel's state (its place on screen in both views, the stage's lighting, backdrop, person and spinning, the stage's height, and which sections are folded) are kept in `Scry` inside the game's own data folder (on Windows `%USERPROFILE%\AppData\LocalLow\IronGate\Valheim\Scry`), outside the BepInEx folder, so a mod manager replacing a profile's configs leaves them alone. Favourites of prefabs that are not in the game right now are kept.

## Game updates

Most of what Scry shows is read from the game as it runs, so new creatures, items and mods appear without a change to Scry. Some previews copy the workings of the game's own scripts, and a few features reach private parts of the game by name; an update can change those. So the first time Scry reads the catalog in a world it checks each of them and says in the log what it found: one line when all is as expected, and otherwise a line for each part that is missing (its feature is off) or has changed (its preview may be slightly wrong).

It checks the private members it reaches by name, that each of its patches is on its method, the code of about thirty game methods whose workings its previews copy (gear attachment, star levels, wear, fire and portal looks, ragdolls, breaking pieces, falling trees, where effects appear, footsteps, the status effects damage causes, and more), that the game answers no animation event Scry does not, the layers falling copies land on, the effect scripts copies keep, and the status effects and prefab it looks up by name. A method's code is compared by its shape: the sequence of its steps, leaving out what they name, which the game numbers anew in every build, so only a rewritten method counts as changed. The shapes were read from Valheim 1.0.16's own assembly with `tools/shapes` (`dotnet run -c Release -- <assembly_valheim.dll> Type.Method ...`), which reads them again after an update. A mod that rewrites one of these methods before the game starts would show it as changed too.

Each patch is applied on its own, so one whose method an update renamed costs only its own feature, and a private member that is gone costs only what needs it.

## Limits

A mod prefab whose look is built by its own scripts may preview bare. Locations cannot be previewed. The stage is lit by its own lights, so materials that depend on the world's lighting can look different there than in the world.

## Building

The project builds with the .NET SDK against the game's assemblies. Set `VALHEIM_DIR` to the game folder if it is not a standard Steam install, then run `dotnet build`, which also copies the DLL into the local Gale profile. `dotnet test tests/Scry.Tests` runs the tests for the parts that need no game: kinds, search, the preview copy's rules, modifiers, favourites and origins.
