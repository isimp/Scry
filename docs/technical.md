# Technical notes

## The catalog

The catalog is read once per world, a few milliseconds each frame, starting ten seconds after the world is entered, once the game has loaded what is around you, so it is usually ready before the panel is first opened. Opened earlier, the panel says how far the reading has got, and it goes faster while the panel waits. Nothing of it is shown until it is all read, since an entry's kind, users, origin, leftovers and links are only known then. The time it took is written to the log. Each prefab's components are looked through once, for its own details, where things come from and its links; a prefab, status effect or entry that cannot be read is left out and the kind of failure told once, rather than costing the rest. It holds every prefab registered with the scene, every status effect in the object database, and every prefab reached through an effect list. Most sounds and many visual effects are never registered on their own: they hang off the effect lists of the items, attacks, pieces, creatures, status effects and interface that play them, so those lists are followed, including effects that point at further effects. Effects that belong only to locations, such as boss altars and runestones, are not reached, because locations are loaded from their bundles on demand and walking them would force the loads.

Each entry's kind comes from its components. What a prefab does outranks how it looks: anything with a character is a creature, then projectiles, items and pieces. What is chopped, mined, picked or grown (the game's `TreeBase`, `TreeLog`, `MineRock`, `MineRock5`, `Pickable`, `PickableItem` and `Plant`), or breaks into drops (a `Destructible` with a `DropOnDestroyed` that drops something), is a resource, unless it spawns creatures, as a nest does. What is chopped, mined or picked stays a resource when a mod also makes it buildable (*MoreVanillaBuildPrefabs* adds a `Piece` to hundreds of the game's own prefabs, trees and rocks among them), while a crop planted with the cultivator (a `Plant` with a `Piece`) is a piece, since it is built like one. A mod that makes one of the game's own prefabs buildable this way adds a `Piece` it leaves switched off on the prefab; none of the 25,802 pieces in the game's own bundles is switched off, so such a piece does not make its prefab a piece at all: a ruin wall or a skull stays what the game made it, and its facts say it is buildable through the mod and what that costs. Something that only has sound is a sound. Particles with nothing solid, or anything drawn that is only ever played from an effect list, is an effect. Everything else, such as spawners and props, is other. A resource's facts tell its health (a rock's per piece, since it is mined a piece at a time), the tool tier it needs, what it resists, and its drops as the game rolls them (`DropTable.GetDropList`: how many times, how often, each item's share of a roll); a bush what it gives, when it grows back and how much that comes to in the game's days (`EnvMan.m_dayLengthSec`); a plant how long it takes to grow, where and on what, and what it grows into. A resource also says what it comes from and what it becomes, each a link: a tree falls as its log and leaves its stump (`TreeBase.SpawnLog`), a log splits into its halves (`TreeLog.m_subLogPrefab`, one for each split point), and a vein's shell breaks into the vein (`Destructible.m_spawnWhenDestroyed`). On the Resources tab they are grouped under headings by how they are gathered: trees, logs, rocks and ore, plants, bushes and pickables, then what is broken for its drops. Where it grows says, besides biome and height, whether only in forests and in what groups. Entries with nothing to draw, light or hear are listed but marked as having nothing to preview.

A prefab is from the game when it was in the scene's lists before any mod ran, read by a first-priority prefix on `ZNetScene.Awake`; status effects the same way on `ObjectDB.Awake`. An effect is from the game when any game prefab, status effect or the interface uses it.

## Preview copies

Every preview is a copy of the prefab made under an inactive holder, so none of its scripts wake. While it sleeps everything is taken off except what draws, animates, lights or sounds: renderers, meshes, particle systems, animators, lights, audio sources, level-of-detail groups, cloth, projectors, and a few of the game's own effect scripts that need neither a network view nor a character (light flicker, random sound clips, timed destruction, fades, billboards and decals). Scripts go first, then joints, then colliders and bodies, and a component another still needs is only removed after it. Scripts added by mods are always removed, since what they need cannot be known. Only then is the copy moved into place and woken. It has no network view, so no other player sees it and nothing of it is saved.

Parts the game fades in by script (`EffectFade`, such as a portal's swirl) start faded out, as they do in the game, until a look fades them in. Star levels and wear are switched by scripts the copy no longer has, so their settings are read from the untouched prefab and applied to the matching parts of the copy, found by their position in the hierarchy. A creature's body is its part called Visual, as `Character.Awake` finds it; some keep an old model switched off beside it (a frost troll's), and joints, feet and the animator are always taken from the body.

## Looks and gear

Some prefabs have several looks that the game switches by script, and one kind is offered per prefab, in this order: a plant's growth (the grown plant is a prefab of its own, so it is copied instead), a fire's state (the enabled, low, high, full, half and empty objects of `Fireplace`, set as `Fireplace.UpdateState` sets them), a portal's state (its runes in the unconnected or connected colour of `TeleportWorld`, and its swirl faded in when open), a door shut or open either way (its animator's state and open part, as `Door.SetState`), a chest shut or open, a windmill still or turning (its blades and millstone turned as `Windmill.Update` turns them at full wind), a smelter, kiln or furnace cold, loaded or working (its parts and bellows as `Smelter.UpdateState`), a fermenter empty, fermenting or ready, a sap collector idle, working or full, a crafting station cold, with its fire lit or in use, a tameable creature with or without its saddle, a pickable's picked state (its hidden-when-picked object), a humanoid's gear, and an item's style (set through the game's material manager as `ItemStyle.Setup` does).

Gear is put on as `VisEquipment.AttachItem` puts it on: each item's `attach` part hangs on the matching hand or head bone, and an `attach_skin` part is bound to the body's bones. Items are hung keeping their size in the world, as the game hangs them, since a creature's bones are often scaled; the item's equip offset and its equipped glow are applied too. A prefab whose body mesh is chosen at run time, such as the player, gets the first of its models. Armour made for a skeleton with a different number of bones is left off rather than drawn twisted.

A humanoid rolls one of its gear sets when it spawns, and each set is a look (a set listed twice is one); a set with nothing drawn, such as a troll's without its log, is a look too. A creature always carrying the same things offers one look with its default items. One that rolls its weapon, shield or armour from a list (`Humanoid.GiveDefaultItems`) has a row for each list with a choice in it, starting on the first entry, with an empty entry offered as nothing and an item listed several times to make it commoner offered once; the extras it may be given by chance can be put on or taken off, one of each kind of item. Items are handed out in the order the game hands them out (default items, shield, weapon, armour, set, extras), and each takes the place of one worn in the same slot, as equipping does.

A creature holds one weapon at a time, as its AI picks one in a fight (`MonsterAI`, `Humanoid.EquipBestWeapon`), chosen in a Holding row from the one it rolled, those it always carries and those of its set. Only weapons that show in the hand are choices, one of each that shows alike, so a troll has none to choose (its two log swings draw the same log, its punch and throw nothing), and what draws nothing is not put on the copy at all. A weapon held in both hands puts the shield away. The items in hand set the stance on the animator as `Humanoid.SetupAnimationState` does, so a draugr stands with its bow as with a bow and a person trying on an axe holds it as one.

An item shown worn is put on a copy of the player the same way, over whatever items are kept on (one per slot, a two-handed weapon taking both hands), and chest and leg armour also paint the body with their own textures, as the game swaps them. `/scry dump` writes every renderer of the stage copy to the log, for finding out why part of a model does not show.

## Animations

Animations are played clip by clip rather than through the animator's switches. The switches only move the animator between states under conditions the game sets together, and those conditions cannot be read at run time, so a switch pulled on its own often does nothing. Each clip in the animator's controller is instead played directly on the copy through a playable graph; when it ends or is stopped the graph is destroyed and the animator carries on as before. Animations play in place.

A clip's events, which the game sends to scripts the copy no longer has, are answered by Scry when every event a creature's clips send is one the game's `CharacterAnimEvent` or `AnimationEffect` knows: an effect the event names (a wolf's howl) plays at the bone it names, a prop hangs on its bone until the clip ends, parts are shown and hidden as `AnimationObjectToggle` does, jumps and deaths play the character's own lists, and a strike plays its attack's strike (below). The rest are heard and ignored. When a clip sends anything else, as some mods' do, its events stay off, since an event nothing hears makes Unity log an error each time. Unity does not allow a new copy's components to be taken off inside an event's callback, so an event is only noted there and answered on the next update. A clip Scry plays sends its events from Scry as its time passes them, with the animator's own events off meanwhile, so each is heard once.

Footsteps are not animation events in the game: its walk animations carry a value for the animator that changes sign as a foot comes down, which a clip played on its own does not set. So while a clip that moves the creature along plays (walking, running, sneaking, turning and the like), the feet the prefab's FootStep names are watched in the body, and a step sounds where one comes down: at the lowest point of its stride, near the bottom of how high it goes, not again within a fifth of a second, and never for a foot that only sways or dips in the air. The step is chosen from the creature's FootStep table for that way of moving on the ground chosen in the panel. A swimming clip steps as the game steps in water: on water, and only where the creature has a swimming step (`FootStep.FindBestStepEffect`).

### What the animator plays

Which clip an animator plays for a trigger or switch cannot be read either, so once per creature (and for a person, once for each stance its weapons give it) each is done on a hidden copy of its animated body, from where the animator starts, and watched beside a run left alone. The game picks idle clips at random (`RandomIdle`), so every run is seeded alike and the two go the same way until what was done takes effect. The watching is spread over frames, about four milliseconds of it each, so choosing a creature never stalls the game; it stops when another entry is chosen and waits while the panel is closed; until it is done the panel lists the clips unsorted and says it is still working out what they play, rather than show the part found so far. A clip played meanwhile shows at once, and what it plays of an attack or with the game's own actions follows as soon as it is known. A watch that fails is tried once more, then given up and said so in the log. The log tells what was seen and how long it took.

An attack is watched as the game watches one (`Humanoid.InAttack`): every clip it plays while in a state tagged "attack" is the attack's, in order, so a draugr's bow drawn in one clip and let go in the next is both. Where an animator tags no attack, the clip of the first state a trigger takes it to that the run left alone is not in is taken, even a clip it also idles in (an asksvin eats in its idle too).

The same watch finds where the game's own actions lead, the ones its code plays an effect list with: the jump trigger (`Character.ForceJump`), the consume trigger (`MonsterAI.UpdateConsumeItem`), the sleeping switch on and off (`MonsterAI.Sleep`, `Wakeup`), the alert switch (`BaseAI.SetAlerted`), the dead switch (`Character.OnDeath`), the stagger trigger (`Character.RPC_Stagger`), the wakeup switch a spawner sets on what it spawns (`CreatureSpawner`, `SpawnAbility`), and the in-water and flying switches, in water both still and swimming along (watched beside a run walking as fast, so a walk is not taken for a swim). A switch is set as the animator starts, since some go to sleep only from there, and waking is watched only once it has fallen asleep. It also notes the clips the animator plays when left alone, over a few seeds, as the clips it idles in.

### Attacks

The game gives creatures their attacks as items and starts an attack by the animator trigger it names, so the clips that trigger leads to are the attack's, and a creature's attacks play with its clips rather than as lists of their own. Where the watch saw none, the clip named as the trigger is taken ("attack_claws" is *Attack Claws*), or else the only clip whose name holds the trigger whole ("stomp_l" in *Attack Stomp L*). A clip two attacks play belongs to the one the creature has in the look shown, else to the first it may carry. On a person, the attacks are those of what it wears, found in the stance the item gives, and an item's attack chips play the clip the person swings it in, lit while it plays.

An attack's clips play it whole, each part where the game plays it. As its first clip starts, the weapon's and the attack's start and trail play at the joint the attack comes from (`Attack.GetAttackOrigin`). When a clip says the attack strikes, its trigger plays: a swing's at that joint, an area attack's in the middle of its area, a throw's where it lets go (`DoMeleeAttack`, `DoAreaAttack`, `ProjectileAttackTriggered`). Its hit plays where it lands: a swing sweeps out from the joint as far as its range and hits the first thing there, and a creature swings once one is within its weapon's attack distance (`m_aiAttackRange`), so the hit is where that one would stand, never beyond the sweep; an area attack hits in its middle. An attack with no hit of its own plays its hit on the ground there instead. What it spawns (`m_spawnOnTrigger`) appears there too, and only an attack that throws or shoots throws what it names, as `Attack.OnAttackTrigger` does, the thing thrown hitting with its own effects where it lands; a throw plays no hit of the attack's at the hand, and a swing that names a projectile throws nothing. Heights, ranges and offsets are in metres, as the game has them whatever the creature's own scale (a frost troll's is four). An attack none of whose clips says when it strikes strikes halfway through its first.

The game names creatures' weapon items only for its makers ("slap", "fireballattack"). Their other lists, such as a block, are one chip under Effects for each list that plays alike, called by the game's name where it has a translated one, else by the item's prefab name made readable without the creature's own name in it ("troll_throw" on a troll is "Throw"). Of the items a creature carries, those of the look shown offer these chips, since its AI takes any of its weapons in a fight.

### What clips play

A clip the game's own action leads to plays the effect list the game plays with that action as it starts: the jump with the jump effects, eating with the consume effects, going to sleep and waking with their effects, being alerted, and dying with the death effects (unless its own Jump or Die event plays them). In water and flying the game keeps its water effect (at the surface, the creature's swim depth above it) and its flying effect going (`Character.UpdateContinousEffects`), so those clips keep them going for as long as they play; a born flyer whose animator has no flying switch flies whatever it plays, so its flying effect is kept going in every clip. Only what the animator was seen to do counts here. Where it could not be seen to swim or jump at all (a troll's controller wants more than its in-water switch or jump trigger, and what cannot be read), clips named for swimming or treading water keep the water effect going and a clip named for jumping plays the jump, listed apart under Found by name, never an attack's clip or one paired already.

Some clips the game plays nothing with, yet something is heard around them, and those are listed apart, under Heard around it, and played as the clip starts: with waking and a spawn roar, what the creature calls out once alerted, which the game plays when it spots you next (a troll wakes silently and roars then); with the clips it idles in, its idle sound, which the game plays now and then while it is awake (`BaseAI.DoIdleSound`); with its stagger, its hit, since the game staggers a creature only once hits have piled up (`Character.AddStaggerDamage`). An attack clip hears nothing around it.

Clips are named by the game's modelers (a troll's side log swing is *Sword-Attack-R2*), so each clip's chip also says what it is, where Scry saw it: the attack it plays, what the creature does in it (jumps, wakes, sleeps, swims, staggers, dies and so on), "by name" where it was only found by its name, or that it idles in it. They are listed in three groups: the attacks (on a person, only those of the item it holds, since a person has hundreds of clips and only a few are the item's), those that play any sound or effect, and the silent; in each, those Scry knows come first. A clip's chip lists what the clip plays, each part lit while it plays. While Scry plays none, the clip the animator plays on its own is marked with a ▶, so a bat that flies whatever it does shows which it is in.

## Effects

Most creatures have no death animation: the game swaps them for a ragdoll that falls under physics. So *Death* on a creature that leaves a ragdoll lets the copy fall as one, as `Character.OnDeath` does: a copy of the ragdoll takes its place in the level's colours and wearing its armour (not what it held, which the game drops), knocked back a little, and lies there as long as the ragdoll's own time before its parting effect plays and the creature stands again. Otherwise an effect list animates the creature the way the game does when it plays that list, where the game sets something on the animator for it: a jump by the jump trigger (a flyer's by its take-off), a death without a ragdoll by the dead switch, being alerted by the alert switch, waking by letting the sleeping switch go, eating by the eat or consume trigger, and a block by holding the blocking switch a moment. When the game would set something the creature's animator does not have, the log says so, since the game then animates nothing either. A list the game sets nothing on the animator for, but that goes with a clip the watch saw the game's own action lead to, plays that clip; no clip is ever looked for by a list's name, and a hit animates nothing, as in the game. A list played last lists under it what it is made of and the clips it goes with, to play from there: those that play it, those found by name to, and those it is heard around.

The effect a piece, tree, rock or log plays when destroyed destroys the copy as the game destroys the prefab: the copy is not drawn while what it leaves behind falls, and then stands again. What falls is what the game leaves: the debris the list throws (loose parts under physics, which other effect lists leave out), the prefab's own parts when the game breaks it apart (`Destructible.CreateFragments`: each mesh on its own, a little smaller, flung out from the middle and gone after two to four seconds, along set lines when the piece has them), or a tree's log, tipped over away from you as `TreeBase.SpawnLog` tips it, beside its stump. A log splits into its halves at its split points (`TreeLog.Destroy`), a rock or prop leaves what the game puts in its place (`Destructible.Destroy`), and trees, logs and props with a drop table let what it rolls fall out where the game puts it (`TreeBase`, `TreeLog`, `DropOnDestroyed`), as items that fall and settle; a rock mined drops piece by piece in the game, so its drops are not shown. Debris and gibs fly out as the game's `Gibber` throws them: each piece away from their middle, turned towards the hit, at its own speed and spinning. A tree's *Hit* shakes its trunk a second, as `TreeBase` does when struck.

A log or an item, which the game leaves to physics, can be let fall: a copy drops from where it stands, a log pushed across its length so it rolls, an item set tumbling, and the model stands again after a while. Falling copies keep their bodies, colliders and joints, and a body is pushed out of what it overlaps no faster than the game's own script sets on waking (`ItemDrop` and `TreeLog` allow 1 m/s), so a log's halves and the items that fall out, made inside one another, part gently instead of flying off. Their colliders are on Scry's own layer, which is set to meet only the ground (terrain, rocks, buildings) and itself, so in the world they land on the real ground while players, creatures, arrows and every check the game makes pass through them. In the world a collider that shares its object with something drawn is copied onto an object of its own, so the drawing stays on a layer the camera sees; a box, sphere or capsule is copied in its real size onto an object of no scale of its own, since under a part scaled unevenly (a log is 12 by 12 by 6.5) the copy would be stretched. On the stage they land on an invisible ground under the model. Effect lists sometimes point at other whole models: creatures a creature splits into, items and skinned bodies. These would only stand frozen beside the preview, so playing an effect leaves them out. What the game places at a point, such as a hit, it creates without a parent (`EffectList.Create`), so such effects never hang on a named part of the model.

What a model plays is shown at the size the model is shown: twice as big, its hits, steps and effects are twice as big and what it throws flies twice as far in the same time. For that a particle effect takes the size of everything above it, where the game's own heed only their own, and trails and lights reach as far again. At the game's own size nothing of them is changed.

A sound or effect shows the effect lists it is part of, as rows: what the list is for, who plays it, and what else it plays, each of which goes where it names. Lists alike in purpose and members are one row naming all their players, since many prefabs share them. Play plays the whole list together: an effect's around it on the stage, a sound's where you are looking.

A lit play button stops what it started when clicked again, and playing again starts over rather than adding to it. It stays lit until everything it started has finished: while a copy it made has particles alive, a sound playing or parts falling, or, having none of those, until it is gone. Each part of a list is lit while its own copy plays. A filter set on the clips or the effects stays visible while it filters, also on an entry with few of them, so it can always be cleared, and a cross in it clears it.

A status effect offers every effect list it carries that has something in it: its start effects are shown on you until taken off, and the others, such as stop, tick or break, play once. One with none says so.

## Knowledge

With the catalog, Scry reads where things live and where they come from: the world spawn lists of the running spawn systems (biome, time of day, levels, group size, required key), raids, nests and spawn points among the registered prefabs, and the world's vegetation (biome and altitude). Items learn what drops them from creatures' drop lists, what they are picked from, every drop table on any prefab, every list that turns one item into another (smelters, kilns, fermenters, cooking stations, and mods' that work the same way), and what loaded traders sell. Status effects learn what gives them: any item, attack or part of a prefab that refers to them, or names them. Spawn points sit inside dungeons and locations, which the game only loads nearby, so their biome is not known. Biomes found this way are searchable with biome:. Items also learn what they are used for: the recipes they go into (by station, with how many each takes), the pieces they build, what stations turn them into, what burns them as fuel (any item field named for fuel, so mods' stations count) and what eats them; a row of more than twenty shows eighteen until asked for the rest.

Which mod added a prefab is read from Jotunn's registry when Jotunn is installed, by reflection on Jotunn's own assembly, so Scry does not depend on it. For the rest, the loaded asset bundle holding an asset of the prefab's name is found, and the mod shipping a bundle of that name, as a file in its folder or embedded in its assembly, is named. A prefab made in code, or a bundle named unlike anything its mod ships, stays unnamed.

What a status effect changes is every number, switch or choice on it that differs from a fresh status effect of the same kind, plus its damage modifiers, so status effects from mods show too.

## Links

Besides effect lists, prefabs point at one another in other ways, and each becomes a link shown at both ends, each under its own heading: the sounds and effects a creature's animation clips name in their events (the wolf's howl), by clip, where going to the creature plays that clip; a creature's footsteps from its FootStep table, by gait and ground; the items a humanoid may be handed when it spawns, whose attacks' sounds and effects also name it as their player; any other prefab a field names, directly, in a list, or one level down in the game's small data classes (the projectile an attack fires, only where the attack throws or shoots, and what it spawns, only where it swings or hits an area, as `Attack.OnAttackTrigger` uses them; what a projectile spawns on hit, a plant's grown version, a spawner's creature), leaving out parts of the prefab itself, drop tables, carried items and leftovers, which are told elsewhere; items of the same armour set, where the game's copies of a piece for creatures to wear (same name, same set, such as the FallenWarrior's FW_ and ShadowPerson's SP_ troll leather) are linked to it as its variants rather than listed as more pieces; a crafting station's upgrades; weapons and the ammo of their ammo type; and the status effects prefabs give, by reference, by name, or by their damage (fire burns, frost, lightning, poison and spirit each put on theirs, as the game adds them). An item's own status effects (worn, used, set bonus, on hit, and those its damage causes) are told in its facts, with links, so only the status effect's side lists the item. Links to anything not in the catalog, and to itself, are left out.

## Leftovers

What a prefab leaves behind is paired with it: a creature's ragdoll and the remains its death throws, a tree's log and stump, a log's halves, a rock's broken version, and the debris anything throws when destroyed. Only what would otherwise be listed as other is paired. A leftover is named after its owner ("Troll · ragdoll") and takes its owner's kind, so it sits beside it in the list and a search for the owner finds it; the two link to each other. One shared by owners of different names, such as the debris of every wooden piece, is named for what it is and how many leave it ("Debris of 30"), and one shared by owners of different kinds stays among the others.

## The stage

The turntable is a copy far above the world on a layer the game does not name or use. It has its own camera, which renders into a texture the panel draws, and its own lights. The world's sun and the main camera are told to ignore that layer, and fog and ambient light are set for the stage only while its camera renders, then restored. The camera frames the copy from the size of what it draws, with particles left out when there is anything else, and only what shows above where a creature stands (a root's base in the ground is left out). A creature on its feet, and the person, stand where the game stands them, on the bottom of their capsule (the character's `CapsuleCollider` rests on the ground), whatever of their model reaches below it: a root's base is in the ground, an atgeir held low reaches below the feet. Anything else stands on the lowest point of its body, not of what hangs on it; a flyer is measured over its flight. The stage copy is taken down while the panel is closed, and a sound playing at your ears is stopped, looping ones such as location music included.

An effect staged on its own, and what a model plays there, are heard as if beside you; a model's own built-in sounds are muted on the stage. While a copy stands in the world, what plays is heard from there, where it is, and the stage's copies are only seen, so nothing sounds twice; their sound scripts are switched off too, since the game lets only so many of one sound play at once, some anywhere at all (`AudioMan.RequestPlaySound`), and a muted one on the stage would keep the world's from playing.

## In the world

A model shown in the world stands where the camera was looking when it was placed, facing you. Effects play at the same spot or attached to you, and are removed after ten seconds if they do not end themselves. Projectiles lose their own script, so they are flown instead with the prefab's gravity and a chosen speed, and a ray along the path stops them at the first ground or building and plays their hit effects there. The ray only looks. Status effects show their start effects attached to you as the game would attach them, and their stop effects when taken off; the status effect itself is never added.

Sounds are copies attached to the camera, kept for as long as something on them plays or is paused, so long clips such as music play to the end and can be skipped through by setting the audio source's position. Most sounds hold several clips and the game's sound script picks one at random each time it plays, when it starts a frame after the copy wakes. To play one variant on purpose, the copy's list is narrowed to that clip before then, so it still plays with the sound's own volume, pitch variation and mix; parts of the same sound that do not hold the clip are kept quiet. The panel lights the clip that is actually playing, so a random play shows which variant was heard. Skipping through or pausing a sound hands its end to Scry: the prefab's timed destruction and the sound script's scheduled fade are called off, since both count time played rather than the place in the clip. Location music does not play on its own; the game's `MusicLocation` starts it when you come near, so Scry starts it instead, at your music volume.

## Input

While the panel is open, `TextInput.IsVisible` answers yes. The game already stops moving, looking, attacking and opening its menu while a text box is up, and frees the cursor, so that covers all of it. The camera zoom reads the wheel regardless, so the one method all of the game's wheel reads go through, `ZInput.Internal_GetMouseScrollWheel`, answers nothing while the panel is open. The block lasts one frame past closing, so the Escape that closed the panel does not also open the game's menu.

Holding the right mouse button, pressed outside the panel, looks around. The game asks for mouse look separately from movement and actions (`PlayerController.TakeInput(look: true)`), so only that question is answered yes while the button is held, and the cursor is captured as in normal play. Walking, blocking and attacking stay off.

The compact view is a slim column at the side of the screen without the turntable, which is not rendered while it is in use. Both views remember their own place and size. The character can walk while the panel is open: the movement half of `TakeInput` is answered yes whenever none of the panel's text boxes has the keyboard, and any click lets go of the keyboard unless it lands on a text box. The full view opens with the search focused, the compact view does not. While the panel is open, `Player.SetControls` never receives attack, block or dodge, so clicks on the panel and the look button stay harmless.

## Files

Favourites and the panel's state (its place on screen in both views, the stage's lighting, backdrop, person and spinning, the stage's height, and which sections are folded) are kept in `Scry` inside the game's own data folder (on Windows `%USERPROFILE%\AppData\LocalLow\IronGate\Valheim\Scry`), outside the BepInEx folder, so a mod manager replacing a profile's configs leaves them alone. Favourites of prefabs that are not in the game right now are kept.

## Game updates

Most of what Scry shows is read from the game as it runs, so new creatures, items and mods appear without a change to Scry. Some previews copy the workings of the game's own scripts, and a few features reach private parts of the game by name; an update can change those. So the first time Scry reads the catalog in a world it checks each of them and says in the log what it found: one line when all is as expected, and otherwise a line for each part that is missing (its feature is off) or has changed (its preview may be slightly wrong).

It checks the private members it reaches by name, that each of its patches is on its method, the code of about seventy game methods whose workings its previews copy (gear attachment, star levels, wear, fire and portal looks, ragdolls, breaking pieces, falling trees, where effects appear, footsteps and which step is made, the status effects damage causes, how attacks start, strike and where they hit, what the animator is seen to do for an attack or the game's own actions and which idle clips it picks, what is kept going in water and flying, what is heard around waking, idling and staggering, and more), that the game answers no animation event Scry does not, the layers falling copies land on, the effect scripts copies keep, and the status effects and prefab it looks up by name. A method's code is compared by its shape: the sequence of its steps, leaving out what they name, which the game numbers anew in every build, so only a rewritten method counts as changed. The shapes were read from Valheim 1.0.16's own assembly with `tools/shapes` (`dotnet run -c Release -- <assembly_valheim.dll> Type.Method ...`), which reads them again after an update. A mod that rewrites one of these methods before the game starts would show it as changed too. Each patch is applied on its own, so one whose method an update renamed costs only its own feature, and a private member that is gone costs only what needs it.

That line, and how long reading the catalog took, is all Scry writes to the log unless `LogPreviews` (in the Diagnostics section of its config) is on. With it on, the log also tells, once for each effect list and clip, what playing it made and whether each part was seen or heard; for each creature what its animator was seen to do and in how long, which animator plays it, which gear sets it rolls and what it holds; what pieces leave when destroyed; where hits land; any frame in which Scry's own work took 20 ms or more, split by part down to the panel's sections; and, for everything let fall, where it was made, how high and fast it went and where it came to rest. These are for finding out why something looks or sounds wrong, and cost a little time while on.

## Limits

A mod prefab whose look is built by its own scripts may preview bare. Locations cannot be previewed. The stage is lit by its own lights, so materials that depend on the world's lighting can look different there than in the world. A few things stand in for what the game does with a target or in the world, and are approximations: a swing's hit is placed where one it swings at would stand, an attack whose clips never say when it strikes strikes halfway, steps are heard where a foot comes down rather than from the animator's own value, a burst of projectiles throws one, and clips the animator could not be seen to reach are only paired by their names where shown so.

## Building

The project builds with the .NET SDK against the game's assemblies. Set `VALHEIM_DIR` to the game folder if it is not a standard Steam install, then run `dotnet build`, which also copies the DLL into the local Gale profile. `dotnet test tests/Scry.Tests` runs the tests for the parts that need no game: kinds, search, the preview copy's rules, modifiers, looks and outfits, gear rows and weapon choices, favourites, origins, history, links, leftovers, what plays in what and what is lit, footsteps from a foot's height, which clips attacks and the game's own actions play, what is found by name or heard around a clip, and the startup check.
