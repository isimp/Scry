# Search, keys and input

What the search takes and how it matches, its suggestions and help, the list's header in both views, and how keys reach the panel rather than the game.

Code: `src/Model/Search.cs`, `src/Model/HeaderLayout.cs`, `src/Model/SearchFlags.cs`, `src/Model/SearchSlip.cs`, `src/Model/SearchHelp.cs`, `src/Model/SearchLit.cs`, `src/UI/ScryPanel.Lit.cs`, `src/Catalog/CatalogBuilder.Terms.cs`, `src/Model/PlaceGrouping.cs`.

See also: [catalog](catalog.md), [showing](compendium/showing.md); all notes in [the index](index.md).

## Search and keys

The search matches names and takes terms (`kind:`, `has:`, `biome:`, `mod:`, `playedby:`, `station:`, `in:`, `is:`, `weak:`, `resists:`, `immune:`, `damage:`, `skill:`, `drops:`, `from:`, `needs:`, `gives:` and `spawns:`), each value matched anywhere in what it names, spaces left out, but for the words `is:` and the fight terms take, matched by the start of the word or of any part of it; a name in quotes matches only what is called exactly that, by the game's name or the prefab's, ignoring case, and a quote not yet closed reads as plain words while it is typed; commas part a value into several, any of which may match (`biome:swamp,plains`), and after a comma the next value is suggested. A search that finds nothing at all is tried once more with each word no name holds read as the word of the catalog's names one slip away from it, a letter wrong, missing, extra or two swapped, or whose start is, for a word still being typed, words of three letters or fewer left alone (`SearchSlip`); where that finds anything, the list shows it under a note naming what was typed and what is shown. The search's help lists an example of each term beside what it finds (`SearchHelp.Lines`, every key named, as a test holds); a click on an example tries it in the search. In the list the part of each name a typed word matched is lit behind the text, each word where the search finds it first and a quoted name whole (`SearchLit`, `ScryPanel.Lit.cs`), measured where the row's style puts the letters.

`is:` asks what a thing is in one word (`SearchFlags`), read into each entry once the catalog has every entry linked and grouped (`CatalogBuilder.Terms.cs`) as its details tell the same: a boss, tameable or flying creature by `Character.m_boss`, a `Tameable` and `Character.m_flying`; a wearable item, a weapon or ammo by its item type, food by its food values, craftable by an enabled recipe; a buildable piece by a tool that builds it; a dungeon, camp or room as the Locations list tags it (`PlaceGrouping.Is`), a location's once it is read, its flag then told again (`SearchFlags.WithPlace`); silent with nothing to see or hear, unsure where a mod was named by clues. The fight terms are read the same way (`SearchFight`): `weak:`, `resists:` and `immune:` from the cells of the resistance grid the details draw (a creature's, worn armour's while worn), so a creature's tool damage it takes none of, drawn quietly, is no immunity; `damage:` from the damage figures the details tell, a creature's from its attack items; `skill:` from the skill a weapon, ammo, shield or tool trains, as the details name it. The link terms follow what the details link, each linked thing named by the game's name or its prefab's (`Entry.TermLinks`): `from:` and `drops:` the lines an item's page tells where it comes from, by a creature's drops, a drop table or picking (not producers or traders). Three words of `is:` ask what the character met rather than what a thing is (`Search.MineWords`): `new` what it saw or came to know on the game's day it is now, as its book noted the moment, `seen` what it saw but does not know, `walked` a dungeon's room it walked and a dungeon holding one. They are taken out of the terms matched on the catalog's words (`Search.AsksMine`) and asked of the compendium (`CompendiumView.Is`), matched only whole, as `is:w` may mean a weapon, and with the compendium off they match nothing.

`needs:` the rows of its page saying what it is used to make, upgrade or build, or turned into; `gives:` what its page lists under *Given by*, a status effect's givers, which include what the game's own code gives, as no field names it (`CodeGivers`): a bed Rested on waking in it (`Player.SetSleeping`), a fire's heat Warm and Resting, as a seat by one does (`EffectArea.CustomFixedUpdate`, `Player.UpdateEnvStatusEffects`), and Resting Rested after its delay (`SE_Cozy.UpdateStatusEffect`), and the weather: a biome by each of its weathers and a raid or boss fight by the one it brings Wet where it is wet, outside without a roof, Freezing where it freezes away from fire and shelter and Cold with one of them, Cold where it is cold away from a fire, all of the time or at night (`EnvMan.CalculateWet`, `CalculateCold`, `CalculateFreezing`, read into links once the biomes are made: `CatalogBuilder.Weather.cs`); `spawns:` a thing's *Spawns* links and the creatures a raid brings. Suggestions come from an index of every value each key can take, built on a worker thread once the catalog is read, each with how many entries it finds. The best one shows faint after the text; Tab takes it, and further presses cycle through the rest.

Enter takes a suggestion while one is offered for a word being typed. Otherwise it does the selection's main thing, as a double click does: a sound or effect plays, a projectile flies, a status effect shows on the person, a creature makes its first attack, a wearable item is put on or taken off, a location or room plays its music or stops it, a raid rolls its creatures anew, and a tree, log, rock or piece is felled or broken. In the clip and effect filters Enter plays the first match. Escape steps back one thing at a time: out of a text box, a room picked let go of, out of a room gone into to its level, from a level opened back to the whole place with the roof on, and only then closes the panel (`ScryPanel.Escape`); Ctrl+F goes to the search.

Above the list the full view holds the search in one row, the list button and the way back and forward before it, its help, favourites and recent after it and the origin switch (all, the game's own, mods') at the row's end (`HeaderLayout.OriginSwitch`), and under it a tab for every kind with something, wrapping. The compact view keeps its header to three rows however many kinds the list holds, so the list keeps its room: the list button and the way back follow the name as drawn, a gap after it, joined as one group as the stage's chips are, back or forward faint where there is nowhere to go (`HeaderLayout.AfterName`, `HeaderLayout.JoinedWidth`), in the title row where the buttons at that row's right leave room for them, else they start the search row as in the full view; every button of the title row is as tall as the view button, and with the compendium on the eye that peeks stands beside it, as in the full view (`HeaderLayout.NavInTitleRow`); the search fills its row up to its help, favourites and recent (`HeaderLayout.CompactSearchRow`); and under it the kind shown stands as one pill, *Learned* after it while it holds news, and where the list is taken from as one chip at the row's end, a long pill giving way to them (`HeaderLayout.CompactKindRow`). A click on the pill opens every kind as the full view's tabs in a box over the list, a click on the chip the origin switch in a box under it; the open box takes a press over it before anything under it does, picks and closes, and closes on Escape, a press elsewhere or the full view (`ScryPanel.HeaderCompact.cs`).

Under the title row the compact view's notices share one strip rather than stacking (`NoticeLine`): of what of Scry is off, what the world's server sets, peeking, players whose games lack Scry and what was learned, in that order, the most pressing up shows first, drawn as its own strip is in the full view, and while more than one is up its place among them stands at the strip's end, "1 of 3 ›", a click going on to the next, round to the first; the one shown stays while it is up, the most pressing takes the strip once it goes, and one newly up that is more pressing than the one shown takes it at once, so a warning is never left behind a step. The self-test's own strip stands apart, over it.

IMGUI's text field uses up any key that types no character, so Enter is taken before the field is drawn. It leaves Tab alone, and an unused Tab moves Unity's keyboard focus on, so Tab is taken after it. The inventory opens on Tab without checking for text boxes, so while one of the panel's boxes has the keyboard Scry releases that key press first (`ZInput.ResetButtonStatus`), as the game does with a key it has handled.

## Input

While the panel is open, `TextInput.IsVisible` answers yes. The game already stops moving, looking, attacking and opening its menu while a text box is up, and frees the cursor. The camera zoom reads the wheel regardless, so `ZInput.Internal_GetMouseScrollWheel`, through which every wheel read of the game passes, answers nothing while the panel is open. The block lasts one frame past closing, so the Escape that closed the panel does not open the game's menu.

The game asks for mouse look apart from movement (`PlayerController.TakeInput`), so holding the right mouse button outside the panel answers only that question yes and captures the cursor as in play, in place of the game's own capture for each frame held (`GameCamera.UpdateMouseCapture`): with the panel open that one frees and shows the cursor, and captured again after it, it was shown in the middle of the window each frame. Locked, the cursor is kept in the middle of the window, so once the button is let go and the game frees it, it is put back where the look began (`PointerReturn`, `ZInput.SetMousePosition`). Walking is allowed whenever none of the panel's boxes has the keyboard, and `Player.SetControls` never receives attack, block or dodge while the panel is open. `WalkWhileOpen` and `LookWithRightMouse` turn these off.
