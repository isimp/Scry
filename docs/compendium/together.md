# Playing together

Events crossing between players' games, the hello, and who the character plays with.

Code: `src/Catalog/EventLink.cs`, `src/Model/TestEvents.cs`.

See also: [network](../network.md), [learning](learning.md), [sources](sources.md); all notes in [the index](../index.md).

## Events between games

Events cross between the players' games (`EventLink`). Each Scry says hello as it joins, with the version of its messages, and answers another's hello, so every Scry knows who plays with Scry (`ScryPeers`); a game of other messages counts as none and is told apart as needing an update. The game that runs an event sends it, as a message of its own (`EventWire`: a mark, the messages' version, the event), to the players who did it, wherever they are, and to those its game has within sight of it and a margin of 10 m, if their games have Scry (`EventRecipients`); where a player stands is read from the game's list of players and their characters' synced places (`EventLink.Players`), the same in a player's game as on a dedicated server, which makes characters only around the world's middle (it builds the world's objects around its reference place, which only a local player's spawn and moves set). Each takes it by the one rule as alone, at its own character's place as it arrives, and an event told twice counts once. A death and a pick carry a mark, the ragdoll's or the pickable's id (`WorldEvent.Mark`), because the players' own games tell the same events from their side without any message: a death another game ran is seen by the ragdoll it makes for every player, with the creature's level and drops on it (`DropWatch.RemainsMade`, `RemainsSeen`), and a character's own pick on a thing another game runs is told from its own game with what the thing gives (`YieldWatch.PickedHere`). A kill the game credits the character with (`Game.RPC_RegisterKill`, sent to every player flagged as an attacker) and the remains seen within five seconds of it, in either order, name the death the character's own (`KillCredits`, `CompendiumKeeper.KillCredited`), so a bow kill from afar counts its loot without the owning game's Scry. The two tellings of one mark are recorded once, the second adding only its pairs (`EventLedger.Marked`, `EventRule.Paired`); a marked event that concerned the character not at all leaves the other telling free to count. The messages are of their second kind since the mark (`ScryPeers.Messages`). The messages are routed, so a server without Scry passes them on all the same; an event is taken only from the game that ran it, and nothing the self-test feeds the hooks is sent. Who did a kill goes by the names the game flags the creature with, so a player who rejoins mid-fight, with a new session, is not told the kill.

## Who the character plays with

Who the character plays with is looked at each second, the players whose characters stand in the world (`EventLink.PlayingWith`, `ScryPeers.PlayingWith`): a player who said no hello counts as playing without Scry only once 10 s in the world, as the hello and the game's list of players travel apart, and a hello heard before its player shows in the list is kept; a player gone is forgotten, hello and all, but only once every player listed is accounted for: one rising again after a death is listed without a character for a while (`Game._RequestRespawn` clears it), so by no session, and whoever is missing may be them. With the compendium on, a notice under the header names those whose games lack Scry or need it updated (`PlayingWithWords.Notice`), as what runs in their games counts nothing for the character; its chip, *Who has Scry*, keeps the compendium's box open until the mouse has been on it and left, and its cross puts it away until another such player joins (`GapNotice`, forgotten with the world). The box lists everyone played with and whether each one's game has Scry, under the world's sources, a hint line under it saying what a game without Scry means (`PlayingWithWords.Row`). The self-test sends made-up events to the game running the world, which sends each back as its own a few times (`EventLink.AskEcho`, `Scry_EventEcho`, answered only by the world's game and only to the game asking): on a dedicated server running Scry they cross the network both ways, the server's hello heard first, and the test's book counts each by the one rule, once, one passed on as from a game that did not run it passed over; in a world the character's game runs, the echo goes through it alone, the network not covered, and with a server without Scry, or of older messages, the part is skipped.

The server's Scry also stands in for a friend's game (`EventLink.AskRun`, `Scry_EventRun`, `Scry_EventRan`): it runs made-up events as its own at places around the character, chooses who gets each by the same code a player's game uses, sends each only to the game asking, where the rules chose it, and then says what it ran and chose and where it had the character; the self-test checks that one 2 m away is sent and counts as seen, one 35 m away is sent within the margin but counts nothing, one 50 m away is not sent, and one 60 m away the character did is sent and counts. This game's own sending is checked against the server too (`EventLink.AskTaken`, `Scry_EventTakenAsk`, answered from the events taken lately, `TakenLately`): an event the server's game did, high above every player, goes to it alone and it says it took it; one no one did goes nowhere. The server runs only things the self-test makes up, whose names begin with its prefix, at most 8 at a time (`TestEvents`), and a made-up event taken reaches no book but the self-test's own, should one come late or reach another player (`TestEvents.Reaches`). Players coming and going are checked through the same reading of the world's players as play, made-up ones listed beside the real ones (`EventLink.AlsoListedForTest`): one whose hello came is played with at once and stays so while listed without a character, one without a hello is named after 10 s, one gone is forgotten. What only a friend's game shows is its own hooks firing on a thing it runs, as a kill both of you hit, and the game's list holding a second real player.
