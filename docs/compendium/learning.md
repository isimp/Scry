# How the compendium learns

The watchers that make a thing seen or known in play, the catch-up from what the game keeps of a character, and the explored map.

Code: `src/Model/ExploredGrid.cs`, `src/Catalog/CompendiumSight.cs`, `src/Patches/UseWatch.cs`, `src/Model/CompendiumCatchUp.cs`, `src/Model/CompendiumBook.cs`, `src/Catalog/CompendiumMap.cs`, `src/Catalog/CompendiumLink.cs`, `src/Catalog/CompendiumKeeper.cs`.

See also: [together](together.md), [showing](showing.md), [selftest](../operations/selftest.md); all notes in [the index](../index.md).

## Watched in play

Watchers learn in play (`CompendiumKeeper`, `CompendiumSight`, `HitWatched`, `HitByWatched`, `ResourceHitWatch`, `DropWatch`, `TogetherWatch`, `UseWatch`). A taming is seen where a creature seen wild in sight is seen tame within half a minute of the last sight of it (`TameWatch.OutOfSight`); what left the inventory on using a thing is paired with it only where the catalog says the thing takes it (`CompendiumKeeper.Takes`); a boss called by the character is seen with a line to its altar once the game running the altar answers that it calls it (`OfferingBowl.RPC_BossSpawnInitiated`), and a boss seen standing by an altar that calls its kind goes with the altar and its place for whoever stands by, with the offering where it stood on the altar's item stands. A creature is known once the character hits it or it hits the character, and a hit taken finds out the attack it came from by its own marks among the creature's weapons, whether it flew, its damage types and its status effect, as the game fills them from the weapon, else by the weapon the creature holds, as a projectile flies up to three seconds and the creature may hold another by then (`AttackOfHit`, `CompendiumKeeper.HitBy`). How a damage type lands on it is told by the character's own hits alone, on whichever game the creature runs (`Character.Damage` on the game dealing the hit, before it is sent to the creature's owner, `TogetherWatch.Hit`), within the popup's distance (`DamageText.m_maxTextDistance`) and by the creature's own modifiers, the page's values (a status effect's modifiers live on the owner alone); another player's hit tells nothing, as the game shows a bystander only a class; a weak spot only by the character's own hit. Anything in the world is seen once it is in the camera's view with nothing solid between. A creature is seen within 30 m for one of a person's size or smaller and as much farther as it is bigger, up to 150 m (`Sighting.Reach`, `Sighting.CreatureReach`, by `Character.GetHeight`), or within the world's seen reach where that is farther. Any other thing is seen within the world's seen reach, as the game names those only near, measured from the eye to its nearest part, as the game names a thing where the eye's ray meets it (`Player.FindHoverObject`), not by its middle, which a tree carries 11 to 20 m overhead, and looked at on its upright at the eye's height (`Sighting.ThingReach`, `Sighting.NearestSq`, `Sighting.Aim`, its box from its meshes, hidden ones among them). A place is seen within its radius and 30 m beyond, or as far as its height makes it out, as a rock spire 4 m round stands out far past its radius (`Sighting.PlaceReach`, its height measured once per kind from what it shows within its radius, `CompendiumSight.PlaceHeight`), looked for three quarters of the way up too where it stands tall. Each is paired with the biome and the place it stands in. Creatures are looked at once a second, and one near enough for the game to name it and mark its stars over it, within the world's seen reach (`WorldRules.SeenReach`, the game's 10 m unless the server sets otherwise), is seen up close: its level and what it carries, as the game shows them on it to every player (`VisEquipment` keeps each slot's item in the creature's data: the hands, chest, legs, head, shoulders, utility and trinket, `VisEquipment.UpdateEquipmentVisuals`), are kept with the book (`CompendiumSight.Carried`, `CompendiumKeeper.Sighted`), and under the strict rules the stage offers a creature only the stars it was seen with so and dresses it only in what it was seen carrying, a place's example and a raid's crowd standing their creatures no higher (`Explorer.StarsOffered`, `StarLevelFor`, `WearsShown`); a creature only seen opens on what sight gave, its stars and gear seen and the biomes and places it was seen in (`Explorer.SeenLines`), and its row in the list is faint, noted seen. A creature's attack begun within that reach, a boss's as much farther as the game shows a boss's health bar, and any as far as its creature uses it from (the attack item's `m_aiAttackRange`: a Seeker soldier's taunt from 20 m, a Gjall's from 50 m, which hurt no one and so are found out by sight alone) (`Sighting.AttackReach`, `EnemyHud.m_maxShowDistanceBoss`), in view with nothing solid between, is found out as taking its hit is: the game sends every player's game the animation trigger the attack's item names (`ZSyncAnimation.RPC_SetTrigger`, `CompendiumSight.SwingSeen`), a chain's later steps numbered, so a creature a friend's game runs counts too; only weapons take part, as the game attacks with nothing else (`Humanoid.EquipBestWeapon` skips what is no weapon, so a Dvergr mage's fire staff, a utility item, leaves the fireball's trigger to the fireball), and a trigger several weapons share is told by the weapon in the creature's hand, which the game equips before the attack begins and keeps in the creature's data for every game (`VisEquipment.SetRightItem`, `AttackTriggers`), read back by the hash of its name as the game draws it, from the item database first (`GamePrefabs.ItemHashed`), as a creature's own attack items, a Dvergr mage's summon and shield among them, are there alone: at once on the game running the creature, else half a second later, as the trigger is sent at once and changed data with the next send on each leg and a creature picks its weapon again only once its attack is over, or sooner by what the attack put beside its caster, an area with a network view of its own (the mage's shield) or the creatures a summon aimed at its caster places within its radius (the mage's Mistiles), within two seconds, after which it tells nothing (`SwingsPending`, `CompendiumSight.MakesOf`). A gear look waits only for the items of it that show on the body (`PrefabGear.Shows`), so a mage in a set stands in it once its robe and its staff were seen worn, the staff being its utility item, hung in its right hand. What a creature was seen wearing counts as met for its looks under either rules, as its own robe and staff can never be picked up and met as items (`Explorer.SeenWorn`), and a creature that rolls a set opens in the first gear look that can show, the bare one only where none can, as every one spawns in a set (`LookPick`), its look row offering its sets first and the bare look last (`LookPick.InRow`). What a creature's set puts on its body counts as seen with an item of the set no other set or list of the creature holds, as the game puts all of a set but its weapons on as the creature spawns and nothing takes it off (`Humanoid.GiveDefaultItems`), each item taking the place of the one before it in its slot, the hands and any slot its chance items may fill later left out: a mage seen in its fire robe held its fire staff, a Fuling shaman seen in its headdress its staff (`WornTogether`, `PrefabGear.WornSets`). Everything else the game keeps in the world, trees, logs, rocks, deposits, buildings, pickables, nests, items on the ground and the rest, is kept as it wakes (`ZNetView.Awake`) and looked at in rounds, a share each frame and all of them once a second, a frame giving it no more than 0.2 ms and owing the rest to the frames after (`Rounds`, `CompendiumSight.Sweep`), and seen only within the world's seen reach besides its size's (`Sighting.ThingReach`), as the game names an item, a piece or a resource only on hover; one is let go once seen, or once it is in reach and was seen before and paired with the biome and place it stands in, and, where it is worth finding again, once its spot is kept for Show on map, looked for within the world's map reach (`SeenOnMapReach`, `SeenOnMap.Reach`, `SeenSpotsKeeper.Saw`), as there is nothing more to learn of it there. One out of reach costs only where it stands. One that cannot move (the game syncs no moving place for it, physics moves no body in it, and it is no bird) rests while it is out of reach, out of view or hidden by more than a step of a quarter metre of the character or the eye, or a turn of the view of two degrees, could change: it keeps its turn but is passed over without a look until either moves that far, counted from where they were when that was last so (`Viewpoint`).

Standing still, the sight so looks only at what can move and at what wakes meanwhile; a thing uncovered while the character and the eye stay put, as behind a door opened or a tree felled, is seen once either moves. What shows nothing (a spawn point) and what the catalog lacks are passed over. An item is known once carried, and seen in a container opened, which is known itself. A place is known once the character stands within its radius as the game measures it (`Location.GetLocation`: its exterior radius, or the larger of its exterior and interior ones where it has an inside), but a dungeon, whose rooms come along with it, only once the character is inside it (`Character.InInterior`, above 3,000 m, where the game builds them); standing at its entrance it is seen, as any place is once its centre is in view within its radius and 30 m beyond and a line from the eye reaches it: to its middle or one of four points half way to its edge, a person's height over its ground, stopped by nothing solid or by something inside its own ground, its walls or the ground in it (`Sighting.PlacePoints`, `Sighting.PlaceInSight`), so a place behind a hill or a cliff is not seen. A room's page relates what its dungeon saw go together with it (`CompendiumView.Related`, through the room's label), as what is met inside is paired with the dungeon the character stands in, not the room. A dungeon only seen shows its entrance alone on the stage, with no switch to its inside, no plan and no floors inside to open, and while it is built the stage says only that its entrance is built, not how many rooms it has (`DungeonWords.Progress`); one with nothing outside, as the sealed tower, whose rooms are what is seen of it, shows them with the place's own floors alone (`Explorer.InsideKnown`, `Stage.InsideUnknown`). A status effect is known once it is on the character, a weather's (Wet, Cold, Freezing) seen together with the biome stood in while that biome's own weather gives it, or with the raid whose weather does (`EnvMan.IsWet`, `IsCold`, `IsFreezing`), the catch-up pairing no weather with a biome entered, a raid once the character is inside its area (`RandEventSystem.GetActiveEvent`), and seen together with the biome the character stands in where the raid counts it as its own (`RandomEvent.InEventBiome`, any biome for a raid naming none), a resource once hit, and so a piece the world placed, as the Dvergr's crates, lanterns, beams and walls, a goblin village's fences or the black marble of the Mistlands' ruins: one no player built (`Piece.GetCreator` 0) or with no building of its own (no `Piece`), while a piece a player built only tells how the hit landed, being learned by its recipe (`PieceHitWatch`, `WorldPiece`), and a rock struck whole by another hit once the pieces it alone breaks into are (`Destructible.m_spawnWhenDestroyed`, `CompendiumCatchUp.Hit`; pieces several rocks break into bring none along; a book kept before that rule knows the whole of pieces it knows as the character loads, caught up, `CompendiumCatchUp.WholesOfPieces`), a creature seen tamed near the character, wild at one look and tame at a later one (`Character.IsTamed`, looked at once a second for the tameable creatures within 30 m, `TameWatch`), or told tame by the game's message to the closest player within 30 m (`MessageHud.ShowMessage`, `TameMessage`), however long the taming took, or petted by it (`Tameable.Interact`), or seen come about tame within 30 m, born, hatched from an egg or grown up from a young one, the creature the game wakes last in that call (`DeedWatch`, `EventRule`, ways *seen born* and *seen grow up*), and a trader whose store is open (`StoreGui`), its wares on show seen; whatever the character points at is seen and paired with where it stands.

Whatever the character uses with success, pressing use on it or using an item on it (both end in `Humanoid.DoInteractAnimation`), is known, as a friend's portal, bed or ship shows what it does in use; and so are the uses the game answers false to, which never get there, where the game does them: sitting, lying down to sleep or taking a ship's helm attaches the character (`Player.AttachStart`), steering (`Player.StartDoodadControl`), a bed claimed (`Bed.IsCurrent` after its use), a portal gone through (`TeleportWorld.Teleport`, the character then teleporting) and a ladder climbed (`Ladder.Interact` moving the character); creatures and items keep their own ways (`UseWatch`, `CompendiumCatchUp.Used`). An item whose tooltip card the game shows, in the inventory, a chest or a trader's store (`UITooltip.OnHoverStart` with the item's name token as its topic and a text under it), or whose card the crafting menu's recipe panel shows (`InventoryGui.UpdateRecipe`, noted as the recipe shown changes, `UseWatch.RecipeShown`), is known. A piece's card in the hammer's build menu adds nothing: the menu lists only pieces unlocked, which are known already. A pickable that grows back, seen bearing again after it was picked, by anyone, shows how long growing back takes, as one grows back while its ground loads, mostly before the player comes near; the game keeps when it was last picked after it bears again (`Pickable.SetPicked`, `Regrowth`), and the sight keeps looking at such a pickable until its growing back is found out. Food on a cooking station seen turning done or burnt, within 30 m and in sight, as every game draws the station's slots once a second (`CookingStation.UpdateCooking`, `CookingSlots`), shows how long cooking takes and pairs the station with what it gives, cooked or left too long, whoever's game runs it; food done before the station was first looked at, or while it was out of sight, was not seen turning. A piece the game lets stand only on or by another (`Piece.m_mustConnectTo`, a sap collector on a root) is paired, when the character places one, with the one in its reach, found as the game's placing finds it. A root a sap tap draws from, which takes no hit and no use, is known by tapping it: by a tap the character builds on it or takes sap from, the root found as the game finds a tap's root, the first with a part within 0.2 m of the tap (`SapCollector.UpdateTick`, `CompendiumKeeper.TappedAt`, way *tapped*); the tapping also finds out what the root holds and how fast it refills, and the sap each line the game shows on it stands for, which its page keeps back until then (`Deed.Tapped`, `RootWords`). A thing that breaks, destroyed by a hit, is told as broken whatever it gave (`Destructible.Destroy`), so a deposit's shell giving way to its vein shows its own health, its loot told by what comes out as for any break. Once the character fed an input into a kind of station, holding the product that kind makes of it, wherever and whenever it came by it, pairs the station with the product, given by it, and the input with the product (`FedThenHeld`): what comes into its hands is looked at as it comes, and all it holds again after each feeding. A fish the character lands is counted as one catch with what it brought besides itself, which the game puts straight into the inventory, each of its extra drops (`Fish.m_extraDrops`) held more of than before the catch seen together with it. A fish landed is told to the players near by as landed by its fisher, with the stars it was caught with (`FishingFloat.Catch`, `Happened.Caught`), so one watched landed within 30 m and in sight shows how it fights, as a kill watched shows its loot; the bait it took is the fisher's alone to see. A treasure lying in the world that the character picks up (`PickableItem.Interact`) is seen together with the item it gives, which every game reads from the treasure's own data. Riding a creature also shows its health, as the game prints it for the one ridden (`Explorer.KeepsBack`). Riding a creature finds out its stamina and speed when ridden, which the strict rules keep back until then (`Sadle`, the player's controller, looked at once a second). Reading a Vegvisir or a rune stone keeps the places it marks on the map as found for *Places marked for you*, shown through that row, never written into the book, and pairs each with the stone and with the place the stone stands in (found where it stands, as a stone with no network view of its own sits under its place's proxy). What the character carries is looked at once a second, and what it takes is held where the game takes it, picked up (`Humanoid.Pickup`) or moved out of a container towards its inventory (`Inventory.MoveAll`, `Inventory.MoveItemToThis`), as a mod may store it in an inventory of its own, a backpack's, which neither that look nor the game's own known items see (`Player.OnInventoryChanged`; `TakeWatch`). The book is copied as it stands when it is saved and its text worked out on a worker thread (`CompendiumBook.ToSave`). A known dungeon makes all its rooms known.

## Caught up from the game's records

A character played before Scry is caught up from what the game keeps of it (`CharacterRecords`, `CompendiumCatchUp`): items picked up, crafted or eaten, and the resources they come from; recipes and pieces unlocked, stations used and pieces placed, a piece the catalog keeps as another kind, as a stone placed as a piece is also a pickable, known by the name it is placed by, but never an item of that name; biomes entered; trophies held and the creatures they fall from that live in a biome entered (not the game's own TheHive, placed nowhere, which drops the drake's trophy too); kinds killed, counted by the name a creature shows, which several can share, as both Dvergr rogues do: of those, the ones the character met in play, come to know some way other than such a kill or paired with a biome it stood in (`CompendiumCatchUp.MetInPlay`); where it met none, the ones living in a biome entered; where none does, the ones the world brings about, living in a biome or spawned by something that is, so never the bosses' aspects, which nothing in the game spawns though the Elder's aspect summons roots going by its roots' name (`CompendiumCatchUp.Comes`); and all of them where none comes about either (the frozen greydwarf, living only in the Deep North, shows the greydwarf's name). Their health shows for all of them as the character loads, unless one of them was seen killed already, which tells which it was; in play the death itself names the creature killed and shows its health alone (`EventRule`), so a kill counted then under a name several share shows none of theirs, and a kill once counted for one it no longer stands for is taken back where nothing else made that creature known: neither the records now nor meeting it in play, which keeps a way it came to be known other than such a kill or pairs it with the biome it stood in (`CompendiumBook.TakeBack`); things picked; and the guardian power chosen at its stone are known. The game counts picks by the item, honey and sap by what was taken and catches by the fish's own name (`IncrementStatPickable`, `BeesHarvested`, `SapHarvested`), so where one kind of thing alone gives an item by picking it is paired with that item, where the game has one kind of beehive or sap collector a take shows its pace, food taken off a kind of cooking station, done or burnt (the game counts food another player took off a station this game ran as made by this game's character, which the book notes apart, `CompendiumBook.CreditedApart`, so an item counted only so is none of its making), as the game counts it by the station's own counts (`CookingStation.m_cookStat`, `m_burntStat`), shows how long its cooking takes, and a fish caught shows how it fights (`CharacterRecords.Kinds`). A book kept before catches were noted as such holds them as each fish paired with the bait it took, so as it is read a fish paired with a bait it bites counts as caught (`CompendiumBook.ProveCaughtByBaits`); a fish a tame creature ate is paired the same way and is no catch. The game's known materials hold everything ever in the inventory and also what a trader offered (`Trader.DiscoverItems`), so an item there is known unless a trader sells it, which stays seen. An item whose recipe is unlocked is known, as the crafting menu shows it whole. The game keeps each biome entered by its name as shown in the language played in then, an area's prefix before it (`BiomeSector.GetName`), so a biome counts where its shown name stands in a kept area as whole words: in the player's language first, and for an area kept while the game ran in another, in every language the game ships.

Those names are read from the game's own language tables, every language in each, as `Localization.LoadCSV` reads them (`LanguageTable`), once and only when an area needs them. A name standing inside a longer one found is part of it (the Black Forest in Bulgarian, "Черна Гора", holds the Mountain in Russian, "Гора"), and an area whose words still name more than one biome is left unplaced. A biome a mod adds has no names there, so an area of it kept in another language stays unplaced. What gives an item so known, itself or through what it leaves behind (a deposit's fractured copy, a tree's log), counts only where it grows in a biome the character entered: the game drops Wood from some 84 kinds of thing and Stone from 68, across every biome. A status effect is known with what gives it where the records make that known, as a bed's Rested. The records are read again whenever they grow or the power chosen changes, and what they tell anew is learned as news.

## The explored map

The explored map catches a character up once per world each session (`CompendiumMap`, `MapCatchUp`, `ExploredGrid`): the kinds of place lying in ground the character explored itself, and the biomes under it (`MapCatchUp.Biomes`, `MapCatchUp.ApplyBiomes`, shown seen at most), are kept as found in its own ground (`CompendiumBook.FoundIn`, *Ground you explored*), never written into the book, as the game explores a ring of 100 m around the player whatever it saw (`Minimap.Explore`, `m_exploreRadius`), by cells of the map's own pixels, about 12 m (`ExploredGrid.PlaceCellMetres`), so a place counts only where the ground at it was uncovered; a map shared at a cartography table does not count. The grid is held and sent as a bit a cell, its cells' size in every packet, which the server reads, so a server's older Scry answers 12 m cells and a client's older Scry's 48 m ones alike; packed, the whole map is 512 KB before the game's compression. Once caught up, the map is looked at once a minute on a worker thread for the ground explored since the last ask, and where at least 25 new cells are (`MapGrowth`), that ground alone is asked about, its places found as they are uncovered (`ExploredGrid.NewSince`, `CompendiumLink.AskMore`). A book that took places off the map while the map wrote them as seen gives them back once as it is read (`CompendiumBook.GiveBackMapPlaces`), and one that took biomes so gives those back the same way (`CompendiumBook.GiveBackMapBiomes`): a place whose only news is the map and which was never sighted leaves the book and *Learned*, and its worlds are read again. The biomes under that ground are written as seen, worked out once per world each session on a worker thread from a point in each explored cell of four pixels, about 48 m (`ExploredGrid.BiomeCellMetres`), as the game's heightmap builder asks the world generator from its own thread. A game that is the world's server, alone or hosting, knows where every place is (`ZoneSystem.m_locationInstances`). A client of a dedicated server sends its explored map, packed, to the server's Scry, which answers with the kinds of place lying in it and never where they are, unless the server tells places as dots (`PlacesOnMap`, `PlacesToldBy`) (`CompendiumLink`).

Whether the server runs Scry is known from its hello (`EventLink.Server`, `ServerScries`): the client asks only once it was heard, and a server not heard within 10 s after this game's hello runs none, so the client gives up on that world for the session at once rather than waiting, the ground from shared maps and the ground's dungeons not asked either; an answer lost is given up on after 30 s. The compendium's box names the server first among those played with ("The server · Scry"), and for one without Scry its faint line says what that leaves out (`PlayingWithWords.Row`, `Hint`). Each answer on a ground tells, after its kinds, how many of each lie there (`MapCatchUp.Counted`), which a client's older Scry leaves unread; held for the session (`CompendiumMap.Counts`, `PlaceCounts`), a whole answer replacing a ground's counts and one on the ground new since adding to them. The world's census, how many of each kind of place the world holds, planned or built, never where (`MapCatchUp.Census`), goes by messages of its own (`Scry_CensusAsk`, `Scry_CensusAnswer`, taken only from the world's server), asked once a session while *The whole world* shows it, or read here where this game runs the world. A place's page then says under its title how many this world holds, how many lie in ground you explored, and in shared maps while that row shows anything ("This world holds 14 · 3 lie in ground you explored · 1 in shared maps", `CensusWords.Line`); without the census, only the grounds' counts. *Read the map again* above the Locations list and `/scry map` read it anew.
