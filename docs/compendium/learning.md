# How the compendium learns

The watchers that make a thing seen or known in play, the catch-up from what the game keeps of a character, and the explored map.

Code: `src/Model/ExploredGrid.cs`, `src/Catalog/CompendiumSight.cs`, `src/Patches/UseWatch.cs`, `src/Model/CompendiumCatchUp.cs`, `src/Model/CompendiumBook.cs`, `src/Catalog/CompendiumMap.cs`, `src/Catalog/CompendiumLink.cs`, `src/Catalog/CompendiumKeeper.cs`.

See also: [together](together.md), [showing](showing.md), [selftest](../operations/selftest.md); all notes in [the index](../index.md).

## Watched in play

Watchers learn in play (`CompendiumKeeper`, `CompendiumSight`, `FoughtWatch`, `ResourceHitWatch`, `DropWatch`, `TogetherWatch`, `UseWatch`). A taming is seen where a creature seen wild in sight is seen tame within half a minute of the last sight of it (`TameWatch.OutOfSight`); what left the inventory on using a thing is paired with it only where the catalog says the thing takes it (`CompendiumKeeper.Takes`); a boss called is seen with a line to its altar. A creature is known once the character hits it or it hits the character. How a damage type lands on it is told by the character's own hits alone, on whichever game the creature runs (`Character.Damage` on the game dealing the hit, before it is sent to the creature's owner, `TogetherWatch.Hit`), within the popup's distance (`DamageText.m_maxTextDistance`) and by the creature's own modifiers, the page's values (a status effect's modifiers live on the owner alone); another player's hit tells nothing, as the game shows a bystander only a class; a weak spot only by the character's own hit. Anything in the world is seen by one rule: once it is in the camera's view with nothing solid between, within 30 m for one of a person's size or smaller and as much farther as it is bigger, up to 150 m (`Sighting.Reach`, by `Character.GetHeight` for a creature and by its meshes for anything else, hidden ones among them), a creature or a thing within the world's seen reach besides, as the game names those only near (`Sighting.ThingReach`, `WorldRules.SeenReach`), a place by its radius and 30 m beyond; and it is paired with the biome and the place it stands in. Creatures are looked at once a second, and one near enough for the game to name it and mark its stars over it, within the world's seen reach (`WorldRules.SeenReach`, the game's 10 m unless the server sets otherwise), is seen up close: its level and what it carries, as the game shows them on it to every player (`VisEquipment` keeps each slot's item in the creature's data), are kept with the book (`CompendiumSight.Carried`, `CompendiumKeeper.Sighted`), and under the strict rules the stage offers a creature only the stars it was seen with so and dresses it only in what it was seen carrying, a place's example and a raid's crowd standing their creatures no higher (`Explorer.StarsOffered`, `StarLevelFor`, `WearsShown`); a creature only seen opens on what sight gave, its stars and gear seen and the biomes and places it was seen in (`Explorer.SeenLines`), and its row in the list is faint, noted seen. A creature's attack begun within that reach, in view with nothing solid between, is found out as taking its hit is: the game sends every player's game the animation trigger the attack's item names (`ZSyncAnimation.RPC_SetTrigger`, `CompendiumSight.SwingSeen`), a chain's later steps numbered, so a creature a friend's game runs counts too; a trigger two of the creature's items share tells neither (`AttackTriggers`). Everything else the game keeps in the world, trees, logs, rocks, deposits, buildings, pickables, nests, items on the ground and the rest, is kept as it wakes (`ZNetView.Awake`) and looked at in rounds, a share each frame and all of them once a second, a frame giving it no more than 0.2 ms and owing the rest to the frames after (`Rounds`, `CompendiumSight.Sweep`), and seen only within the world's seen reach besides its size's (`Sighting.ThingReach`), as the game names an item, a piece or a resource only on hover; one is let go once seen, or once it is in reach and was seen before and paired with the biome and place it stands in, as there is nothing more to learn of it there. One out of reach costs only where it stands. One that cannot move (the game syncs no moving place for it, physics moves no body in it, and it is no bird) rests while it is out of reach, out of view or hidden by more than a step of a quarter metre of the character or the eye, or a turn of the view of two degrees, could change: it keeps its turn but is passed over without a look until either moves that far, counted from where they were when that was last so (`Viewpoint`).

Standing still, the sight so looks only at what can move and at what wakes meanwhile; a thing uncovered while the character and the eye stay put, as behind a door opened or a tree felled, is seen once either moves. What shows nothing (a spawn point) and what the catalog lacks are passed over. An item is known once carried, and seen in a container opened, which is known itself. A place is known once the character stands within its radius as the game measures it (`Location.GetLocation`: its exterior radius, or the larger of its exterior and interior ones where it has an inside), but a dungeon, whose rooms come along with it, only once the character is inside it (`Character.InInterior`, above 3,000 m, where the game builds them); standing at its entrance it is seen, as any place is once its centre is in view within its radius and 30 m beyond and a line from the eye reaches it: to its middle or one of four points half way to its edge, a person's height over its ground, stopped by nothing solid or by something inside its own ground, its walls or the ground in it (`Sighting.PlacePoints`, `Sighting.PlaceInSight`), so a place behind a hill or a cliff is not seen. A dungeon only seen shows its entrance alone on the stage, with no switch to its inside, no plan and no floors inside to open, and while it is built the stage says only that its entrance is built, not how many rooms it has (`DungeonWords.Progress`); one with nothing outside, as the sealed tower, whose rooms are what is seen of it, shows them with the place's own floors alone (`Explorer.InsideKnown`, `Stage.InsideUnknown`). A status effect is known once it is on the character, a raid once the character is inside its area (`RandEventSystem.GetActiveEvent`), a resource once hit, a creature seen tamed near the character, wild at one look and tame at a later one (`Character.IsTamed`, looked at once a second for the tameable creatures within 30 m, `TameWatch`), or petted by it (`Tameable.Interact`), or seen come about tame within 30 m, born, hatched from an egg or grown up from a young one, the creature the game wakes last in that call (`DeedWatch`, `EventRule`, ways *seen born* and *seen grow up*), and a trader whose store is open (`StoreGui`), its wares on show seen; whatever the character points at is seen and paired with where it stands.

Whatever the character uses with success, pressing use on it or using an item on it (both end in `Humanoid.DoInteractAnimation`), is known, as a friend's portal, bed or ship shows what it does in use; creatures and items keep their own ways (`UseWatch`, `CompendiumCatchUp.Used`). An item whose tooltip card the game shows, in the inventory, a chest or a trader's store (`UITooltip.OnHoverStart` with the item's name token as its topic and a text under it), or whose card the crafting menu's recipe panel shows (`InventoryGui.UpdateRecipe`, noted as the recipe shown changes, `UseWatch.RecipeShown`), is known. A piece's card in the hammer's build menu adds nothing: the menu lists only pieces unlocked, which are known already. Riding a creature also shows its health, as the game prints it for the one ridden (`Explorer.KeepsBack`). Riding a creature finds out its stamina and speed when ridden, which the strict rules keep back until then (`Sadle`, the player's controller, looked at once a second). Reading a Vegvisir or a rune stone keeps the places it marks on the map as found for *Places marked for you*, shown through that row, never written into the book. What the character carries is looked at once a second. The book is copied as it stands when it is saved and its text worked out on a worker thread (`CompendiumBook.ToSave`). A known dungeon makes all its rooms known.

## Caught up from the game's records

A character played before Scry is caught up from what the game keeps of it (`CharacterRecords`, `CompendiumCatchUp`): items picked up, crafted or eaten, and the resources they come from; recipes and pieces unlocked, stations used and pieces placed; biomes entered; trophies held and the creatures they fall from that live in a biome entered (not the game's own TheHive, placed nowhere, which drops the drake's trophy too); kinds killed, counted by the name a creature shows, which several can share: of those, the ones living in a biome entered; where none does, the ones the world brings about, living in a biome or spawned by something that is, so never the bosses' aspects, which nothing in the game spawns though the Elder's aspect summons roots going by its roots' name (`CompendiumCatchUp.Comes`); and all of them where none comes about either (the frozen greydwarf, living only in the Deep North, shows the greydwarf's name), and a kill once counted for one it no longer stands for is taken back where nothing else made that creature known: neither the records now nor meeting it in play, which keeps the way it came to be known or pairs it with the biome it stood in (`CompendiumBook.TakeBack`); things picked; and the guardian power chosen at its stone are known. The game's known materials hold everything ever in the inventory and also what a trader offered (`Trader.DiscoverItems`), so an item there is known unless a trader sells it, which stays seen. An item whose recipe is unlocked is known, as the crafting menu shows it whole. The game keeps each biome entered by its name as shown in the language played in then, an area's prefix before it (`BiomeSector.GetName`), so a biome counts where its shown name stands in a kept area as whole words: in the player's language first, and for an area kept while the game ran in another, in every language the game ships.

Those names are read from the game's own language tables, every language in each, as `Localization.LoadCSV` reads them (`LanguageTable`), once and only when an area needs them. A name standing inside a longer one found is part of it (the Black Forest in Bulgarian, "Черна Гора", holds the Mountain in Russian, "Гора"), and an area whose words still name more than one biome is left unplaced. A biome a mod adds has no names there, so an area of it kept in another language stays unplaced. What gives an item so known, itself or through what it leaves behind (a deposit's fractured copy, a tree's log), counts only where it grows in a biome the character entered: the game drops Wood from some 84 kinds of thing and Stone from 68, across every biome. A status effect is known with what gives it where the records make that known, as a bed's Rested. The records are read again whenever they grow or the power chosen changes, and what they tell anew is learned as news.

## The explored map

The explored map catches a character up once per world each session (`CompendiumMap`, `MapCatchUp`, `ExploredGrid`): the kinds of place lying in ground the character explored itself, and the biomes under it (`MapCatchUp.Biomes`, `MapCatchUp.ApplyBiomes`, shown seen at most), are kept as found in its own ground (`CompendiumBook.FoundIn`, *Ground you explored*), never written into the book, as the game explores a ring of 100 m around the player whatever it saw (`Minimap.Explore`, `m_exploreRadius`), by cells of the map's own pixels, about 12 m (`ExploredGrid.PlaceCellMetres`), so a place counts only where the ground at it was uncovered; a map shared at a cartography table does not count. The grid is held and sent as a bit a cell, its cells' size in every packet, which the server reads, so a server's older Scry answers 12 m cells and a client's older Scry's 48 m ones alike; packed, the whole map is 512 KB before the game's compression. Once caught up, the map is looked at once a minute on a worker thread for the ground explored since the last ask, and where at least 25 new cells are (`MapGrowth`), that ground alone is asked about, its places found as they are uncovered (`ExploredGrid.NewSince`, `CompendiumLink.AskMore`). A book that took places off the map while the map wrote them as seen gives them back once as it is read (`CompendiumBook.GiveBackMapPlaces`), and one that took biomes so gives those back the same way (`CompendiumBook.GiveBackMapBiomes`): a place whose only news is the map and which was never sighted leaves the book and *Learned*, and its worlds are read again. The biomes under that ground are written as seen, worked out once per world each session on a worker thread from a point in each explored cell of four pixels, about 48 m (`ExploredGrid.BiomeCellMetres`), as the game's heightmap builder asks the world generator from its own thread. A game that is the world's server, alone or hosting, knows where every place is (`ZoneSystem.m_locationInstances`). A client of a dedicated server sends its explored map, packed, to the server's Scry, which answers with the kinds of place lying in it and never where they are (`CompendiumLink`).

Whether the server runs Scry is known from its hello (`EventLink.Server`, `ServerScries`): the client asks only once it was heard, and a server not heard within 10 s after this game's hello runs none, so the client gives up on that world for the session at once rather than waiting, the ground from shared maps and the ground's dungeons not asked either; an answer lost is given up on after 30 s. The compendium's box names the server first among those played with ("The server · Scry"), and for one without Scry its faint line says what that leaves out (`PlayingWithWords.Row`, `Hint`). Each answer on a ground tells, after its kinds, how many of each lie there (`MapCatchUp.Counted`), which a client's older Scry leaves unread; held for the session (`CompendiumMap.Counts`, `PlaceCounts`), a whole answer replacing a ground's counts and one on the ground new since adding to them. The world's census, how many of each kind of place the world holds, planned or built, never where (`MapCatchUp.Census`), goes by messages of its own (`Scry_CensusAsk`, `Scry_CensusAnswer`, taken only from the world's server), asked once a session while *The whole world* shows it, or read here where this game runs the world. A place's page then says under its title how many this world holds, how many lie in ground you explored, and in shared maps while that row shows anything ("This world holds 14 · 3 lie in ground you explored · 1 in shared maps", `CensusWords.Line`); without the census, only the grounds' counts. *Look at the map again* above the Locations list and `/scry map` read it anew.
