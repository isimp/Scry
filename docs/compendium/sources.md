# What a world shows as known, and the server's rules

The sources of the world (explored ground, shared maps, marked places, places on every map, the whole world), their levels and pins, and what the world's server allows.

Code: `src/Model/WorldRules.cs`, `src/Model/CompendiumView.cs`, `src/Model/CompendiumBook.cs`, `src/Model/CompendiumWords.cs`, `src/Model/Explorer.cs`, `src/Model/WorldSources.cs`, `src/UI/Skin.cs`, `src/Catalog/CompendiumLink.cs`, `src/Model/BookColumns.cs`.

See also: [showing](showing.md), [server](../server.md), [network](../network.md); all notes in [the index](../index.md).

## What a world shows as known

What a world shows as known is the player's choice, source by source, in the compendium's box while it is on (`WorldSource`, `SourceLevel`, `CompendiumBook.Shows`): *Ground you explored*, the kinds of place the explored map's answer named, starting at *Seen* and, as nothing of it is written into the book, offered *Off* as well (`WorldSources.Starts`, `WorldSources.Least`); *Shared maps*, the kinds lying in the ground the character took from maps read at a cartography table, which the game keeps apart from its own (`Minimap.m_exploredOthers`), asked about once a session and again after a map is read or written at a table (`MapTable.OnRead`, which the game answers false to every time, so the hook goes by its read: no item held out), by messages of their own so a server whose Scry knows only the first still answers it (`CompendiumLink.AskShared`); *Places marked for you*, by a Vegvisir or a rune stone, both of which mark the nearest place of a kind (`Game.DiscoverClosestLocation`); and *Places on every map*, the places with a map icon the game tells every client of (`ZoneSystem.GetLocationIcons`), looked at every half minute; and *The whole world*, every kind of place the world's census names (above), offered only *Off* or *Counted*: counted, each is heard of, a name and no more, marked as the census's ("Heard of from this world's census", `CompendiumWords.ByWorld`), pages say how many this world holds, and the Locations tab counts against the places the world holds and the rooms of their dungeons (`PlaceCensus.Held`, `Explorer.WorldHolds`; its tip says of how many the game and its mods can place, `CensusWords.TabTip`).

A level a source does not offer, as written by hand in the book, is read as its least (`WorldSources.Allows`). The book keeps the kinds each source found per world (`CompendiumBook.FoundIn`, lines `place` and `shows`). In the box each mark stands where it is chosen: the stairs after *Known up to*, the folded map at the start of the sources' hint line in place of the compass ("What these show is marked with this on its row and page; ..."). The two grounds, the character's own and from shared maps, have a pin at the end of their rows, the big map's dungeon mark drawn in the accent while on and faint while off, its tip saying what it does and whether it is on (`CompendiumWords.PinTip`); off until turned on, it is kept in the book (lines `marks`, `CompendiumBook.MarkDungeons`) and marks while its row shows anything (`MarksDungeonsNow`; the marks themselves are under the large map, above). Each source shows its places *Off*, *Seen*, *Known* or *With insides*; the character's own ground and the places marked for it are written as seen as they come, as before, so they offer no *Off*, and the other two start off (`WorldSources.Least`). *With insides* is dimmed on a row none of whose places found in this world is a dungeon lying inside, its tip saying which would (`WorldSources.HasInsides`, `CompendiumWords.NoInsides`); while no place is read yet to tell, it is offered.

The box stands its rows in columns after their captions (`BookColumns`): each level at one place down the box, a row without one leaving its cell empty, *Counted* across *Seen* and *Known*, the rules and the floor in the same columns, each column as wide as the widest that stands in it drawn lit, so the one chosen is never clipped, and every ground's pin in a column of its own after them; a level that cannot be chosen keeps its box, faint. The choice is laid over the book as the floor is, never written into it (`CompendiumView.ShowFromWorld`): the strongest source first, a place shown known bringing along what a known place does, with insides what anyone on this server walked inside the source's dungeons (`CompendiumView.WalkedOnServer`, `ServerWalks`, gathered by `SourceWalks`): a ground's from the dungeons lying in it as the server answered, the walks on them in; a source of single places from the server's records of its kinds of dungeon; the character's own walks added to each, all of them to its own ground. A dungeon shown so brings, under the strict rules too, the kinds of room walked there. What shows only so is the source's (`CompendiumView.ByWorld`), marked with a folded map (`Skin.FoldedMap`) on its row, under its title and after each chip and link going to it, where the floor's things carry its stairs (`Skin.Steps`; `ScryPanel.ChoiceMark`), with a tip and a line under the page's title saying from where and as what (`CompendiumWords.ByWorld`), the You box saying "shown as known from" the source; it shows whole under either rules (`CompendiumView.Whole`), a dungeon with insides opening the server's record of it as the floor does (`InsidesShown`); and met for real it is the book's own.

Turning a source down takes back all it showed. A room walked there names all it holds on its page and card, met or not; its place names what those rooms hold (`CompendiumView.HeldByWalks`), and all it holds once one of its dungeons there was walked through whole, its end caps and dividers left out, a room of a kind not read counted as one; its example on the stage stands every creature so named (`CompendiumView.HoldsNamed`, passed over by `Explorer.LeavesOut` and `NotMet`); nothing of it becomes seen anywhere else, so a name never met stays plain text rather than a link (`Explorer.Reaches`), and a skill not learned is named nowhere still.

## What the server allows

What the world's server allows the compendium is set in Scry's config, section *4 - World*, synced to every player by blaxxun's ServerSync, compiled in unchanged (`src/ServerSync/`, its README saying what it does and needs): set on the server, its values hold for every player connected to it, each player's own kept on disk beneath; `ModRequired` is off, so players without Scry join as before, and the entries are locked, so only an admin changes them in game. `Rules` sets Off, Loose or Strict for everyone, `PlayersChoose` (as it starts) leaving them to each player; `Peeking` and `KnownUpTo` allow those or not; `<source>UpTo` caps how far each source may show, never below what it shows at least (`WorldRules`, `Settings.World`). The book keeps the player's own choices and plays under the world's (`CompendiumBook.Under`): `Rule`, `Floor` and `Shows` are the choices within what the world allows, `ChosenRule`, `ChosenFloor` and `Chosen` the choices themselves, which alone are kept; the keeper puts the book under the world's rules once a second (`CompendiumKeeper`), and a peek ends where none is allowed. The compendium's box shows what the server sets locked: a padlock after *Rules* and *Known up to* (`Skin.Lock`), the rules it does not set dimmed, the floor's arrows and the peek's eye dimmed, a source's levels above its cap dimmed, each tip saying so ("This server sets the rules: Strict. An admin changes it in the server's Scry config.", `WorldRulesWords`). Joining a world whose server sets any of it, a notice under the header says what in one line, as the box's locks alone are easy to miss (`WorldRulesWords.Notice`, `ScryPanel.RulesNotice`): "This server plays Strict; peeking and Known up to are off." *Show the rules* opens the compendium's guide, and either it or the cross keeps the notice told in the book, by the world and the line, so it is told again only when the server's rules change (`CompendiumBook.ToldRules`). It is an agreement among friends, as with every client mod: a changed copy of Scry ignores it. Where this game runs the world, its own settings are the world's for those joining it. The standards compile the vendored file as a library of its own beside Scry's source and read none of it (`ScrySource`); the build needs .NET's compression library, TextMeshPro (a reference stub in `lib/`) and nullable annotations, as its README lists.
