# The compendium: the book, tiers and cost

What the compendium is, the book each character keeps, the tiers and the floor (Known up to), and what it costs a frame.

Code: `src/Model/CompendiumBook.cs`, `src/Model/CompendiumView.cs`, `src/Model/Explorer.cs`, `src/UI/ScryPanel.Settings.cs`, `src/Model/SettingsWords.cs`, `src/Catalog/CompendiumKeeper.cs`, `src/Catalog/CompendiumTiers.cs`, `src/Model/ButtonSize.cs`, `src/Patches/DeathWatch.cs`, `src/Model/Moment.cs`.

See also: [learning](learning.md), [showing](showing.md), [sources](sources.md); all notes in [the index](../index.md).

## The compendium

With the compendium on, the panel shows only what the character playing has come to know (`CompendiumView`, `Explorer.Know`). Its rules are chosen in a box under the *Compendium* button in the title row, which names the rules chosen and opens its box while the mouse is on it, as *Clear world* opens its list, as wide as its label drawn bold while the box is open, so it neither clips nor changes width (`ButtonSize`): *Off*, *Loose* or *Strict* (`CompendiumRule`), the floor under them while it is on, and the eye that peeks beside the button while it is on, and kept in the character's own book rather than in the settings, so a profile's sync never carries one player's choice to another; a new character starts *Off*. What a character learns is collected whatever the rules, so turning the compendium on later shows everything met meanwhile.

A character whose compendium is off is asked once, in the list's place, whether to keep one: *Loose*, *Strict* or *Not now*, each with a line on what it means (`ScryPanel.WelcomeCard`). Any answer, or rules chosen in the box, keeps it asked (`CompendiumBook.Welcomed`), and none is asked where the world's server sets the rules (`CompendiumBook.WelcomeDue`) or while the self-test's strip shows. The *?* in the compendium's box opens its guide in the list's place: a few paragraphs on the rules, *Known up to*, peeking, *Learned* and *You*, what a world shows as known, the pins and playing together (`SettingsWords.Guide`).

## The book

Each character's book (`CompendiumBook`) holds a level for each entry it met, by key, heard of, seen or known, only ever rising; the pairs it saw go together (`saw` lines, `CompendiumBook.Witness`) and, kept apart from them, the proofs of what it saw done to a thing (`did` lines, `CompendiumBook.Prove`: a deed, a damage type a hit told, a weak spot found, an attack taken by its weapon's key, `EntryKeys.Attack`), so a rule about what goes together never reads a proof and a rule about numbers never reads a pair; a book kept before held proofs among its pairs and reads them apart, and counts a creature paired with a weapon as that attack taken, as nothing can tell such a pair from any other, saying so in an `attacksaspairs` line, told from a book kept since by the `format` line every save begins with; what it saw of each creature up close, the stars it had and what it carried (`sight` lines, `CompendiumBook.Sighted`, `StarsSeen`, `WornSeen`); everything it learned, newest first, with the game day of each thing's latest news, and apart from it what it met, seen or known, with the day of meeting (`met` lines, `CompendiumBook.MetLately`; a book kept before has its met list in the order of the learned list, a thing whose latest news was found out with no day); its rules and floor; the worlds whose explored map it was caught up from; how and at which moment each thing came to be known in play (`CameBy`, `CompendiumBook.Came`), kept once, as a thing is come to know once, by the way each watcher or record tells it, and none for what was known before the book; the moment each thing was first seen in play, the world's name among its parts since 0.3.1 (a character's book spans worlds, whose days run apart; a moment kept before has none), with a creature's stars (`CompendiumBook.FirstSeen`), none for what came off the explored map or out of the game's records; and how often each creature killed the character, with the day of the last (`CompendiumBook.DeathsBy`, read off the last hit as `Player.OnDeath` runs on the character's own client, `DeathWatch`). A moment (`Moment`) is the game's day, the biome stood in, the time of day by the game's turn of it (dawn from 0.2 to 0.3, day to 0.7, dusk to 0.8, night the rest, as the game's morning and evening come within 0.05 of a quarter and three quarters), the weather by the game's name for it, and the weapon or tool in hand, none bare-handed (`CompendiumKeeper.Now`). What a character's first load in a session catches up from the game's records is learned with no moment and no day (`CompendiumBook.CatchingUp`). It is kept in `BepInEx/cache/isimp.Scry/compendium/`, a file per character named by the character's file, where that lives and the character's id (`CompendiumBook.FileName`). The game draws a new id for each character it makes, so a character made under the name of one deleted starts with an empty book. The book is written through `FileSwap` on a worker (`FileQueue`), at most every ten seconds while it changes (`CompendiumBook.WriteEvery`), so a crash loses no more, and at once on leaving a world, switching character and quitting (`CompendiumKeeper`). A book that cannot be read gives way to the copy kept from before it; where neither can be read, both are set aside beside it as `.unreadable-` and the date, never written over nor lost, and a new book starts and saves, told once in the log and in a notice under the panel's header naming what it was set aside as, with *Copy path* and a cross putting it away, as for the walked dungeons' maps and a world's dungeon records (`KeptFile`, `FileSwap.SetAside`, `SetAsideNotice`, `ScryPanel.SetAsideStrip`); only where even that fails is nothing written.

## Tiers and the floor

Each boss closes its biome's tier, Eikthyr the Meadows (1) up to Fader the Ashlands (7) (`Tiers`); the Ocean and the Deep North have none, so only meeting their things shows them. An entry's tier is worked out from how it is come by (`CompendiumTiers`, `TierWays`, `TierPass`): the lowest over the ways to come by it, a way costing the highest of its own tier and the tiers of everything it needs. The ways are the biomes it lives in; the places holding it; whatever drops, yields, grows or bears it; a trader, once the boss it waits for has fallen; each recipe with its station; each piece's cost with each tool that builds it; each thing a station turns into another; the spawners and raids that bring a creature; the owners of what is played or left behind; and the givers of a status effect, a boss stone giving its power only for the trophy it takes. An ingredient asked for only by an upgrade does not count, a recipe making a thing from itself is no way in, and neither is a piece no tool builds, as the world's chests are, whose cost tells only what breaking it gives back. A raid comes in its earliest biome, no sooner than the bosses' fall its keys mark, the items it waits to be known and the weakest creature it brings. Passes repeat until no tier lowers, on a worker thread, and again whenever the catalog's groups change; an entry no way reaches has no tier.

A tier is beaten once its boss's kill is in the character's statistics, its defeat key among the character's own keys, or its trophy held (`Milestones`). The floor (`CompendiumBook.Floor`), set with *Known up to* beside the rules, can be raised tier by tier as far as the bosses beaten in a row from the first, and lowered again. Everything of a tier at or below it shows as known, with what it brings along that is of no higher a tier (a Meadows thing's link to an effect of the Swamp waits for the floor to reach the Swamp), marked in the list and told on its page by the lowest tier that shows it, its own or, for what is brought along, that of what brings it unless its own is higher, so a Black Forest thing says the Black Forest's tier with the floor stepped up to the Mountain (`CompendiumView.ByFloor`, `CompendiumView.FloorOf`); what the game places only to be glimpsed, standing far off and gone as a player comes near (its `Odin` behaviour, the hooded figure, placed in the Meadows' world as a bush is), no floor shows or brings along however low its tier: it is known once the character has seen it, in the few metres between where Scry's sight reaches and where it vanishes, and below the floor a page shows whole under either rules. A chip or link on any page going to such an entry carries the same faint steps mark at its end, room made for it, and its tip says the floor's tier that shows it above where it goes (`Explorer.ShownByFloor`, `CompendiumWords.FloorTip`, `ScryPanel.FloorMark`): items and drops, links and owners, a chain's steps, a fact's value, a table's first cell and later cells, a row's title, a line of where it comes from, and a mod's list.

## What it costs

Telling every entry's level goes through the whole catalog, so it is done only when the rules, the floor, the catalog's groups or the book change, and only while the panel is open (`Session.ShowKnown`): what is learned in play is told once the panel opens. Where only more was learned in the same book, as levels only rise, just what the book raised since (`CompendiumBook.RaisedSince`, the latest 4,096 raises remembered) and what it brings along are told on top, as a count afresh would have them (`CompendiumView.TellRaised`), and while the panel is open that is done at most once a second, so things seen one frame after another do not each cost the list a listing again. A pair seen changes no level, so it is counted apart (`CompendiumBook.PairsVersion`) and under *Strict* only marks the page shown and lists the search again. A place looked at for the first time regroups the catalog, and the tiers are worked out again: what they are gathered from is read on the main thread 3 ms a frame (`CompendiumTiers`), the tiers worked out on a worker, and where they come out as before nothing is counted afresh (`CompendiumView.Milestones`).

What a chest held is counted the first time the character opens it, the chest known by its world, its prefab and its place to a tenth of a metre (`CompendiumBook.ChestId`, `FirstOpen`, `Held`), as the game numbers its objects afresh each time a world loads. Earlier versions went by that number and counted a chest again after every load; `/scry forget chests` drops what every kind of chest was counted holding, keeping which chests were opened, so the figures start afresh from chests not opened before (`CompendiumBook.ForgetChests`).
