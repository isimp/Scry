# The stage and the world

The stage the panel films: its chips and backdrops, the biome ground, what it keeps, its camera, and previews placed in the world.

Code: `src/Model/StageGround.cs`, `src/Preview/Stage.cs`, `src/UI/ScryPanel.ViewMenu.cs`, `src/Model/AfterQuiet.cs`, `src/Preview/WaterParts.cs`, `src/Preview/Stage.Ground.cs`, `src/Preview/BiomeGround.cs`, `src/Preview/StageCreatures.cs`.

See also: [previews](previews.md), [floors](locations/floors.md); all notes in [the index](index.md).

## Chips and backdrops

The stage's top right corner holds few chips: *View*, opening a box for the spin and its speed, the backdrop, the lighting, a person beside the model for size and, while floors stand, whether the rooms below the floor opened keep their lights, each row switched by a click, a speed going on to the next of 7, 14, 28 and 56 degrees a second (`ScryPanel.ViewMenu`, `StageWords.SpinSpeeds`), the spin, its speed and the lights written to the config file, and for a place shown its example *Inside* or *Outside* and its *Creatures*; the roof is put on and off over the ruler, and the plan folds on itself. The backdrop is plain, a sky, the grid ruled in metres, or the ground of the model's own biome, with or without the sky (`StageGround`): of the biomes it is in, the first players meet, Meadows for none. A new player first sees the ground under the sky, until a backdrop is picked and kept (`StageGround.FirstBackdrop`).

## The biome ground

That ground is a round piece laid under the model with the world's own terrain material, each of its points coloured as the game colours its terrain to tell the biome (`Heightmap.GetBiomeColor`), nothing painted on it (`Heightmap.m_paintMaskNothing`) but the share the world keeps in the mask's fourth part, the Ashlands' lava and the Mistlands' own, here none (`StageGround.MaskShare`), a far distance for the shader to hide it by as the game gives each piece of its terrain one (`_LodHideDistance`, without which nothing draws), and for the sea's floor water over it (`_WaterLevel`), so the terrain's shader paints it as it paints the world, with the bumps it raises from each biome's own ground; it lies five centimetres under the floor, as high as those bumps rise, under the model whatever the camera does, several times as wide as what is framed and with the sky twenty times, out toward a horizon, its edge fading into the backdrop's colour or the sky's horizon under a sheet clear in its middle (`StageGround.Across`, `StageGround.Fade`).

A place paints its paths, dirt and paving on it as the game paints its terrain under it: its terrain modifiers are read off its copy while it sleeps, as stripping takes them off, and laid on the ground's mask in their order, each blending toward its colour by one less its distance over its reach, to the tenth power, times its strength (`TerrainModifier`, `Heightmap.PaintCleared`, `StageGround.Painted`); a camp's rooms paint theirs too. Each paint is noted first in the cells of a coarse grid it may reach, so each point of the mask weighs only its cell's paints rather than every one of a camp's hundreds (`StageGround.PaintMask`). As a camp's rooms come in one by one, each changing the paints, the mask is painted again at once and the grass scattered again once they have stopped coming for 0.3 s (`AfterQuiet`); another entry, biome or sky scatters it at once.

The biome's grass and small plants grow on it out to where the ground has faded away, eighty metres at the most, thinning as the ground fades so the two fade together rather than the grass ending in a ring (`StageGround.GrassOut`, `StageGround.GrassKept`), scattered patch by patch as the game scatters them (`ClutterSystem.GenerateVegPatch`), a few milliseconds a frame, the grass scattered last shown until the new is done (`BiomeGround.StepScatter`): each kind for the biome with the game's own dice for the patch, the stage's patches numbered as if out in the world, as the small numbers round its middle, multiplied as the game seeds a patch, gave a cross of patches and mirrored pairs the same dice (`StageGround.PatchSeed`), kept by its forest, noise, height, slope, sea depth, vegetation and cleared-ground rules, the ground taken at a height typical of the biome (`StageGround.Altitude`). It is kept off round a piece or an item, its footprint and a margin, at least 1.5 m round anything lower than grass grows so the grass does not hide it, and never round a creature taller than that, which stands in the grass up to its feet as in the game however wide its arms or wings (`StageGround.ClearOfGrass`). The kinds the game draws many times over are drawn so for the stage's camera alone (`InstanceRenderer`, `Graphics.DrawMeshInstanced`), the rest are copies. The sea's floor has the world's water over it, its surface's mesh and material with its depth and time told as the game tells them (`WaterVolume`). The camera gives the sea's water the depth of what is under it, which its shader reads to show what lies beneath and soften its edges. In a dungeon's example, the game's water, as a sunken crypt's flood, which its shader draws as a flat, lit slab looking like a floor of its own, is drawn instead as a faint see-through sheet of a muddy water's green at its own level, so the water still shows where it lies and the room's floor shows through it, dimmed with its room (`WaterParts`).

The light and sky take the colours the game's most common weather in the biome has at the lighting's time of day, Studio and Day by day, Dusk by evening, Night by night, the cave keeping its own (`EnvSetup`, `StageGround.TimeFor`), and the key light casts shadows while the ground is laid. The View box's *Ground* row picks the biome: Auto for the entry's own, or any of the game's, remembered; it lies at the place's ground, so a floor opened below that cuts it away as it cuts all else, and a dungeon's inside or a room of one keeps the plain floor (`Stage.Ground.cs`).

## What the stage keeps

The ground is an object of its own that keeps everything it makes, its piece of terrain, mask, paints, grass, water and edge, and lets go of it itself (`BiomeGround`); the stage tells it each frame whether it is wanted, of which biome and where. The creatures standing on the stage are kept the same way, which wait, which stand in which room, how high each flies and which stand above a cut (`StageCreatures`), while the stage makes each copy. The example dungeon or camp standing on the stage is kept so too, its rooms, which are dimmed, what each room's rays found and the floors they make (`StageExample`), and the cut over a floor in the model (`FloorCut`); what is left in the stage itself is its camera, lights and framing.

## The stage

The stage is a preview far above the world on a layer the game does not use, with its own camera rendering into a texture the panel draws and its own lights. Filmed fast (`StageQuality`, on the settings card), the texture is half the stage's size each way with no antialiasing, drawn stretched, the biome ground's grass is left out and the rooms below a dungeon's floor opened keep no lights (`Stage.EnsureTexture`, `StageExample.KeepDimmedLights`), for a big dungeon on a weaker machine. The main camera ignores that layer, the world's directional lights (the sun, and the flash a lightning strike makes for a moment, `Thunder.DoFlash`) are kept off it while the stage is filmed (`Light.GetLights`), and fog and ambient light are set for the stage only while its camera renders. The camera frames what the copy draws, far enough off for it to fill the picture, gliding out quickly so nothing leaves the picture and back in slowly once it settles, with the floor, grid and sky reaching past it (`StageFraming`). Particles are left out of what it frames where anything else is drawn, and so is a see-through part reaching both past twice the width of the solid ones and fifty metres past it, as the beacon a Charred fortress's bell altar sends three hundred metres into the sky, which still stands on the stage (`StageFraming.FramesSeeThrough`). The wheel's and a drag's limits and the picture's rays are the camera's moves (`StageCamera`). A creature stands on the bottom of its capsule, as the game stands it. The stage copy is taken down while the panel is closed.

Everything shown on the stage keeps its last level of detail however small it is in the picture (`DetailLevels`, `Stage.KeepEveryLevel`): the game culls a part once it fills less than its group's share of the screen's height, a tenth to a third for a cave's rocks, which a place zoomed out on soon does, so its rooms went piece by piece; a part still turns to its lesser levels as it shrinks. A big place has thousands of such groups, so they are changed a millisecond's worth a frame (`Stage.StepDetail`). The biome ground's scattered growth keeps the game's culling.

On the stage, an effect and what a model plays are heard as if beside you, and a model's own built-in sounds are muted. While a copy stands in the world, sound comes from there instead, and the stage's copies have their sound scripts switched off, since the game lets only so many of one sound play at once (`AudioMan.RequestPlaySound`).

## In the world

A model shown in the world stands where you were looking, facing you. Effects play there or on you. Projectiles are flown with the prefab's gravity and a chosen speed, and a ray stops them at the first ground or building, where their hit plays. A raid comes in no waves: for as long as it lasts, each of its creatures is rolled again at its pace, one with a cap topped up to it near you, one without one more each time (`SpawnSystem.UpdateSpawnList`), which its page tells under *Keeps coming* (`RaidWords.KeepsComing`). A raid on the stage is the first roll of each of its creatures as the game rolls it (`RaidRoll`, `RaidCrowd`), as `SpawnSystem.UpdateSpawnList` does the first time a raid's spawners run with none of its creatures about: each creature is tried as often as its cap (once without one), each try at its chance; a try stops once the creatures the pass has spawned reach its cap, and a group of its size is cut only to the cap itself, so a pass can bring more than a cap and a later creature with a smaller cap may bring none. Each creature's stars are rolled up from its lowest level at the level-up chance (`SpawnSystem.Spawn`, `GetLevelUpChance`: its own chance or 10, with the world's level or enemy level-up rate). Left out is what depends on the world: the time of day, weather and world keys a creature waits for, finding ground for it, the biome's level-up multiplier and the distance from the world's centre its stars may wait for. The copies have no bodies to push each other aside as live creatures do, so each group stands in rings around a point of its own, every body (its capsule's radius) clear of the next and the group as wide as the game spreads it (`m_groupRadius`) where that is wider, and the groups' points lie in a ring far enough apart that no two groups touch (`CrowdLayout`); a line under the stage says what came, and *Roll again* rolls a new wave. A raid that brings no creature, such as a boss's fight, keeps its card.

Sounds are copies on the camera, kept while they play, so long clips such as music play to the end and can be skipped through. The game's sound script picks a clip at random a frame after the copy wakes, so to play one variant, the copy's list is narrowed to that clip first. Location music is started by the game's `MusicLocation` when you come near, so Scry starts it instead, at your music volume.
